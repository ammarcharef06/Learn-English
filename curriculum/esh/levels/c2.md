# C2 — Proficiency

## 1. Purpose and scope

C2 represents highly sophisticated English use. Learners can manage nuance, rhetorical style, high-level persuasion, and precise communication in demanding professional, academic, and social settings.

## 2. Learning outcomes

By the end of C2, learners can:

- interpret dense and complex language accurately
- produce precise, nuanced spoken and written communication
- adapt style and register to different audiences and goals
- operate in negotiations, debates, and high-stakes communication
- express subtle distinctions in meaning and attitude
- present and defend complex arguments with control

## 3. Grammar and stylistic focus

- precise stance and modality
- nuanced lexical choice and collocation
- complex rhetorical structure
- advanced register shifts
- subtle discourse management and audience awareness

## 4. Unit sequence

### C2.U01 — Precision and nuance

Focus:
- lexical precision, tone adjustment, and collocation

Tasks:
- choose the best phrasing for a formal document
- compare near-synonyms and explain nuance

### C2.U02 — Advanced reading and interpretation

Focus:
- explicit and implied meaning in dense texts

Tasks:
- analyze rhetorical moves in editorials or reports
- infer assumptions, tone, and intent

### C2.U03 — Public speaking and influence

Focus:
- rhetorical control and persuasive oral delivery

Tasks:
- deliver a formal address and respond to challenging questions

### C2.U04 — Professional communication

Focus:
- negotiation, briefing, and crisis communication

Tasks:
- simulate a high-stakes professional meeting

### C2.U05 — High-level written sophistication

Focus:
- essay writing, analysis, reviews, and policy writing

Tasks:
- write a long-form piece with strong organization and tone

### C2.U06 — Advanced interaction and mediation

Focus:
- handling complexity, ambiguity, and disagreement gracefully

Tasks:
- mediate perspectives while preserving clarity and neutrality

## 5. Performance task

Deliver a 5–7 minute formal presentation on a complex topic, then answer challenging follow-up questions.

## 6. Assessment

- dense-text comprehension task
- high-level oral debate or negotiation
- extended written analysis or review
- final portfolio of advanced performance tasks

## 7. Mastery criteria

The learner is ready for Post-C2 specialization when they can:

- communicate with control under complexity
- adapt to audience, register, and purpose naturally
- sustain advanced argumentation and interpretation
- operate in professional and academic contexts with minimal strain

## 8. Common errors

- using formal language inappropriately
- overcomplicating simple points
- weak audience awareness
- loss of clarity under pressure

Support:
- deliberate style modeling
- rhetorical rehearsal tasks
- precision drills for high-stakes communication

## 9. Developed lesson route

Use the linked unit readings, teacher-read scripts, practice, and repair routines in [`resources/c1-c2-learning-pack.md`](../resources/c1-c2-learning-pack.md). Unit checks and the stage-end sample are in [`resources/c1-c2-assessment.md`](../resources/c1-c2-assessment.md). Read the relevant pack section before each lesson; the short sequences below identify what to teach and what evidence to collect. A completed lesson is exposure/practice, not mastery.

### C2.U01.L01 — Choose precise words in context

**Outcome:** distinguish the contextual force of four near-synonyms and justify a choice with the source's communicative purpose. **Prerequisite:** C1 attribution and register awareness. Teach `assert`, `suggest`, `concede`, and `acknowledge` through the C2 Unit 1 passage; stress that `significant` can be technical or general. **Practice/check:** explain why `proved` overstates the pilot. **Success:** wording preserves observation and causal limit. **Repair:** label each candidate as observation, interpretation, or decision before choosing a verb.

### C2.U01.L02 — Edit commitment and focus

**Outcome:** revise a short passage without changing date, quantity, condition, scope, or modality. **Prerequisite:** U01.L01. Model `did not establish` versus `disproved` and `provisionally` versus `permanently`. Learners edit an executive and public version and log emphasis changes. **Assessment:** C2-T-U01 language item. **Success:** all factual commitments remain stable. **Repair:** run a five-item commitment check: actor, action, scope, condition, certainty.

### C2.U01.L03 — Adapt two audience versions

**Outcome:** produce two audience-specific versions and explain one deliberate change that does not alter the evidence. **Prerequisite:** U01.L01–L02. Learners rewrite the pilot update for executives and the public. **Assessment:** C2-T-U01 transfer. **Success:** both versions preserve the provisional decision and the missing causal evidence. **Repair:** compare the two versions sentence by sentence for changed commitment.

### C2.U02.L01 — Reconstruct premises

**Outcome:** distinguish explicit proposition, presupposition, entailment, and defeasible implication in a dense short argument. **Prerequisite:** C1 cue-supported inference. Teach the pack's timetable example and the `even` presupposition; do not label every unstated idea a presupposition. **Practice/check:** identify the explicit decision boundary and the missing workload measure. **Success:** categories are correctly assigned and supported by wording. **Repair:** test whether the inference survives a change in context; if not, it may be defeasible.

### C2.U02.L02 — Rank competing readings

**Outcome:** rank three interpretations by textual support and explain why one plausible claim is unsupported. **Prerequisite:** U02.L01. Model the timetable reading, contrasting “ready for wider discussion” with adoption. **Practice:** complete the pack's (a)–(c) ranking; then analyze the safety listening script. **Success:** strongest reading is supported; contradiction and absence of evidence are distinguished. **Repair:** quote the limiting phrase and state exactly what it rules out.

### C2.U02.L03 — Write interpretive commentary

**Outcome:** write a 120–160-word commentary separating explicit content, inference, and unknown, with an alternative interpretation. **Prerequisite:** U02.L01–L02. Learners annotate before drafting and calibrate confidence. **Assessment:** C2-T-U02 transfer. **Success:** every interpretive claim cites a cue and at least one plausible alternative is acknowledged. **Repair:** add an uncertainty phrase and identify what further context would resolve it.

### C2.U03.L01 — Shape an address for an audience

**Outcome:** organize an address with a claim, progression, and close suited to a stated audience. **Prerequisite:** C1 presentation structure. Use “The address” to map repeated contrast from outputs to outcomes. **Practice/check:** label what the repetition emphasizes and what remains unmeasured. **Success:** audience can restate the recommendation without mistaking it for cancellation. **Repair:** replace one rhetorical flourish with a statement of evidence.

### C2.U03.L02 — Control parallelism and emphasis

**Outcome:** use one parallel contrast to sharpen a distinction without exaggerating evidence. **Prerequisite:** U03.L01. Model `not abandon the pilot; neither declare success` and discuss when repeated structures become manipulative. **Practice:** rehearse a mixed-audience address, testing pause and stress with a partner. **Success:** wording is parallel and the listener identifies output versus outcome. **Repair:** remove repeated wording that does not mark a real progression.

### C2.U03.L03 — Answer adversarial questions

**Outcome:** answer two challenging questions by acknowledging their premise, addressing evidence or values, and preserving qualifications. **Prerequisite:** U03.L01–L02. Use the dashboard listening script; practice answering the publication/trust challenge and governance questions. **Assessment:** C2-T-U03 transfer. **Success:** direct response, evidence boundary, and one useful clarification/repair are observable. **Repair:** separate the question's assumption from the part that can be answered.

### C2.U04.L01 — Map authority, interests, and unknowns

**Outcome:** record each stakeholder's position, condition, authority, and unknown before negotiation. **Prerequisite:** C1 position/interest distinction. Use “The service pilot”; distinguish the requested pilot from permanent adoption. **Practice/check:** role-card map with the missing weekend/customer-satisfaction evidence. **Success:** preferences are not presented as commitments, and scope is accurate. **Repair:** ask which party can authorize each decision.

### C2.U04.L02 — Make a high-stakes recommendation with precision

**Workshop:** see [`resources/integrated-skill-labs.md`](../resources/integrated-skill-labs.md).

### C2.U04.L03 — Write and verify a decision record

**Outcome:** produce a concise record that names decision, actor, condition, owner, measure, review date, and unresolved issue. **Prerequisite:** U04.L01 and the existing workshop C2.U04.L02. Use the listening script to preserve the busiest-shift exception and non-agreement on permanent expansion. **Assessment:** C2-T-U04 transfer. **Success:** a reader can identify exactly what was authorized and what was not. **Repair:** compare each verb with the role's recorded commitment.

### C2.U05.L01 — Audit the claim chain

**Outcome:** trace a report's evidence, alternative explanation, limit, and recommendation. **Prerequisite:** C1 synthesis. Use “Editorial alternatives”; identify both the booking change and voluntary clinic selection. **Practice/check:** draw evidence → interpretation → decision arrows. **Success:** staffing is not named as the sole cause. **Repair:** insert the missing alternative explanation next to the causal claim.

### C2.U05.L02 — Revise information focus and rhythm

**Outcome:** revise sentence order and rhythm to foreground the decision boundary without changing claim strength. **Prerequisite:** U05.L01. Compare the pack's short boundary sentences with a longer qualified sentence; discuss clefts as focus, not decoration. **Practice/check:** revise a paragraph for an oversight reader and annotate one change. **Success:** focus improves while scope and qualification remain unchanged. **Repair:** check each deletion/reordering against the source commitments.

### C2.U05.L03 — Edit for fidelity and concision

**Outcome:** produce a 180–230-word evidence-based note and a revision note explaining an improvement that preserves meaning. **Prerequisite:** U05.L01–L02. Use the source dossier or explicitly identify the single-source task; audit attribution, causality, and register. **Assessment:** C2-T-U05 transfer. **Success:** no invented evidence, exact causal limit, and a defensible audience choice. **Repair:** reverse-outline the claim chain, then edit one sentence at a time.

### C2.U06.L01 — Track exact commitments

**Outcome:** distinguish a stated commitment, conditional plan, opinion, and inference in conflicting accounts. **Prerequisite:** C1 negotiation record. Use “Two accounts of the same decision”; map Tuesday, Thursday, and conditional Friday positions. **Practice/check:** identify what none of the speakers claims. **Success:** no one is reported as saying analysis is complete. **Repair:** add a modality/condition label to every reported position.

### C2.U06.L02 — Test and repair understanding

**Outcome:** ask a clarification question that tests scope or deadline and reformulate a partner's position for confirmation. **Prerequisite:** U06.L01. Model `Have I understood correctly that...` and `Should we include the appendix and move the date...?`. **Practice:** incomplete-notes role-play; partner must correct one deliberately incomplete paraphrase. **Success:** ambiguity is surfaced and correction incorporated. **Repair:** offer two explicit alternatives rather than a leading yes/no question.

### C2.U06.L03 — Mediate for two audiences

**Outcome:** create specialist and public summaries that preserve the same condition and uncertainty. **Prerequisite:** U06.L01–L02. Learners write a 120-word specialist version and a 60-word public version; list what was simplified and what could not be omitted. **Assessment:** C2-T-U06 transfer. **Success:** both accounts preserve the completeness condition and do not call Friday unconditional. **Repair:** compare summaries against the source's actor, date, scope, and condition.

## 10. Assessment route and progression

Use the ungraded C2 diagnostic before instruction, the linked formative C2-T-U01…U06 checks after each unit, and C2-END-01 after fresh-topic practice. The pack provides the teaching inputs; the assessment file supplies prompts, keys, open-task criteria, score boundaries, and local readiness rules. Prepared materials are not learner results. The local rule is unpiloted and is not CEFR certification.

### C1-to-C2 transition evidence

Before entering this route, collect evidence that the learner can synthesize sources, support inference with textual cues, adapt register, and negotiate with conditions. These are local prerequisites, not an official C1 gate. C2 adds editorial control over commitment, adjudication among competing readings, high-pressure response, exact decision records, and fidelity across audience adaptations.

### C2-to-specialization readiness evidence

Use at least two fresh, complex tasks from different genres, including one interaction and one written mediation/editing task. Require accurate commitment control, justified interpretation, audience adaptation, and repair under challenge. This is a local recommendation only; it does not certify C2 or readiness for any professional specialization.
