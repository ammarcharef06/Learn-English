# A1 — Beginner

## 1. Purpose and scope

At A1, learners handle familiar everyday communication with short, simple language. This course interprets A1 as the ability to understand and use familiar everyday expressions, introduce oneself and others, ask and answer simple personal questions, and interact simply when the other person speaks clearly and is willing to help. These are course-level summaries, not quoted or descriptor-by-descriptor CEFR claims; see `REFERENCES.md` for source-access limits.

The course assumes the project-specific Pre-A0/A0 foundation, or equivalent demonstrated ability: basic print decoding, personal details, `be`, simple present statements/questions, basic numbers/time, and a short understandable sentence. A1 retrieves these briefly while extending them to everyday exchanges.
Selected unit vocabulary with contextual Arabic glosses, collocations, and examples is in `resources/a1-a2-vocabulary.md`; the set is illustrative rather than exhaustive.

## 2. Learning outcomes

By the end of A1, learners can:

- introduce themselves and others
- talk about family, home, and work
- talk about daily routines and free time
- ask for and give basic information
- buy food and ask simple service questions
- understand short, clearly presented messages, instructions, dialogues, and familiar-topic paragraphs
- write a short message or a connected 6–8-sentence description using familiar vocabulary
- ask for repetition or clarification with phrases such as `Could you say that again?` and `What does ... mean?`

## 3. Grammar progression

- simple present with time expressions
- present continuous for temporary actions
- `there is / there are`
- `a / an / the`
- `can` for ability and requests
- basic question forms
- countable and uncountable nouns
- adverbs of frequency

### A1.U01 — Meeting people and daily life

#### A1.U01.L01 — Ask and answer personal questions

**Goal:** ask and answer four personal questions using appropriate `be` or `do` forms. **Prerequisite:** A0 personal details and present simple.

Use `be` for identity/origin (`Where are you from? — I'm from Rabat.`) and `do` with ordinary verbs (`Where do you work? — I work at a hotel.`). `What do you do?` asks about work/study, not an immediate action. Arabic: «من أين أنت؟» و«ماذا تعمل؟».

**Guided:** complete `___ you from?`, `Where ___ she work?`, `What ___ your brother do?` **Key:** are; does; does. **Independent/interaction:** conduct a four-question interview: name, home city, work/study, one free-time activity; ask one follow-up.

**Check:** 4/4 questions have correct auxiliaries and the partner can report two answers. **Remedy:** sort cards into `be` questions and `do` questions, then rebuild questions with new names. **Links:** A0.U01; A1.U01.L02; integrated speaking rubric in `resources/assessment-rubrics.md`.

#### A1.U01.L02 — Describe people and relationships

**Goal:** write and say five accurate sentences about two people using family/job words and `be`, `have`, and present-simple verbs. **Prerequisite:** A1.U01.L01; A0 possessives.

Use `my/your/his/her` before a noun: `Her brother is a nurse.` Use `has` for one person: `Omar has two sisters.` A job after `be` normally takes an article: `She is a teacher.` Arabic: «أخوها ممرض» و«عمر لديه أختان».

**Guided:** `Mina / student` → `Mina is a student.`; `Ali / two brothers` → `Ali has two brothers.` **Key:** those complete sentences. **Reading:** `Nadia is from Tunis. She is a cook. Her husband works at a school. They have one child.` Ask who is a cook and where her husband works. **Key:** Nadia; at a school.

### A1.U02 — Home and routines

#### A1.U02.L01 — Describe a home and locate objects

**Goal:** describe a room in five sentences using `there is/are` and three place phrases. **Prerequisite:** A0 articles, plurals, prepositions.

Use `There is` for one item and `There are` for more than one: `There is a lamp next to the bed.` `There are two chairs by the table.` The noun after the phrase controls singular/plural agreement. Arabic: «يوجد مصباح بجانب السرير».

#### A1.U02.L02 — Describe routines and frequency

**Goal:** describe a weekday routine in six sentences, placing frequency words correctly and using third-person `-s` in 4/5 target clauses. **Prerequisite:** A0 present simple and clock times.

Frequency words usually come before an ordinary verb (`I often cook`) but after `be` (`She is usually at home`). For `he/she/it`, add `-s/-es`: `He watches TV`; after `does`, use the base form. `At night` is a common time phrase. Arabic: «غالبًا أطبخ» / «هي غالبًا في المنزل».

**Guided:** reorder `usually / Mina / walks / home` → `Mina usually walks home.` **Key:** same. **Independent:** use a timetable to tell a partner a routine, then compare one difference.

**Check:** six relevant sentences, 4/5 verb forms and 4/5 adverb positions. **Remedy:** separate the `be` column from the ordinary-verb column; practice a new subject. **Links:** A0.U03; A1.U02.L01 and `A1.U02.L03` workshop.

### A1.U03 — Food and shopping

#### A1.U03.L01 — Name foods and express preferences

**Goal:** name eight foods and state three preferences using `like/don't like` and a reason. **Prerequisite:** A0 present-simple statements and `and/but`.

Use `I like rice.` For a general dislike, `I don't like onions.` Countable items can be plural (`apples`); many foods are uncountable in ordinary shopping (`some bread`, not usually `a bread`). Arabic gloss depends on context: `some bread` = «بعض الخبز».

**Guided:** choose `a/an/some`: `___ apple`, `___ rice`, `___ sandwich`. **Key:** an; some; a. **Independent speaking:** ask a partner `Do you like ...?` and record three answers; report one accurate fact.

**Check:** 8/10 food-picture matches and three understandable preference sentences. **Remedy:** teach food in meal groups and contrast singular count items with uncountable food; retest using new pictures. **Links:** A0 articles/plurals; A1.U03.L02.

#### A1.U03.L02 — Order politely and ask about prices

**Goal:** complete a café exchange with an item, quantity, price question, and confirmation. **Prerequisite:** familiar food nouns, numbers/money.

Use `Can I have ...?` or `I'd like ...` for a polite order. Use `How much is it?` for one item; `How much are they?` for plural items. `A cup of tea` is a useful measure phrase. Arabic: «هل يمكنني الحصول على ...؟» and «كم ثمنه؟».

#### A1.U04.L02 — Say what is happening now

**Goal:** contrast routine and current action in six spoken/written sentences, with correct `be + -ing` in 4/5 clauses. **Prerequisite:** present simple, subject pronouns, `be`.

Use present simple for routines (`I study at night`) and present continuous for an action happening now (`I am studying now`). Form `am/is/are + verb-ing`; do not omit `be`. `Today` can signal a temporary situation but is not a mechanical tense rule. Arabic: «أدرس عادةً ليلًا» vs «أدرس الآن».

**Guided:** choose: `She (works/is working) now`; `He usually (walks/is walking) to school.` **Key:** is working; walks. **Independent:** describe two routine pictures and two “now” pictures.

**Check:** classify 5/6 contexts and form 4/5 continuous clauses. **Remedy:** underline time/context clue, then build subject + `be` + `-ing`. **Links:** A1.U02.L02; A1.U04.L03.

#### A1.U04.L03 — Write a short schedule message

**Goal:** write a 40–60-word schedule message with two activities, two times/days, and one question. **Prerequisite:** timetable vocabulary and present forms.

Use a greeting, short information, and a closing. `I have class on Monday at nine.` `I am studying at the library now.` Match the tense to meaning, not merely to a word such as `today`.

**Guided:** correct `On Tuesday I have English at 9. It finish at 10.` **Key:** `On Tuesday I have English at 9. It finishes at 10.` **Independent writing:** message a classmate about a changed schedule and ask whether they can meet.

**Peer response:** partner answers the question and repeats the agreed day/time. **Check:** 40–60 words, required schedule details, readable sentence punctuation, and accurate forms in 4/5 clauses. **Remedy:** use a schedule grid, then convert each row into a sentence. **Links:** A1.U04.L01–L02; A1 writing rubric.

### A1.U05 — Places and services

#### A1.U05.L01 — Ask for and follow directions

**Goal:** ask where a place is and follow a two-step route on a simple map. **Prerequisite:** place names and prepositions.

Useful phrases: `Excuse me. Where is the station?` `Go straight, then turn right.` `It's opposite the bank.` `Is it far?` A listener can ask `Could you repeat that, please?` Arabic: «عذرًا، أين المحطة؟» / «تابع مستقيمًا ثم انعطف يمينًا».

**Guided:** follow teacher directions on a map; start at the school and go straight, then left. **Independent pair task:** each learner gets a different start/destination card and gives directions without pointing.

**Map for guided practice** (north is up):

```text
       LIBRARY
          |
SCHOOL — BANK — CLINIC
```

From the school, go east to the bank, then north to the library. **Key:** go straight/east to the bank; then turn left/north to the library. **Key/criteria:** for the independent task, the route must reach the named destination; include two direction/location phrases; partner confirms or asks for repetition. **Check:** 4/5 routes followed accurately. **Remedy:** draw one arrow per instruction, then remove the drawing aid. **Links:** A0.U04; A1.U05.L02.

**Five route-card keys:** school → bank: east/straight; school → clinic: east to bank, then east; school → library: east to bank, then north; clinic → school: west twice; library → clinic: south to bank, then east. Use one card for modeling, then ask the learner to follow or give the other four routes.

#### A1.U05.L02 — Ask for a simple service

**Goal:** complete a service exchange with a greeting, need, one information question, and a confirmation. **Prerequisite:** `can` requests and place/direction phrases.

Model at a station: `Can I have a ticket to the city center, please?` — `Single or return?` — `A single, please. What time is the next bus?` `Single` means one-way; `return` means a ticket for travel back. These meanings depend on the ticket context.

### A1.U06 — Free time and invitations

#### A1.U06.L01 — Talk about hobbies and preferences

**Goal:** ask and answer five questions about free-time activities and give one reason. **Prerequisite:** present simple, like/dislike.

Use `I like swimming` or `I like to swim`; both are common. `Do you like ...?` takes `do`, while `Are you ...?` uses `be`: `Are you interested in music?` Do not use `do` with `be`.

**Guided:** form a question from `you / play / football`; choose `Do/Are you like music?` **Key:** `Do you play football?`; `Do you like music?`

**Independent interaction:** interview a partner, ask one follow-up (`When do you play?`), and report two facts accurately. **Check:** 4/5 questions well formed, one reason, and two correct reported facts. **Remedy:** sort prompts by verb type and practice question frames. **Links:** A1.U01; A1.U06.L02.

#### A1.U06.L02 — Invite, accept, and decline politely

**Goal:** make an invitation, accept or decline, and agree on a day/time/place. **Prerequisite:** days/times and preference questions.

`Would you like to come to the park?` is an invitation; `Do you want to ...?` is more direct but also ordinary. Accept: `Yes, I'd love to.` Decline politely: `Sorry, I can't. I'm busy on Saturday.` Offer another time: `Are you free on Sunday?`

**Guided:** match invitation responses to context. **Key:** several responses are valid if they accept or decline clearly and politely. **Independent role-play:** plan a free-time activity; change one constraint and negotiate a new time.

**Check:** include invitation, response, alternative/confirmation; partner can repeat the agreed plan. **Remedy:** rehearse a three-turn frame, then remove the written prompts and change the activity. **Links:** A1.U06.L03; A1.U04 schedule.

#### A1.U06.L03 — Write and relay an invitation

**Goal:** write a 50–70-word invitation message and accurately relay the agreed details. **Prerequisite:** invitation phrases and time/place expressions.

**Model:** `Hi Lina, Would you like to see a film on Friday? It starts at six at the City Cinema. We can meet outside at 5:45. Let me know if you can come. — Noor`

## 6. Reading sample

`I get up at six thirty every day. I have breakfast and leave home at seven fifteen. My office is near the station, so I usually walk there. At lunch, I eat a sandwich and drink tea. In the evening, I cook dinner and watch a film with my family.`

Questions:
- What time does the learner get up?
- How do they go to work?
- What do they do at lunch?
- What do they do in the evening?

## 9. Assessment

The learner demonstrates ability through:

- dialogues with a partner
- written paragraph on a routine or family topic
- listening comprehension on daily-life situations
- short end-of-level test

Use the ungraded entry diagnostic, six keyed mixed-skill unit checks, and 40-point stage-end test in `resources/a1-a2-assessment.md`. Record attempted, completed, and mastered separately. The local thresholds are unstandardized and are not CEFR certification.

## 10. Mastery criteria

The learner is ready for A2 when they can:

- give basic information in simple but correct sentences
- ask and answer questions about daily life
- write a short connected paragraph
- sustain a short conversation without excessive prompting

## 11. Common learner errors

- missing `s` in the third person singular
- confusion between `there is` and `there are`
- using `a` and `an` incorrectly
- word order in questions

Support:
- sentence frames
- short grammar reviews
- regular revisiting of high-frequency vocabulary
