# A0 / Pre-A1 — First usable English

## Purpose and position in the pathway

A0 / Pre-A1 is a project-specific foundation stage, not an official CEFR level. It assumes the learner has begun the Pre-A0 literacy pathway or has demonstrated equivalent ability in the entry check. It builds short, meaningful sentences and routine exchanges before A1.

The sequence moves from identity and the `be` verb, to nouns and descriptions, then time and routine, everyday locations and requests, and finally connected reading, writing, review, and a readiness assessment. Pre-A0 letter/sound work is retrieved in each unit when it helps read or spell a new word; it is not retaught as a second alphabet unit.

## Outcomes and gate to A1

By the end of the stage, the learner can:

1. exchange basic personal details and spell a name or simple word;
2. understand and use numbers to 100, age, days, months, dates, and common clock times;
3. use subject pronouns with present-tense `be`, including affirmative, negative, and yes/no or wh- questions;
4. use common nouns, `a/an`, regular plurals, selected irregular plurals, basic adjectives, and possessives;
5. use `have/has`, `can/can't`, and a small set of everyday present-simple verbs;
6. ask and answer simple questions with `be` and with `do/does` in the taught present-simple patterns;
7. describe a home, family, daily routine, and familiar place using short connected sentences;
8. understand a short familiar spoken exchange/instruction and take turns in a simple practical interaction;
9. read a short message/profile and write a short, legible, connected text with basic punctuation.

**A1 readiness gate:** on two different occasions, learner should: convey at least 4/5 personal details; read a 50–70-word familiar-topic passage and correctly answer 4/5 factual questions; follow 4/5 spoken instructions or questions; ask and answer at least four relevant questions in a role-play; write 6–8 connected sentences containing a subject and an appropriate verb in at least 5/6 required clauses; correctly use the taught time/place expressions in 4/5 prompted situations. This is a local placement check, not a CEFR certificate. Provide a separate listening check with a new teacher-read script; do not score a memorized response as listening.

## Teaching and assessment conventions

- Every answer below that is factual has an explicit key. For open personal responses, a sample is illustrative; accept any truthful, comprehensible, grammatically suitable answer.
- `be` questions invert the verb and subject (`Are you ...?`). Ordinary present-simple verbs use `do/does` (`Do you live ...?`). Do not combine these patterns (`Do you are ...?`).
- Teach time/place language in phrases as well as lists: `at six`, `on Monday`, `in May`, `in the kitchen`, `next to the door`.
- Reading aloud is not a substitute for listening comprehension. Teacher-read scripts remain part of the course; selected prompts now have supplemental Piper Amy clips linked at the relevant activities. They are experimental, have no human phonetic validation, and carry no automated scores.
- Pre-A0 sound-to-letter skills are revisited only in short spelling/reading retrieval. Review prior knowledge; do not reteach mastered targets without evidence of need.

## Unit A0.U01 — People, names, numbers, and dates

**Unit outcome:** exchange identity details and understand common numbers, dates, and personal questions.  
**Prerequisite:** Pre-A0 transition gate or equivalent; no complete-sentence grammar assumed.
**Unit check:** T-A0-U01 in [the foundation assessments](../resources/foundation-assessment.md).

### A0.U01.L01 — Subject pronouns and `be`

**Goal:** complete 8/10 sentence prompts with the correct subject pronoun and present `be`.  
**Prerequisite:** understand names and familiar people.

**Teach:** a subject tells who or what a sentence is about. `I, you, he, she, it, we, they` usually come before the verb. In the present, `I am`; `he/she/it is`; `you/we/they are`. `You` can address one person or more than one person. Use `be` for identity, state, age, and location: `I am Noor.`, `She is tired.`, `We are at home.` In natural speech, `I’m`, `she’s`, and `we’re` are common contractions; the full forms are useful in early writing.

### A0.U01.L03 — Numbers, age, and spelling personal details

**Goal:** identify, say, and write 18/20 numbers from 1–20 and the taught tens; state age using a complete phrase.  
**Prerequisite:** Pre-A0 number/letter familiarity; L01 `be`.

**Teach:** numbers 13–19 often end in `-teen`; tens such as `thirty`, `forty`, `fifty` have a different ending and stress pattern. The spelling of `forty` has no `u`. For 21–99, combine tens and units: `twenty-one`, `forty-two`. `I am 18.` is the usual age pattern in English; do not say `I have 18 years` in this pattern.

### A0.U01.L05 — Days, months, dates, and birthdays

**Goal:** sequence days and months, understand 4/5 read dates, and say or write a personal date using an appropriate preposition.  
**Prerequisite:** number recognition; L01–L03.

**Teach:** days begin with capitals: `Monday` through `Sunday`; months begin with capitals: `January` through `December`. For a day/date, use `on`: `on Monday`, `on 14 April` (common British format) or `on April 14` (common US format). Use `in` with a month or year: `in April`, `in 2026`. Ask `When is your birthday?` Answer `It is in May` or `It is on 14 May.` Date order varies by region; show one format at a time and label alternatives.

## Unit A0.U02 — Family, objects, articles, and ownership

**Unit outcome:** describe familiar people and objects using nouns, plural forms, simple adjectives, and possessives.  
**Prerequisite:** U01 introductions and `be`.
**Unit check:** T-A0-U02 in [the foundation assessments](../resources/foundation-assessment.md).

### A0.U02.L01 — Family and basic plural nouns

**Goal:** identify 10 family words and form the plural of 8/10 taught regular nouns.  
**Prerequisite:** recognize people and objects.

**Teach:** most count nouns form the plural with `-s`: `book/books`, `sister/sisters`. Add `-es` after many endings such as `s`, `sh`, `ch`, or `x`: `bus/buses`, `box/boxes`. Some high-frequency plurals are irregular: `child/children`, `man/men`, `woman/women`. Teach each irregular pair as a word family, not a productive rule.

**Vocabulary:** `mother` أم، `father` أب، `sister` أخت، `brother` أخ، `grandmother` جدة، `grandfather` جد، `parent` أحد الوالدين، `child` طفل.

**Guided:** sort singular/plural pairs. **Key:** `book/books`, `box/boxes`, `child/children`, `sister/sisters`.

**Independent:** say and write the plural: `pen`, `chair`, `bus`, `child`, `box`. **Key:** pens, chairs, buses, children, boxes.

**Check:** 8/10 plural forms and 8/10 picture-word matches. **Remediation:** identify the ending sound/spelling; practice the matched rule, then one irregular at a time.

**Links:** A0.U02.L02 articles; L04 family profile.

### A0.U02.L02 — `a/an`, `the`, and familiar objects

**Goal:** choose a suitable article in 8/10 familiar noun phrases and identify when the object is already known.  
**Prerequisite:** singular/plural nouns; letter/sound awareness.

**Teach:** a singular count noun usually needs a determiner: `a book`, `the book`, `my book`. Use `a` before a consonant sound (`a pen`, `a university` begins with /j/ in many standard pronunciations); use `an` before a vowel sound (`an apple`, `an orange`, `an hour` where h is silent). Choice depends on sound, not first written letter. Use `the` when speaker and listener can identify the particular thing: `I see a door. The door is open.` Do not use `a/an` with plural `books` or an uncountable noun such as `water` in the basic sense.

### A0.U02.L03 — Basic adjectives and describing objects

**Goal:** produce five noun phrases with a correct simple adjective-before-noun order and matching `be` sentence.  
**Prerequisite:** common nouns and `be`.

**Teach:** in ordinary descriptions, a simple adjective usually comes before its noun: `a small bag`, `a red book`. The adjective does not take plural `-s`: `two red books`, not `two reds books`. Use `be + adjective` to describe a subject: `The bag is small.` Teach only a few useful adjectives at first: `big/small`, `new/old`, `red/blue`, `hot/cold`, `good`.

### A0.U02.L04 — Possessive adjectives, `have/has`, and `’s`

**Goal:** use four possessive adjectives and `have/has` in 8/10 descriptions; identify ownership with a name + `'s`.  
**Prerequisite:** subject pronouns; family words; nouns.

**Teach:** possessive adjectives come before a noun: `my book`, `your sister`, `his bag`, `her pen`, `our home`, `their room`. They do not change to plural: `their books`. Use `have` with `I/you/we/they`; use `has` with `he/she/it`: `I have a sister.` / `She has two brothers.` To name a person's possession, add `'s`: `Mona's book`. Do not confuse `it's` (it is) with `its` (possessive); this distinction is recognition only at this stage.

### A0.U02.L05 — Read and describe a family/home profile

## Unit A0.U03 — Clock time, daily routines, and present simple

**Unit outcome:** tell common times, describe routine actions, and ask/answer basic habit questions.  
**Prerequisite:** numbers, pronouns, familiar verbs.
**Unit check:** T-A0-U03 in [the foundation assessments](../resources/foundation-assessment.md).

### A0.U03.L01 — Clock time and time prepositions

**Goal:** identify 8/10 full-hour/half-hour clock times and choose `at/on/in` in 8/10 taught time phrases.  
**Prerequisite:** numbers 1–60; days/months in U01.L05.

**Teach:** `It is seven o'clock.` / `It is half past two.` Digital alternatives (`2:30`) can be read `two thirty`. Use `at` with a clock time (`at 7:00`), `on` with a day/date (`on Monday`), and `in` with a month/year or broad part of day (`in May`, `in 2026`, `in the morning`). Common exception: `at night`. These are high-frequency phrases; learn them with examples.

**Prerequisite:** subject pronouns and time phrases.

### A0.U04.L01 — Prepositions of place

**Goal:** describe 5/6 object locations using a taught preposition.  
**Prerequisite:** object nouns and `be`.

**Teach:** a preposition shows a relationship. `in` = inside; `on` = touching the top surface; `under` = below; `next to` = at the side; `behind` = at the back; `between` = in the space separating two items.

**Prerequisite:** completion of taught units, not presumed mastery.

**Retrieval stations:** (1) read a familiar phrase; (2) spell a name or object; (3) ask a `be` question; (4) ask a `do` question; (5) describe where an object is; (6) read a date/time; (7) understand a new spoken instruction; (8) make a polite request.

**Teacher key:** check that `be` questions use `be` inversion and ordinary present verbs use do-support; verify object/preposition relation in the actual setup; compare date/time to displayed card; follow the spoken instruction accurately. Open stations use the rubrics and accept semantically valid alternatives.

**Spaced review:** revisit selected items the next day, in 3 days, and in 7 days, mixing topics. A correct answer after a hint is recorded as supported practice, not independent retrieval.

**Check:** complete `resources/foundation-assessment.md` diagnostic repair check, then final transition gate on a later day. Link results to the specific outcome/lesson; do not average a missed listening skill away with strong writing.

**Links:** all A0 units; Pre-A0 transition gate; A1 placement is the next stage.

## Consolidation: what is reviewed and what is new

| Pre-A0 knowledge | How A0 retrieves it | New A0 learning |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize/write letters; letter names vs word sounds | spell name and object words; read dates and messages | subject pronouns, `be`, ordinary sentence pattern |
| Hear initial/final sounds; blend selected CVC/digraph words | decode and spell a small set of new unit words | articles, plurals, adjective/noun phrases, possessives |
| Follow one-step commands and use hello/name | listen to profiles, instructions, service requests | personal Q&A, `do/does`, `can`, `there is/are` |
| Recognize objects and a few personal words | use the same words in descriptions and locations | family/home vocabulary, routine verbs, time/place expressions |
| Copy/spell short words | write labels, sentences, short profile | connected 6–8 sentence text and practical message |

Review is purposeful retrieval, not a duplicate course: a teacher briefly samples a prerequisite, provides support only if needed, and spends the main practice time on the new target.
