# C1 — Advanced

## 1. Purpose and scope

C1 focuses on flexibility, precision, and complexity. Learners can interpret nuance, understand implied meaning, and produce formal or professional English with structure and control.

## 2. Learning outcomes

By the end of C1, learners can:

- read and summarize long and demanding texts
- analyze argument structure and evidence
- write formal academic or professional texts
- present and defend ideas with clarity and nuance
- interpret implied meaning and attitude in spoken and written discourse
- manage register shifts across contexts

## 3. Grammar and discourse focus

- nominalization and abstract noun usage
- modal nuance and hedging
- reporting and paraphrasing
- rhetorical organization
- formal vocabulary and register control
- synthesis across multiple sources

## 4. Unit sequence

### C1.U01 — Reading and analytic comprehension

Topics:
- article structure, thesis, evidence, counterargument

Tasks:
- summary writing
- text evaluation
- identifying tone and stance

### C1.U02 — Academic and professional writing

Topics:
- reports, formal emails, research summaries, briefings

Skills:
- precise wording
- logical structure
- modification of tone for audience

### C1.U03 — Oral argumentation and presentations

Tasks:
- deliver a structured presentation
- respond to questions with evidence and clarity
- use signposting language naturally

### C1.U04 — Implicit meaning and nuance

Topics:
- tone, implication, attitude, irony, persuasion

Listening:
- interviews, lectures, podcasts, panel discussions

### C1.U05 — Research and synthesis

Tasks:
- compare two or more sources
- write a synthesized summary or critique
- track the difference between facts, claims, and conclusions

### C1.U06 — Negotiation and complex interaction

Topics:
- workplace discussion, disagreement, compromise, and persuasion

Functions:
- express reservations, settle differences, and maintain rapport

## 5. Reading sample

`The policy may appear effective in the short term, yet its long-term impact remains uncertain given the fragmented evidence base. While some analysts emphasize productivity gains, others stress the social costs of reduced personal interaction and uneven access. A balanced assessment therefore requires both quantitative measurement and qualitative evaluation, particularly when the policy affects different communities in unequal ways.`

Tasks:
- identify hedging language
- rewrite the text in a more formal academic style
- summarize it in 70 words

## 6. Listening and speaking activities

- present a case with evidence and examples
- defend a position under questioning
- analyze an interview for stance and meaning
- negotiate a solution in a multi-role scenario

## 7. Writing tasks

- 500–700 word analytical essay
- formal report or policy brief
- literature or article summary with evaluation

## 8. Assessment

- summary of academic reading
- oral presentation with follow-up questions
- extended report or essay
- formal end-of-level task

## 9. Mastery criteria

The learner can move to C2 when they can:

- write structured, analytically strong text
- manage nuance and register in complex contexts
- summarize and respond to dense input with clarity
- perform at a sustained advanced level under pressure

## 10. Common errors

- vague thesis statements
- weak paraphrasing
- over-generalization without support
- inconsistent register

Support:
- explicit model for strong academic structure
- sentence-level precision review
- contrastive register practice

## 11. Developed lesson route

Use the linked unit readings, teacher-read scripts, practice, and repair routines in [`resources/c1-c2-learning-pack.md`](../resources/c1-c2-learning-pack.md). Unit checks and the stage-end sample are in [`resources/c1-c2-assessment.md`](../resources/c1-c2-assessment.md). Read the relevant pack section before each lesson; the short sequences below identify what to teach and what evidence to collect. A completed lesson is exposure/practice, not mastery.

### C1.U01.L01 — Map an argument

**Outcome:** label a passage's claim, evidence, qualification, and conclusion, each with a text cue. **Prerequisite:** B2 distinction between claim and evidence. Teach the difference between what a report records and what its author concludes; model this on “The quiet measure.” Learners annotate each sentence and explain how the access limitation qualifies “success.” **Practice/check:** partner compare, then answer the U01 reading questions. **Success:** all four functions are located and the qualification is not mistaken for a refutation. **Repair:** color-code source fact, author claim, and learner inference.

### C1.U01.L02 — Follow cohesion and scope

**Outcome:** trace two reference or connective links and identify the scope of a claim. **Prerequisite:** U01.L01. Model how `that description` refers to the council's claim and how `if` limits its force. Learners draw a link map and replace one ambiguous `this` with its referent. **Practice/check:** explain why the attendance figure does not itself establish equitable access. **Success:** both links are accurate and no broader population or causal claim is invented. **Repair:** underline the antecedent before interpreting each pronoun.

### C1.U01.L03 — Write an analytical summary

**Outcome:** write a 70–90-word summary retaining evidence, limitation, and implication. **Prerequisite:** U01.L01–L02. Model a summary that connects the limitation to the interpretation rather than listing details. Learners write for a policy analyst and peer-check attribution and qualification. **Assessment:** C1-T-U01 transfer. **Success:** includes all three required relations and makes no unsupported causal claim. **Repair:** rewrite one sentence with `although` or `if` to restore the missing qualification.

### C1.U02.L01 — Plan for a decision reader

**Outcome:** organize a report note around a decision question, evidence, limitation, recommendation, and next check. **Prerequisite:** B2 paragraph cohesion. Use “A report that travels” to identify those functions; distinguish the two simultaneous interventions. Learners order a shuffled note before drafting an outline. **Practice/check:** annotate each sentence by function. **Success:** all five functions appear in a logical order and the recommendation remains limited. **Repair:** add a source/limitation column to the outline.

### C1.U02.L02 — Edit agency and register

**Outcome:** revise nominalized or passive wording when it hides a relevant actor, without making the tone less professional. **Prerequisite:** B2 passive/nominalization. Teach the contrast `A review was undertaken` / `The team reviewed`; identify when the actor matters and when a neutral passive is useful. **Practice/check:** revise the report sentence and the client-response opening in the pack. **Assessment:** C1-T-U02 language item. **Success:** actor and meaning are preserved; tone is direct, not inflated. **Repair:** ask “Who did what?” before retaining an abstract noun.

### C1.U02.L03 — Draft an evidence-based note

**Outcome:** produce a 70–100-word manager note including evidence, limitation, and action. **Prerequisite:** U02.L01–L02. Learners draft from the pack input, then edit for attribution and claim strength. **Assessment:** C1-T-U02 transfer. **Success:** all three elements are present and the guide is not credited with an effect that may also come from demonstrations. **Repair:** mark every causal verb and support it with the stated design.

### C1.U03.L01 — Build a spoken argument map

**Outcome:** plan a short talk with claim, evidence, limitation, and decision rule. **Prerequisite:** B2 supported position/counterargument. Model the clinic script's distinction between attendance and waiting time. Learners turn the script into a four-box speaking plan. **Practice/check:** explain which evidence supports which claim. **Success:** no evidence point is used to prove an unmeasured outcome. **Repair:** pair each claim with a “what this does not show” note.

### C1.U03.L02 — Make relations audible

**Outcome:** use signposting and pausing to make two reasoning relations clear in a spoken explanation. **Prerequisite:** U03.L01. Contrast useful signposts (`The limitation matters because...`) with empty repeated formulae. Learners rehearse the same paragraph twice, changing only signposting and emphasis. **Practice/check:** listener reconstructs claim, evidence, and limitation. **Success:** listener identifies the intended relation without seeing the notes. **Repair:** replace stock transitions with a phrase naming the relation.

### C1.U03.L03 — Respond and repair

**Outcome:** answer a challenge question relevantly and clarify one ambiguous term. **Prerequisite:** U03.L01–L02. Teach `I would distinguish...`, `What I mean by...`, and acknowledging genuinely new information. **Practice:** deliver the clinic recommendation; partner asks one evidence question and one cost question. **Assessment:** C1-T-U03 transfer. **Success:** direct response, accurate qualification, and successful clarification/repair. **Repair:** restate the question in a few words before answering.

### C1.U04.L01 — Infer stance from cues

**Outcome:** support a stance inference with two wording/context cues and label it as inference. **Prerequisite:** B2 source-framing. Annotate “The launch”; separate explicit facts (delay, draft screen, rescheduling) from possible evaluation. **Practice/check:** give a literal reading and a contextual reading. **Success:** two cues cited; no inferred emotion is stated as fact. **Repair:** use the frame “The text states…; this may suggest… because…”.

### C1.U04.L02 — Interpret irony cautiously

**Outcome:** compare a literal and a plausible ironic reading and state what remains uncertain. **Prerequisite:** U04.L01. Explain that irony depends on contextual mismatch, not a single word; compare “useful rehearsal” with the later rescheduling. **Practice/check:** analyze the teacher-read script's “one way to read the figures.” **Success:** the challenge is recognized while hostility/sarcasm is not assumed. **Repair:** propose an alternative earnest interpretation and identify what evidence would distinguish it.

### C1.U04.L03 — Paraphrase implication

**Outcome:** produce a neutral paraphrase and a clearly labeled inference from the same input. **Prerequisite:** U04.L01–L02. Model how to retain a cautious challenge without attributing an unstated motive. **Practice/check:** write two versions of the listening line. **Assessment:** C1-T-U04 transfer. **Success:** explicit content and inference remain distinct and cue-supported. **Repair:** remove mental-state verbs unless the source supports them.

### C1.U05.L01 — Compare methods and constructs

**Outcome:** state what two sources measure and whether they support a common outcome claim. **Prerequisite:** B2 source attribution. Use Sources A/B in the pack; model preference versus tutor observation. Learners complete a source-by-question grid. **Practice/check:** answer what the sources establish and what they do not. **Success:** no learning-effect claim is inferred from preference or tutor views alone. **Repair:** add a “not measured” row to the grid.

### C1.U05.L02 — Synthesize sources and communicate uncertainty

**Workshop:** see [`resources/integrated-skill-labs.md`](../resources/integrated-skill-labs.md).

### C1.U05.L03 — Synthesize and qualify

**Outcome:** write a 70–100-word synthesis that attributes both sources, identifies one shared theme and one difference, and proposes no unsupported conclusion. **Prerequisite:** U05.L01 and the existing workshop C1.U05.L02. Model organizing by question rather than source order. **Practice/check:** annotate every clause as source A, source B, or inference. **Assessment:** C1-T-U05 transfer. **Success:** attribution and distinction are accurate; no causal outcome is invented. **Repair:** rewrite source-by-source notes under one shared question.

### C1.U06.L01 — Listen for interests

**Outcome:** distinguish a stakeholder's stated position from the interest or constraint it may represent. **Prerequisite:** B2 counterargument response. Read “A shared deadline”; map positions (Friday, preserve uncertainty) to interests (verification, fidelity). **Practice/check:** infer only where a cue exists. **Success:** label proposals as proposals and do not assign motives as facts. **Repair:** ask a clarifying question instead of treating an inferred interest as certain.

### C1.U06.L02 — Negotiate conditional options

**Outcome:** formulate an option with a clear condition and confirm whether it is accepted. **Prerequisite:** U06.L01. Model `I can support X if Y` versus unconditional agreement, using the listening script. Learners role-play with the pack roles and record agreed, conditional, and unresolved items. **Practice/check:** partner restates the decision. **Success:** every condition is retained and no discussion is reported as consensus. **Repair:** use a three-column decision record.

### C1.U06.L03 — Mediate the decision

**Outcome:** relay the decision and one unresolved issue to a new audience without altering either. **Prerequisite:** U06.L01–L02. Learners produce a neutral decision note and a short oral briefing. **Assessment:** C1-T-U06 transfer. **Success:** condition, common ground, and unresolved matter are all accurately represented. **Repair:** compare each sentence with the role notes and mark it agreement, condition, proposal, or unresolved.

## 12. Assessment route and progression

Use the ungraded C1 diagnostic before instruction, the linked formative C1-T-U01…U06 checks after each unit, and C1-END-01 after fresh-topic practice. The pack provides the teaching inputs; the assessment file supplies prompts, keys, open-task criteria, score boundaries, and local readiness rules. Prepared materials are not learner results. The local rule is unpiloted and is not CEFR certification.

### B2-to-C1 transition evidence

Before entering this route, collect evidence that the learner can organize a supported position, distinguish a claim from evidence, answer a counterargument, and produce a connected text. These are local prerequisites, not an official B2 gate. C1 adds source synthesis, cue-supported inference, controlled register and audience changes, and negotiation with conditions.

### C1-to-C2 readiness evidence

Use at least two fresh tasks: one extended synthesis/editing task and one interaction task. Look for accurate attribution, qualified interpretation, clear argument structure, and repair after challenge. Require successful transfer after feedback; do not infer readiness from one test score or a vocabulary list. The local C1-END-01 threshold is provisional and unpiloted.
