# B1 — Intermediate

## 1. Purpose and scope

B1 is the stage of growing independence. Learners can discuss experience, explain reasons, narrate events, express opinions, and write connected paragraphs on familiar topics. This course treats B1 as a real, teachable stage in which learners move from short functional communication to sustained, reasonably independent use in everyday and social situations. It is a course-level design description, not an official CEFR certificate or an unchecked descriptor mapping.

The stage assumes successful A2 outcomes or equivalent: accurate basic past and present forms, basic talk about routines, good command of a short practical message, and a dependable ability to ask for clarification. See `REFERENCES.md` for the source limits and `resources/integrated-skill-labs.md` for a linked B1 workshop.

## 2. Learning outcomes

By the end of B1, learners can:

- explain opinions and give reasons with examples
- narrate recent experiences and earlier life events in a clear sequence
- describe problems, suggest solutions, and justify a recommendation
- understand and summarize straightforward information from longer spoken or written texts
- participate in sustained conversation on familiar topics and social issues
- write connected paragraphs and short formal/informal messages with appropriate linking devices
- use a wider range of adverbials, comparisons, and conditionals with reasonable control
- ask for clarification, reformulate ideas, and indicate uncertainty when meaning is not fully clear

## 3. Grammar and discourse progression

- simple and complex conditionals in clear everyday contexts
- basic relative clauses
- passive in common contexts (`The bus is delayed.`, `The report was sent.`)
- past continuous and past perfect basics
- reported speech in simple forms
- linking devices: `however`, `therefore`, `although`, `in addition`, `as a result`, `on the other hand`
- comparative and quantity expressions
- short formal/informal style shifts appropriate to the task
- source-based summary and explanation of a simple decision or recommendation

## 4. Unit overview and sequence

The teaching sequence is organized into six units, each with its own lesson set and task cycle. The unit labels below are used for continuity and tracking only; the actual teaching content appears in the lesson sections that follow.

- B1.U01 — Experiences and narratives: personal stories, time order, change over time, and short reflective summaries.
- B1.U02 — Opinions and reasons: formulating views, giving examples, and responding to a different opinion.
- B1.U03 — Problem solving and advice: diagnosing a realistic issue, comparing options, and recommending a solution.
- B1.U04 — Work and study contexts: responsibilities, routines, short reports, and planning under constraints.
- B1.U05 — Society and public issues: balanced discussion, local decision-making, and concise public-issue mediation.
- B1.U06 — Media and technology: evaluating information, comparing formats, and reporting a recommendation clearly.

Each unit is paired with original reading and teacher-read listening input, vocabulary in context, language retrieval, a speaking/mediation task, and a writing transfer task in [`resources/b1-b2-learning-pack.md`](../resources/b1-b2-learning-pack.md). The selected unit glossary is in [`resources/b1-b2-vocabulary.md`](../resources/b1-b2-vocabulary.md), and targeted language explanations are in [`resources/grammar-explanations.md`](../resources/grammar-explanations.md). Unit checks and a fresh stage-end assessment with answer keys and separate skill scores are in [`resources/b1-b2-assessment.md`](../resources/b1-b2-assessment.md). B1 extends A2 by moving from short practical narration/messages to connected explanation and reasoned decisions; the pack's progression table maps this bridge and the B2 extension. Teacher-read scripts remain available; selected scripts have supplemental experimental Amy recordings, not validated assessment audio.

## 5. Detailed teach-ready lesson sequence

The lessons below build the six units into actual teaching content. Each lesson includes a measurable goal, a prerequisite, explanation, guided and independent practice, answer keys or criteria, and a repair path. Teacher-read scripts are text assets; selected linked synthetic Amy recordings are experimental and do not establish listening or pronunciation skill.

### B1.U01 — Experiences and narratives

### B1.U01.L01 — Tell a personal story with time order

**Goal:** narrate a short personal event in sequence using at least four past forms and three sequence markers. **Prerequisite:** A2 past time and common irregular verbs.

Use past simple for finished events: `I went to the coast last weekend.` Use past continuous for an action in progress in the background: `I was waiting when my cousin arrived.` Use past perfect for an earlier event before another past event: `When we arrived, the shop had closed.` Do not use past perfect unless the time relation is clear. Arabic: «ذهبت إلى الشاطئ» / «كنت أنتظر عندما وصل صديقي».

**Guided practice:** complete: `When we ___ (arrive), the museum ___ (already close).` **Key:** arrived; had already closed. Reorder: `after that / then / first / finally` into a story sequence.

**Independent speaking:** tell a real or imagined event from last week using six events and three connectives. **Check:** at least four correct past forms and a clear chronological order. **Remedy:** sort events into `before`, `during`, and `after` and retell. **Links:** A2.U01; B1.U01.L02.

### B1.U01.L02 — Describe change and experience

**Goal:** express how life changed, using a past state, a contrast, and a present result. **Prerequisite:** B1.U01.L01.

`When I was younger, I used to walk to school.` describes a repeated past habit. `I have changed a lot since I started work.` describes a present result from a past process. `I had never seen snow before that trip.` emphasizes an earlier before-now experience.

**Guided:** rewrite: `When I was younger, I loved football.` → `When I was younger, I used to love football.` **Key:** accepted. **Reading:** “Before I moved to this city, I lived with my parents. I had never worked full-time, and I was nervous at first. Now I feel more confident.” Which idea is the strongest result? **Key:** the speaker feels more confident now.

### B1.U01.L03 — Summarize a personal experience

**Goal:** give a short spoken summary of an experience with reason, result, and reflection. **Prerequisite:** B1.U01.L01–L02.

Use `At first`, `Later`, `In the end`, and `Overall` for organization. `I thought the task was difficult at first, but I managed it in the end.` `The experience taught me to be more patient.` Distinguish `I was tired` (feeling) from `I got tired` (change). A summary is not a full story; it is the key events and what they taught you.

**Listening script:** “I was nervous before the interview. I had prepared my answers, but I still felt unsure. After the first question, I relaxed and spoke more naturally. In the end, I got the job.”

### B1.U02 — Opinions and reasons

### B1.U02.L01 — Express an opinion clearly

**Goal:** state and justify an opinion using at least three reason phrases. **Prerequisite:** A2 opinion words and cause/effect connectors.

Use `I think`, `In my opinion`, `I believe`, and `From my point of view`. Add reasons with `because`, `since`, `for example`, `which is why`, `that is why`. `Even though` and `although` introduce a contrast. `On the other hand` introduces another perspective.

**Guided practice:** complete: `I think public transport should be cheaper because ___ .` **Key:** many people rely on it; it reduces traffic; etc. **Reading:** “Online learning is flexible, but it can feel isolating. I prefer a mixed model because it combines independence with teacher support.” Which word introduces contrast? **Key:** `but`.

**Independent speaking:** discuss a familiar topic such as study methods, transport, or city life. **Check:** 3 clear reasons and one contrast or example. **Remedy:** gather reasons in a list before building full sentences. **Links:** B1.U02.L02; B1.U05.

### B1.U02.L02 — Build a paragraph with reasons and examples

**Goal:** write a 120–150-word opinion paragraph with a clear point, reasons, and a conclusion. **Prerequisite:** B1.U02.L01.

A paragraph often follows: claim → reason 1 → example → reason 2 → conclusion. `For example`, `such as`, and `in particular` help detail. Avoid overusing one connector; vary the linking words. `Therefore` and `as a result` show consequence. `However` marks contrast.

**Guided model:** “I believe students should have more project-based learning. It helps them apply theory to real problems. For example, a class can design a school campaign or a small business plan. This method also improves cooperation. Therefore, it prepares students better for work.”

**Independent writing:** choose a topic and write a paragraph. **Check:** clear topic sentence, at least two reasons, one example, and a closing sentence. **Remedy:** underline the topic sentence and each reason before revising. **Links:** B1.U02.L01; B1.U05.L02.

### B1.U02.L03 — Respond to a counterargument

**Goal:** acknowledge an opposing view and still defend a position with evidence or experience. **Prerequisite:** B1.U02.L01–L02.

`It is true that ...` and `I understand why some people prefer ...` show respect. `However`, `although`, and `even if` help contrast. `The main problem is ...` introduces the stronger point. `What matters most is ...` moves to the core. In B1, this skill is often more important than absolute certainty.

**Role-play:** discuss whether school uniforms are a good idea. Partner A argues in favour. Partner B opposes. Switch roles after 90 seconds. **Check:** one concession, one counterargument, and one justification. **Remedy:** note “yes, but” frames and add a reason. **Links:** B1.U05; B1.U06.

### B1.U03 — Problem solving and advice

### B1.U03.L01 — Define the problem and the cause

**Goal:** identify a realistic problem, explain its cause, and describe its effect in a short spoken or written answer. **Prerequisite:** A2 problem language and time expressions.

Use `The problem is ...`, `The main cause is ...`, `This leads to ...`, and `As a result, ...`. Good advice begins with a problem, not by jumping immediately to the solution. `The bus system is unreliable because routes overlap and drivers are under pressure.`

**Guided reading:** “Many students complain that the library closes too early. They cannot finish group work after six, and they study late in the evening. The main reason is limited staffing.” Which is the main cause? **Key:** limited staffing. What is the direct effect? **Key:** students cannot finish group work after six.

### B1.U03.L02 — Recommend a solution and weigh a trade-off

**Goal:** recommend a realistic solution, explain why it helps, and identify a trade-off. **Prerequisite:** B1.U03.L01; conditional patterns.

`If the city adds a bus lane, buses will move faster.` introduces a likely result. `If students studied more regularly, they would feel less stressed.` is more hypothetical. `However`, `but`, and `although` help show the cost or limit: `The plan could reduce delays, but it would remove some parking spaces.` 

**Guided practice:** complete `If the library opens later, ____ .` **Key:** more students will be able to study there. **Reading:** “The city should test a later bus service for three months and publish the results.” Which part is the recommendation? **Key:** test a later bus service and publish results. What is the trade-off? **Key:** cost / service resources / reduced parking etc., depending on the exact case.

### B1.U03.L03 — Discuss alternatives and choose one

**Goal:** compare two or three options and reach a practical decision with reasons. **Prerequisite:** B1.U03.L02.

When discussing options, say `On the one hand ... On the other hand ...` and then conclude with `Overall ...` or `Ultimately ...`. Good decisions use evidence and practical constraints: time, cost, and impact. `The park project is cheaper, but the bus route is more useful to commuters.`

**Group task:** choose one improvement for a school, library, or neighborhood. Each learner presents one idea with a benefit and a drawback. Then the group agrees on one change and explains why. **Check:** all members speak, at least one reason and one drawback are named, and the final decision is supported by the group. **Remedy:** use a decision table with `option / benefit / cost / final choice`. **Links:** B1.U03.L02; B1.U05.

### B1.U04 — Work and study contexts

### B1.U04.L01 — Describe responsibilities and routine work

**Goal:** describe work or study responsibilities using present simple and adverbs of frequency. **Prerequisite:** A1/A2 routine patterns.

`I am responsible for scheduling appointments.` `She often checks the inventory.` `We have to prepare reports every Friday.` For a specific, ongoing task, use present continuous: `I am helping with the new project this week.`

**Guided practice:** choose the correct form: `At the moment, I (manage / am managing) a small team.` **Key:** am managing. `She usually (checks / is checking) the stock before lunch.` **Key:** checks.

**Independent speaking:** describe a job or study routine in five sentences. **Check:** at least three correct verbs and clear time language. **Remedy:** sort verbs into `routine` vs `temporary/now` categories. **Links:** A1.U04; B1.U04.L02.

### B1.U04.L02 — Write a short report or summary

**Goal:** organize and summarize a work or study issue in a clear sequence. **Prerequisite:** B1.U04.L01.

A short report often uses `Firstly`, `Secondly`, `Finally`, and `Overall`. Example: `Firstly, the issue affected student attendance. Secondly, it caused delays in admin tasks. Finally, a clearer timetable solved the problem.` Use evidence, not just emotion: `The survey showed ...` or `After the change, attendance rose by 12%`.

### B1.U04.L03 — Explain a difficult situation and a plan

**Goal:** explain a problem, suggest a plan, and give a realistic timeline. **Prerequisite:** B1.U04.L01–L02.

Use `The main problem is...`, `If we ... then ...`, `We need to ... before ...`, and `By next month, ...`. `The team has been late because the software was not ready.` `We will finish the training in two weeks.`

**Role-play:** manager and employee discuss a delay. Candidate language: `We need an updated plan.`, `If we reduce the scope, we can finish on time.`, `By Friday, we should have a revised schedule.` **Check:** plan, timeline, and reason are all clear. **Remedy:** write a short plan with dates before speaking. **Links:** B1.U03; B1.U04.L02.

### B1.U05 — Society and public issues

### B1.U05.L01 — Discuss benefits and drawbacks

**Goal:** compare two options and present balanced views on a public issue. **Prerequisite:** B1.U02 and B1.U03.

Useful phrases: `On the one hand ...`, `On the other hand ...`, `A clear advantage is ...`, `The main disadvantage is ...`, `It may be cheaper, but ...`.

**Reading:** “Many cities encourage cycling. A bike lane reduces congestion and improves health, but it may reduce parking space. The main challenge is that drivers need time to adapt.” What are the benefits? **Key:** reduces congestion and improves health. What is the disadvantage? **Key:** less parking space.

### B1.U05.L02 — Write a balanced paragraph

**Goal:** write a 150–180-word paragraph that presents a problem, two sides, and a recommendation. **Prerequisite:** B1.U05.L01.

A balanced paragraph begins with the issue, briefly states two positions, then shows a judgment. Use `although`, `however`, `in addition`, and `therefore` for flow. Avoid writing only personal opinion without evidence or examples.

**Guided model:** “Public transport is often cheaper and more sustainable, but many commuters still prefer cars. The main advantage is lower pollution and lower cost. However, cars are often seen as more comfortable and convenient. In my view, better bus services and cleaner stations would reduce the gap.”

### B1.U05.L03 — Speak and mediate on a public issue

**Goal:** summarize a discussion and relay a position neutrally to another person. **Prerequisite:** B1.U05.L01–L02.

Mediation includes preserving key facts, not inventing details. Use `The group agreed that ...`, `One concern was ...`, and `The final recommendation was ...`. For a neutral summary, avoid adding personal judgments unless the task asks for them.

**Group discussion:** local transport, school start times, or cost of study materials. Each group decides one recommendation. Then a spokesperson reports to another group. **Check:** the spokesperson restates the recommendation, the reason, and one objection. **Remedy:** list three bullet points before speaking. **Links:** B1.U05.L02; B1.U06.

### B1.U06 — Media and technology

### B1.U06.L01 — Evaluate information and source quality

**Goal:** compare two media sources, assess their reliability, and identify a main claim. **Prerequisite:** B1.U02 and general media vocabulary.

Not all sources are equally reliable. Use `This source is reliable because ...`, `The article is biased because ...`, `The information is useful, but it is incomplete.` `Evidence`, `claims`, and `limitations` are useful academic and discussion terms. Avoid claiming certainty from one small sample.

### B1.U06.L02 — Compare digital and face-to-face learning

**Goal:** discuss and justify a preference between two learning modes. **Prerequisite:** B1.U02 and social/education vocabulary.

Use `I prefer ... because ...`, `In my view, ...`, `Although online classes are flexible, ...`, and `The strongest advantage is ...` Use evidence and consequences. `A blended model` is a common B1 solution: combining benefits while reducing drawbacks.

**Guided speaking:** “Online study is flexible, but classroom learning gives stronger feedback.” Build a balanced answer. **Key:** accepted answers vary but must mention both benefits and drawbacks. **Independent writing:** 120–150 words comparing online and in-person study. **Check:** contrast, at least two reasons, conclusion. **Remedy:** compare in a table with `online / face-to-face / my choice`. **Links:** B1.U06.L01; B1.U06.L03.

### B1.U06.L03 — Write and relay a recommendation about media or technology

**Goal:** write a short recommendation about digital habits or online study and relay it clearly to another person. **Prerequisite:** B1.U06.L01–L02.

A strong recommendation should name the issue, propose a change, and justify it. `I recommend a shorter screen schedule during study hours because students often lose focus after the first hour.` Use `In conclusion` or `Overall` for the final sentence. 

**Independent writing:** 120–150 words recommending a digital habit change in a school, workplace, or family context. **Check:** issue, action, reason, and conclusion. **Remedy:** plan: problem / recommendation / reason / evidence / conclusion. **Links:** B1.U06.L01–L02; B2.U01.

## 6. Reading sample and skills integration

### Reading sample

`In many cities, public transport has improved, but congestion still causes delays. Many people prefer cars because they feel more comfortable, even though public transport is often cheaper and safer. In my opinion, governments should invest more in buses and trains if they want to reduce pollution and save time.`

**Questions:**
- What is the problem described?
- Why do people still prefer cars?
- What is the proposed solution?
- What is the writer’s opinion?

**Key:** delays and congestion; comfort; invest more in buses and trains; governments should reduce pollution and save time.

**Mastery criteria:**
- explain opinions and reasons in a clear sequence with relevant examples
- write coherent paragraphs with cause/effect, contrast, and recommendation language
- understand longer instructions and spoken passages that contain some new vocabulary and a clear point of view
- maintain discussion on familiar topics with moderate independence and repair strategies

These are course outcomes, not validated descriptor-level CEFR claims. Exposure, task completion, performance on a check, and a mastery decision must be recorded separately.

## 9. Common errors and support

**Common errors:**
- weak paragraph cohesion
- repeated use of `because` and `but` without structural variety
- tense shifts in narrative tasks
- unclear main point in opinion writing
- narrow connectors without contrast or result language

**Support:**
- provide model paragraph frames with claim, reason, example, result, and conclusion
- encourage one strong idea per paragraph
- revise connectors and sequencing words
- retell a short experience with a time line before writing
