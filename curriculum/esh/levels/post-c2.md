# Post-C2 — Advanced specialization tracks

## 1. Purpose and scope

Post-C2 is a specialized extension for learners who already have strong C2-level competence. It is not a new CEFR level; it is an extension for advanced professional, academic, and editorial work.

## 2. Core tracks

### POST-C2.T01 — Academic English and research communication

Audience:
- students, researchers, academics

Focus:
- research reading
- literature review writing
- evidence-based argumentation
- oral presentation of findings

**Prerequisite:** strong C2-level source attribution, qualified claims, and distinctions between evidence and interpretation.

### POST-C2.T02 — Business and professional English

Audience:
- managers, project teams, consultants, professionals

Focus:
- negotiation, proposals, briefings, strategic communication
- persuasive and precise workplace English

**Prerequisite:** strong C2-level register adaptation, conditional commitments, and accurate mediation.

### POST-C2.T03 — Advanced writing and editing

Audience:
- editors, writers, researchers, communicators

Focus:
- sentence-level precision
- tone control
- revision and editing logic
- clarity, brevity, and flow

**Prerequisite:** strong C2-level control of scope, modality, register, and source fidelity.

### POST-C2.T04 — Public speaking and discourse leadership

Audience:
- presenters, moderators, advocates, trainers

Focus:
- speech structure
- audience adaptation
- rhetorical delivery
- handling challenging questions

**Prerequisite:** strong C2-level argument structure, pragmatic interpretation, and interactional repair.

### POST-C2.T05 — Translation and advanced textual analysis

Audience:
- translators, analysts, communicators

Focus:
- register and nuance comparison
- meaning transfer across languages
- layered textual interpretation

**Prerequisite:** strong C2-level pragmatic interpretation and audience mediation, plus competence in the chosen language pair.

### POST-C2.T06 — Discourse analysis and rhetoric

Audience:
- researchers, analysts, language specialists

Focus:
- framing, rhetoric, persuasion, and linguistic patterning
- explicit explanation of style and situational meaning

**Prerequisite:** strong C2-level analysis of entailment, presupposition, implicature, stance, and rhetorical emphasis.

## 3. Shared requirements

All tracks require:

- precise vocabulary and collocation
- rhetorical flexibility
- evidence-based reasoning
- style adaptation for audience and purpose
- control of complexity without loss of clarity

## 4. Sample assessment patterns

- research brief and summary
- business proposal or briefing
- editorial revision task
- formal speech with Q&A
- translation or mediation task with commentary

## 5. Program note

This stage should be taught through authentic tasks, not only through abstract vocabulary lists. Advanced learners benefit most from real communication work that uses discipline-specific language in context.

## 6. Course design and progression

Post-C2 is a project-designed specialization extension, not a CEFR level and not a claim of limitless proficiency. Learners select a track after a diagnostic portfolio review. Each track has three lessons: analyze a model, practice a controlled decision, and transfer the method to an unfamiliar text or audience. The six tracks share a source-fidelity rule: separate what a source states, what the learner infers, and what remains unknown.

The existing workshop `POST-C2.T03.L01` in [`resources/integrated-skill-labs.md`](../resources/integrated-skill-labs.md) is retained as an additional editorial/translation clinic. The lessons below do not redefine that ID. Full supporting materials and project briefs are in [`resources/post-c2-learning-pack.md`](../resources/post-c2-learning-pack.md); checks, keys, and project rubrics are in [`resources/post-c2-assessment.md`](../resources/post-c2-assessment.md).

### Track T01 — Academic English lesson sequence

**Specialist outcome:** evaluate the relation among a research question, method, evidence, and claim; synthesize sources without treating different measures as equivalent; communicate limitations and implications accurately. **Prerequisite:** C2 source attribution, qualified claims, and evidence/interpretation distinctions. Use invented case materials in the lessons; real research topics require subject-matter review.

#### POST-C2.T01.L01 — Read a study as an argument

**Outcome:** annotate a short abstract for question, sample, method, result, limitation, and claim, and identify one claim that exceeds the reported evidence. **Instruction:** read each clause for its role rather than relying on academic-sounding vocabulary. A result is not automatically a causal explanation. Example: “In a voluntary survey of 84 staff, 61% preferred the new rota. The study did not compare absence rates.” This supports a statement about reported preference among respondents, not a claim that the rota reduced absence or that all staff prefer it.

**Guided practice:** label the sample, measure, and missing outcome in the example; **key:** 84 voluntary respondents; stated preference; absence-rate comparison. **Independent task:** annotate a short abstract supplied by the learner or teacher and mark one inference as supported, plausible-but-untested, or unsupported. **Check:** all six abstract elements are located or explicitly marked “not reported”; the proposed overclaim is justified with a textual cue. **Repair:** use a six-column evidence table and rewrite each claim beginning “The abstract reports…”.

#### POST-C2.T01.L02 — Synthesize sources and qualify a claim

**Outcome:** produce a thematic synthesis of two sources, preserving their different constructs and limitations. **Instruction:** organize by a shared research question, not by “Source A says / Source B says.” Attribution verbs identify the source’s move; they do not upgrade its evidence.

**Model evidence cards:** A reports that 61% of 84 voluntary respondents preferred the rota; B reports that 9 of 12 supervisors considered handovers clearer after a trial. Neither measures absence or patient outcomes. A faithful synthesis is: “Staff respondents favored the rota, while most interviewed supervisors described clearer handovers; the small, self-selected samples and different measures do not establish an effect on absence or patient outcomes.” It would be inaccurate to say both studies prove the rota works.

**Guided practice/key:** What construct does A measure? stated preference. What does B capture? supervisor judgment about handovers. Do they establish patient impact? no. **Independent task:** write 120–160 words that answers a question selected by the teacher, attributes both sources, names one shared theme and one difference, and identifies the evidence gap. **Check:** both sources and their measures are accurately distinguished; the synthesis does not invent causation. **Repair:** color-code source claims, writer inference, and limitation; revise any sentence that merges them.

#### POST-C2.T01.L03 — Write and present a research brief

**Outcome:** create a concise research brief with a question, method, findings, limitation, and appropriately bounded implication. **Instruction:** use APA-style author–date citations only when the course or institution requires APA; otherwise follow the specified house style consistently. A citation records provenance; it does not guarantee source quality. Do not invent publication details.

**Model:** “The pilot survey found a preference among respondents for the revised rota (Lee, 2024). Because participation was voluntary and no absence data were collected, the result should not be read as evidence of improved attendance.” The citation here is a fictional classroom placeholder, not a real source.

### Track T02 — Professional English lesson sequence

**Specialist outcome:** turn incomplete operational information into a clear, audience-specific recommendation, negotiate constraints, and communicate responsibilities and uncertainty without promising unsupported outcomes. **Prerequisite:** C2 register adaptation, conditional commitments, and accurate mediation.

#### POST-C2.T02.L01 — Frame a decision brief

**Outcome:** distinguish decision, evidence, risk, owner, deadline, and unresolved question in a short workplace brief. **Instruction:** lead with the decision needed; separate observed facts from forecasts and proposals. “The test processed 120 requests” is an observation; “the system will reduce costs” is a forecast that needs a basis.

**Worked example:** “Decision requested: approve a four-week limited trial. Evidence: the trial processed 120 requests; no comparison period is available. Risk: evening coverage is untested. Owner: operations lead. Review date: 30 June.” The number is a fictional exercise datum.

#### POST-C2.T02.L02 — Listen, clarify, and negotiate constraints

**Outcome:** capture each stakeholder’s condition, ask a clarifying question, and restate a proposed agreement accurately. **Instruction:** listen for scope and conditions, not just repeated keywords. Confirm who will do what by when; a preference is not a commitment.

**Outcome:** give a direct, respectful update that states the change, reason, effect, next step, and contact route. **Instruction:** respect is not vagueness. Do not hide a decision in nominalizations or imply a cause that is not known.

**Model:** “The launch is moving from 12 to 19 May because the accessibility review is not complete. The new date is confirmed. We will send the revised test schedule by Wednesday; please contact the project lead if the change creates a delivery risk.” The reason and dates are fictional.

### Track T03 — Writing and editing lesson sequence

**Specialist outcome:** diagnose and revise meaning, structure, cohesion, tone, and sentence-level choices while documenting consequential edits. **Prerequisite:** C2 control of scope, modality, register, and source fidelity. The preserved workshop `POST-C2.T03.L01` is an additional practice, not a replacement for this sequence.

#### POST-C2.T03.L01 — Editorial and translation review

**Workshop:** see [`resources/integrated-skill-labs.md`](../resources/integrated-skill-labs.md).

#### POST-C2.T03.L02 — Diagnose before rewriting

**Outcome:** annotate a draft for ambiguity, unsupported claims, repetition, hidden agency, and audience mismatch before editing it. **Instruction:** edit in passes: purpose and claim; paragraph order; evidence and reference; sentence clarity; tone and mechanics. A sentence can be grammatical yet misleading.

**Worked example:** “It was decided that implementation would be delayed due to concerns.” Questions: who decided? what concerns? is the delay confirmed? A neutral query to the author is better than inventing answers. If the record identifies the steering group and an unresolved safety review, write: “The steering group delayed implementation while the safety review remains unresolved.”

#### POST-C2.T03.L03 — Edit for flow, register, and fidelity

**Outcome:** produce two audience-specific revisions of the same short message and explain how wording affects emphasis and commitment. **Instruction:** use cohesion to show relationships, not to decorate. Shortening is not automatically improvement; preserve necessary conditions, actors, and qualifications.

**Source:** “The review team has not completed its checks. The release may move to Friday if the missing data arrive by noon Thursday.” **Model for staff:** “The review is still in progress. Release may move to Friday if the missing data arrive by noon Thursday.” **Model for executives:** “Friday remains a conditional release date; the missing data are due by noon Thursday.”

**Guided practice/key:** both models preserve incomplete review, conditionality, and deadline. Neither says Friday is confirmed. **Independent task:** edit a new 180–250-word notice for two audiences; provide a change log with three consequential edits and one query. **Check:** meaning and commitment are stable, both registers suit their readers, and each rationale points to evidence in the source. **Repair:** compare source and edit for actor, date, condition, certainty, and audience.

**Track project:** edit a 700–900-word public-facing report. Submit the annotated source, clean copy, three audience-specific edits, and a rationale log separating mandatory corrections from optional style choices. Score fidelity, structural coherence, clarity, register, query discipline, and rationale quality (0–3 each; 12/18 minimum; no silent resolution of factual ambiguity).

### Track T04 — Speaking and discourse leadership lesson sequence

**Specialist outcome:** build an evidence-led address, make its reasoning audible, chair disagreement fairly, and answer challenge questions without overstating evidence. **Prerequisite:** C2 argument structure, pragmatic interpretation, and repair strategies.

#### POST-C2.T04.L01 — Build an argument listeners can follow

**Outcome:** outline a talk with a question, claim, evidence, qualification, counterargument, and decision request. **Instruction:** each section must advance the answer; signposting should name the relationship between ideas. A compelling story is an illustration, not proof of frequency or causation.

**Model outline:** Question—should the library extend evening hours? Claim—run a limited trial. Evidence—fictional visitor comments request later access. Limit—no attendance-after-hours data exist. Counterargument—staffing costs. Decision—authorize a four-week trial with a usage and cost review.

**Guided practice/key:** the outline supports a trial, not permanent expansion; comments are qualitative evidence, not proof of demand level. **Independent task:** prepare a 3–4-minute argument from a new evidence card. **Check:** each claim has a source or is labeled as a proposal; the decision follows from stated evidence and limits. **Repair:** use a claim-to-evidence map and remove unsupported intensifiers.

#### POST-C2.T04.L02 — Handle questions and chair disagreement

**Outcome:** answer a challenge, clarify a term, and summarize two positions fairly before moving a discussion forward. **Instruction:** answer the question first; distinguish a request for evidence from disagreement about values. When you do not know, say what information is missing and how it can be checked.

**Model exchange:** “Does the pilot prove demand is higher?” “No. It records requests for later access, but not a comparative demand measure. We would need usage data from the trial to test that.” The answer is direct and proposes evidence without claiming it already exists.

**Guided practice/key:** the answer rejects “prove,” identifies the measured material, names the missing comparison, and proposes a test. **Independent task:** deliver an argument and respond to one evidence challenge and one value-based objection; chair a partner exchange and summarize both positions. **Check:** answer is relevant, uncertainty is retained, and each position is recognizable to its speaker. **Repair:** use “What I can say is… / What remains unknown is… / The next test would be…”.

#### POST-C2.T04.L03 — Rehearse delivery and adapt to audience

**Outcome:** adapt an argument for a specialist and a public audience while preserving its evidence and decision. **Instruction:** vary explanation and examples, not the underlying facts. Rehearsal can check timing, audibility, and transitions; without a recording or trained assessor it cannot establish a formal pronunciation score.

**Guided practice:** compare the specialist phrase “comparative utilization data” with “how many people use the service compared with before.” Both can be suitable; choose for audience and define the measure. **Independent task:** record or deliver two versions, then annotate one adaptation and one unchanged qualification. **Check:** both versions retain the same claim, limit, and decision; the explanation fits the audience. **Repair:** align both scripts in a claim/evidence/limit table before delivery.

**Track project:** deliver a 6–8-minute public address, provide a one-page evidence map, answer three unscripted questions, and chair a ten-minute structured discussion. Score reasoning, evidence/qualification, audience adaptation, response/repair, fair facilitation, and delivery organization (0–3 each; 12/18 minimum; delivery conditions recorded).

### Track T05 — Translation and mediation lesson sequence

**Specialist outcome:** analyze meaning and context before translation, produce audience-appropriate versions, identify consequential ambiguity, and justify choices without claiming equivalence where it is absent. **Prerequisite:** C2 pragmatic interpretation and audience mediation. Arabic examples below are teaching comparisons, not a claim that one Arabic variety suits every audience.

#### POST-C2.T05.L01 — Analyze the source before translating

**Outcome:** identify proposition, actor, time, modality, register, implied relationship, and unresolved ambiguity before drafting a translation. **Instruction:** create a source brief first. A dictionary equivalent does not settle context, genre, or tone.

**Source:** “We may be able to accommodate the request, provided the revised figures arrive by noon.” The message is cautious, conditional, and makes no promise that accommodation will happen. A translation that uses a definite “we will accommodate” changes the commitment.

**Guided practice/key:** modality = possibility; condition = revised figures by noon; outcome = not guaranteed. **Independent task:** complete a source brief for a short notice and flag one phrase requiring author clarification. **Check:** proposition, time, condition, and commitment are captured separately. **Repair:** underline every modal and conditional expression before translating.

#### POST-C2.T05.L02 — Compare translations and mediate meaning

**Outcome:** compare two translations for proposition, register, audience, and pragmatic effect; explain a preferred version and one residual loss. **Instruction:** do not label a version “wrong” solely because it is less literal. Judge whether it preserves function and relevant nuance for the stated audience.

**Source:** “Please note that the date is provisional.” **Version A:** “يرجى ملاحظة أن التاريخ مؤقت.” **Version B:** “يرجى العلم بأن الموعد المقترح غير نهائي.” For an event notice, B may clarify that this is a proposed date and not final; A is closer lexically but “مؤقت” can suggest temporary rather than unconfirmed. Context could change the choice.

**Guided practice/key:** both state non-finality imperfectly without context; B makes proposal status explicit, so use it only if “proposed” is supported. **Independent task:** compare two learner-generated translations of a new source, note the audience, and explain one alternative. **Check:** comparison cites specific wording and distinguishes source facts from translator inference. **Repair:** return to the source brief and mark unsupported additions.

#### POST-C2.T05.L03 — Revise translation and explain uncertainty

**Outcome:** deliver a translation plus a concise translator’s note identifying ambiguity, chosen interpretation, and a question that would resolve it. **Instruction:** use a query or note rather than silently deciding a consequential ambiguity. For mediation, relay the decision-relevant information and keep uncertainty visible.

**Worked example:** “The board will review the plan next week.” Ambiguity: “next week” may be relative to the date of the message; if the message is forwarded later, the reference may be unclear. Translator’s query: “Can we replace ‘next week’ with the calendar date?” Do not invent the date.

### Track T06 — Discourse analysis lesson sequence

**Specialist outcome:** analyze how wording, structure, evidence selection, and context shape interpretation while distinguishing textual observation from inferred effect. **Prerequisite:** C2 work on entailment, presupposition, implicature, stance, and rhetorical emphasis.

#### POST-C2.T06.L01 — Trace framing and agency

**Outcome:** compare two short accounts of the same event for actor, agency, evaluation, and omitted information. **Instruction:** describe observable choices before inferring motives. Passive voice can omit an actor, but omission alone does not prove deliberate concealment.

**Pair:** A: “The committee delayed the launch after the review.” B: “The launch was delayed after the review.” Both state delay and sequence; A names the committee, B does not. Neither sentence alone says why the committee acted or whether the delay was justified.

**Guided practice/key:** agency is explicit only in A; cause or motive is not established by either. **Independent task:** annotate a short public statement for actors, verbs, evaluative terms, and omissions; separate observation from inference in two columns. **Check:** every inference cites a linguistic cue and an alternative explanation is considered. **Repair:** rewrite each claim as “The text says…” or “This may suggest…”.

#### POST-C2.T06.L02 — Analyze stance, implicature, and audience

**Outcome:** explain an explicit proposition and one context-dependent implication, then state what additional context would support or weaken that reading. **Instruction:** an implicature is a defeasible contextual inference, not a hidden fact. Tone claims require evidence in wording and situation.

**Example:** “The report is certainly ambitious.” Literal proposition: the report is ambitious. Depending on context and prosody, “certainly” may be sincere or skeptical; the sentence alone does not settle the speaker’s attitude. A follow-up contrast or delivery may alter the reading.

#### POST-C2.T06.L03 — Evaluate rhetorical effects and write an analysis

**Outcome:** produce a short discourse analysis linking at least three textual features to plausible audience effects, with evidence and limits. **Instruction:** distinguish an effect that is plausible from one demonstrated in actual audiences. Parallelism can foreground contrast; it does not make the contrast true.

**Model:** “We counted the forms processed, not the cases resolved.” The parallel construction makes the measure distinction salient; it does not prove either number is high or low. A defensible analysis states the feature, textual cue, possible effect, and limit.

## 7. Cross-track transfer and completion rules

The six tracks build on C1/C2 attribution, qualification, register, audience adaptation, and source fidelity; they reuse these skills for specialist products rather than treating them as new grammar levels. Learners may take one track independently. Completion means submission of the named portfolio and meeting its local rubric threshold in a new task; it is not CEFR attainment or professional licensure. A teacher records task conditions, evidence, and reviewer identity. No learner work has been collected for this project, so no practical attainment is currently verified.
