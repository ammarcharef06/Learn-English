# B2 — Upper intermediate

## 1. Purpose and scope

B2 is the stage where learners move beyond everyday survival and into sustained argumentation, explanation, and more nuanced communication. They can handle more complex texts, weigh evidence, discuss decisions, and produce organized writing that is clear and appropriately formal for familiar contexts. This course treats B2 as a teachable phase of expansion rather than a claim of CEFR certification. It assumes learners have a reliable B1 base in paragraphing, reasons and opinions, structured advice, and short reading/listening comprehension.

## 2. Learning outcomes

By the end of B2, learners can:

- discuss social, educational, political, and cultural issues with a clear line of argument
- read and summarize more detailed texts, identifying the main claim, supporting evidence, and key limitation
- explain and defend a point of view with examples and counterargument
- write extended paragraphs and short essays with logical structure and accurate signposting
- choose an appropriate level of formality for different audiences and contexts
- respond to opposing views and refine a position using cautious and precise language
- interpret uncertainty, probability, and evidence without overstating conclusions
- deliver a short formal or semi-formal spoken presentation with a clear introduction, body, and conclusion

## 3. Grammar and discourse focus

- conditionals and hypothetical reasoning
- passive and nominalization in formal contexts
- caution, hedging, and qualification (`suggests`, `appears to`, `may indicate`, `it is reasonable to assume`)
- discourse markers and argument structure (`however`, `therefore`, `on the other hand`, `in contrast`, `as a result`)
- formal/neutral register management
- comparison and evaluation of evidence
- relative clauses and tighter sentence linking for coherence
- paraphrase and source attribution in reading and speaking tasks

## 4. Unit overview and sequence

The teaching sequence is organized into six units, each with a lesson set and a transfer task. The overview below identifies the unit arc without creating duplicate unit IDs.

- B2.U01 — Debate and argumentative structure: claim, evidence, counterargument, and conclusion.
- B2.U02 — Media and persuasion: assessing framing, bias, influence, and public claim-making.
- B2.U03 — Education and society: policy trade-offs, access, fairness, and balanced discussion.
- B2.U04 — Work, leadership, and environment: strategy, risk, long-term sustainability, and presentation.
- B2.U05 — Health, science, and technology: cautious evidence use, uncertainty, and responsible recommendation.
- B2.U06 — Culture, identity, and global issues: nuanced opinion, formal discussion, and respectful disagreement.

Each unit is paired with original reading and teacher-read listening input, contextual vocabulary, language retrieval, a speaking/mediation task, and a writing transfer task in [`resources/b1-b2-learning-pack.md`](../resources/b1-b2-learning-pack.md). ### B2.U01 — Debate and argumentative structure

### B2.U01.L01 — Identify a claim and weigh the evidence

**Goal:** distinguish a main claim from supporting evidence and explain whether the evidence is strong enough to support it. **Prerequisite:** B1 paragraphing and opinion language.

A claim is the main point; evidence is the reason or data that supports it. `The survey suggests ...` is more cautious than `The survey proves ...`. In B2, learners should learn to express qualified certainty: `The results are promising`, `The evidence is limited`, `This may be explained by ...`.

**Guided practice:** sort `claim`, `evidence`, and `limitation` cards. Example claim: “The new timetable improved student attendance.” Evidence: “Attendance rose from 72% to 81% in two months.” Limitation: “The sample covered only one department.”

**Reading:** “A city tested a slower school-start schedule for one term. Attendance improved and students reported lower stress. The pilot had 120 students and no comparison group.” Which word shows measurement? **Key:** `improved` and `120 students`. What is the key limitation? **Key:** no comparison group.

### B2.U01.L02 — Build a structured argument with cautious language

**Goal:** write a short argument paragraph that includes a claim, evidence, a limitation, and a response to a possible objection. **Prerequisite:** B1.U02 and B2.U01.L01.

An argument is stronger when it acknowledges uncertainty. `Although the trial was short, the pattern is encouraging.` `It is reasonable to assume ...` is a cautious, useful phrase. `Therefore` is appropriate when a conclusion follows clearly from the evidence; `however` introduces a contrast. Avoid claiming more than the data allow.

### B2.U01.L03 — Respond to a counterargument and refine a position

**Goal:** explain a counterargument and then defend the original position without becoming defensive or irrational. **Prerequisite:** B2.U01.L02.

Useful patterns: `Critics argue that ...`, `It is true that ...`, `Nevertheless ...`, `The stronger point is ...`, `What matters most is ...`. A good response distinguishes the weak version of the objection from the valid part. Example: `Critics say that the plan is expensive. It is true that the initial cost is high. Nevertheless, the long-term gains in health and productivity may outweigh it.`

### B2.U02 — Media and persuasion

### B2.U02.L01 — Analyze a media claim

**Goal:** identify the claim, the evidence, and the audience effect in a short media text. **Prerequisite:** B1.U06 and argument basics.

Writing in public media often appeals to emotion and authority as well as evidence. `The report claims that ...` helps distance the writer from a claim. `The article is persuasive because ...` is a useful evaluation sentence.

**Reading:** “A local campaign claims that a new shopping district will create jobs and improve the city center. The article uses interviews with business owners and optimistic language, but no independent figures are provided.” What is the claim? **Key:** new shopping district will create jobs and improve the city center. What is missing? **Key:** independent figures or data. How does the tone affect the argument? **Key:** optimistic language, with limited evidence.

### B2.U02.L02 — Evaluate bias and audience appeal

**Goal:** explain how a text tries to influence the audience and whether the argument is balanced or one-sided. **Prerequisite:** B2.U02.L01.

Bias is not simply a negative word; it is a pattern of selection or framing. `The text emphasizes ...` `The language is designed to appeal to ...` `It omits ...` `This makes the argument persuasive to a specific audience.` Use formal and cautious vocabulary when describing media strategies.

**Listening script:** “The campaign video presents the new policy as a safe and necessary step for families. It uses images of children and quiet streets, but it does not mention the cost or the disruption during the building phase.” What does the video emphasize? **Key:** safety and family concerns. What is missing? **Key:** cost and disruption.

**Independent writing:** write 150–180 words evaluating a short ad or campaign. **Check:** one claim, one method of persuasion, and one missing fact. **Remedy:** label `appeal`, `evidence`, and `bias` before drafting. **Links:** B2.U02.L01; B2.U04.

### B2.U02.L03 — Respond to a claim in a formal discussion

**Goal:** answer a media or policy claim politely but clearly, with a reason and a counterexample. **Prerequisite:** B2.U02.L01–L02.

Use `I would question that claim because ...`, `That may be true in some cases, but ...`, `The stronger view is ...`. A strong response brings one example or one practical limit. `One problem with that argument is that it ignores the cost to families who already depend on the service.`

### B2.U03 — Education and society

### B2.U03.L01 — Discuss social and educational inequality

**Goal:** explain a problem, identify a cause, and suggest a policy response. **Prerequisite:** B2.U01 and B1 public-issue writing.

A balanced policy discussion includes both the problem and the trade-off. `One major issue is ...` `This is partly because ...` `A practical response would be ...` `Although the cost is significant, ...` Use `fairness`, `access`, `inequality`, and `outcome` in formal expression.

**Reading:** “School transport is often unreliable in rural areas, which limits attendance and lowers opportunities for students who need extra support. A small grant for transport could increase access, but it would require local funding and oversight.” What is the problem? **Key:** unreliable transport. What is the proposed solution? **Key:** a small grant for transport. What is the trade-off? **Key:** local funding and oversight.

**Independent discussion:** choose one social issue and propose a policy. **Check:** problem, cause, and response all present. **Remedy:** use a `problem / cause / solution / trade-off` chart. **Links:** B2.U03.L02; B2.U05.

### B2.U03.L02 — Write a balanced policy paragraph

**Goal:** write 200–250 words arguing for or against a policy change in education, transport, or public services. **Prerequisite:** B2.U03.L01.

A strong policy paragraph ends with a realistic recommendation rather than a vague appeal. `The policy might improve access, but it would also require stronger resources and monitoring.` The best writing distinguishes between a short-term benefit and a wider, long-term issue.

**Guided model:** “A more flexible school timetable could improve wellbeing, yet it could also create timetable pressure for teachers and families.” Use comparison and nuance rather than a single extreme statement.

**Independent writing:** choose one policy question and write an argument with introduction, evidence, counterargument, and conclusion. **Check:** all four parts appear and the position is justified with examples. **Remedy:** draft a thesis sentence first, then add one evidence sentence and one objection sentence. **Links:** B2.U03.L01; B2.U06.

### B2.U03.L03 — Present and mediate a policy recommendation

**Goal:** speak for 1–2 minutes on a social issue and then relay the decision in neutral language. **Prerequisite:** B2.U03.L01–L02.

A spoken presentation should have: introduction, main reasons, limitation, and recommendation. In mediation, preserve the uncertainty: `The recommendation remains tentative until more data is available.`

**Group task:** propose one reform for a public issue. Each group prepares a 1-minute summary and then relays it to another group. **Check:** clarity, evidence, and concession. **Remedy:** use an outline: `issue / evidence / concern / recommendation`. **Links:** B2.U03.L02; B2.U05.

### B2.U04 — Work, leadership, and environment

### B2.U04.L01 — Explain a business or leadership issue

**Goal:** explain how a workplace issue affects employees and propose a next step. **Prerequisite:** B1 work/school context and B2 formal language.

`The challenge is not simply cost; it is the effect on morale and productivity.` `A sustainable solution should balance short-term pressure with long-term risk.` Good business language is precise and not exaggerated.

**Reading:** “A company reduced support staff to save costs, but the result was slower customer response and higher employee stress. Managers argued that the policy was temporary. Staff questioned whether it was sustainable.” Which term indicates a concern about long-term viability? **Key:** `sustainable`. What is the downside of the cost-saving policy? **Key:** slower customer response and higher stress.

**Independent speaking:** explain a workplace issue from a case card. **Check:** cause, effect, and one proposed solution. **Remedy:** use a `problem / effect / response` organizer. **Links:** B2.U04.L02; B1.U04.

### B2.U04.L02 — Write a brief recommendation for a policy or strategy

**Goal:** produce a concise recommendation with safeguards, risk, and evaluation. **Prerequisite:** B2.U04.L01.

Useful language: `The plan should be piloted ...`, `This reduces risk ...`, `A key safeguard is ...`, `The policy needs clear monitoring.` Also useful: `The main trade-off is ...` `The change may save time, but it could compromise quality.`

**Guided practice:** complete `A limited pilot is useful because ____ .` **Key:** it tests the effect without committing the whole organization. **Independent writing:** 180–220 words advising on a workplace or environmental improvement. **Check:** clear recommendation, at least two risks or safeguards, and a final decision. **Remedy:** state the problem first, then the proposed plan, then the measure of success. **Links:** B2.U04.L01; B2.U05.

### B2.U04.L03 — Deliver a short presentation and answer questions

**Goal:** present a structured viewpoint in 1–2 minutes and answer brief follow-up questions. **Prerequisite:** B2.U04.L01–L02.

Presentation language: `I would like to focus on ...`, `The main concern is ...`, `It is worth noting that ...`, `The strongest argument in favour is ...` In questions, use `Could you explain why ...?` `What would be the main risk?`

**Independent task:** present a workplace or environmental recommendation to a small group. **Check:** clear structure, audience awareness, and response to at least one follow-up question. **Remedy:** prepare a 3-part outline before speaking. **Links:** B2.U04.L02; B2.U05.

### B2.U05 — Health, science, and technology

### B2.U05.L01 — Discuss evidence in health and science communication

**Goal:** explain the difference between a claim, evidence, and a limitation in a health or science article. **Prerequisite:** B1/U06 evaluation language and B2.U01 evidence work.

`The data suggest ...` `The evidence is preliminary.` `There is still uncertainty about ...` `The study is small, so the findings cannot be generalized.` Good scientific discussion uses precise, cautious language. Avoid `proves` when the evidence is limited.

**Reading:** “A small study reported that a daily breathing exercise reduced stress in a sample of 30 adults. The report noted that results may be linked to motivation and self-selection, and it did not compare the exercise with other treatments.” What can be concluded? **Key:** the results are promising but limited. Why cautious? **Key:** small sample and no comparison group.

**Independent speaking:** choose a health or technology claim and explain the evidence and limitation. **Check:** explicit caution and one valid limit. **Remedy:** separate `what is claimed` from `what is actually supported`. **Links:** B2.U05.L02; B2.U01.

### B2.U05.L02 — Present a recommendation with conditions

**Goal:** formulate a recommendation under conditions, including a reason and a safeguard. **Prerequisite:** B2.U05.L01.

Use conditionals and qualification: `If the data are consistent across larger samples, the policy may be worth expanding.` `A reasonable next step would be ...` `This should be monitored for six months.` Good recommendations keep the condition clear.

**Guided practice:** “If the trial continues to show lower stress and no adverse side effects, ___ .” **Key:** the programme could be expanded. **Independent writing:** create a brief proposal for a health, science, or technology issue. **Check:** recommendation, condition, risk/safeguard, and mention of evidence. **Remedy:** plan with `claim / evidence / condition / next step`. **Links:** B2.U05.L01; B2.U06.

### B2.U05.L03 — Explain uncertainty and respond to a challenge

**Goal:** answer a challenge to a recommendation by acknowledging uncertainty and explaining the next evidence needed. **Prerequisite:** B2.U05.L01–L02.

Useful patterns: `That is a valid concern.`, `The current evidence is limited, but ...`, `We need to test ... before deciding.` Good technical discussion is not simply “yes” or “no”; it is a reasoned position under uncertainty.

**Discussion task:** compare two policies in health or technology settings. Each learner presents a recommendation and answers a challenge. **Check:** evidence, challenge response, and next evidence needed. **Remedy:** use `concern / evidence / need` feedback notes. **Links:** B2.U05.L02; B2.U06.

### B2.U06 — Culture, identity, and global issues

### B2.U06.L01 — Express a nuanced opinion on identity or culture

**Goal:** discuss a cultural or identity issue using respectful, balanced language. **Prerequisite:** B2.U03 and B1 opinion paragraphs.

Nuance means not saying all people agree or all people disagree. `Many people value ...`, `There are clear benefits, but ...`, `The issue is complex because ...` Good B2 discussion can capture complexity without losing clarity.

**Reading:** “Cultural exchange programmes can broaden understanding and improve young people’s confidence in communication, although some students still find local customs difficult to interpret. The main challenge is not opposition, but misunderstanding.” What is the central tension? **Key:** benefits of exchange versus difficulty of interpretation. What is the challenge? **Key:** misunderstanding rather than open rejection.

### B2.U06.L02 — Write a formal opinion essay

**Goal:** write a 250–300-word essay on a global or cultural issue, using thesis, supporting points, and a counterargument. **Prerequisite:** B2.U06.L01 and B2.U01.L02.

Formal essay language includes phrases like `It is important to note that ...`, `This raises a wider question about ...`, `In conclusion, ...` Use precise verbs and maintain tone. The essay should not slide into personal anecdote unless the task specifically asks for it.

**Independent writing:** choose one of the following: cultural exchange, migration, digital identity, or local language policy. **Check:** introduction, evidence, at least one counterargument, and conclusion. **Remedy:** draft a thesis first and test whether each paragraph supports it. **Links:** B2.U06.L01; B2.U04.

### B2.U06.L03 — Debate and clarify a position

**Goal:** take part in a formal group discussion, respond to disagreement, and summarize the final decision. **Prerequisite:** B2.U06.L01–L02.

A participatory discussion requires not only speech but listening. Use `Let me respond to that point ...`, `I see your concern, but ...`, `The key issue is ...`, `In summary, ...` Summaries should preserve the core argument and the main uncertainty.

**Group discussion:** address a policy issue about migration, media, education, or culture. Each learner states an initial claim, responds to a challenge, and then gives a summary of the group’s conclusion. **Check:** clear claim, respectful challenge response, and summary of final position. **Remedy:** write a brief summary sentence after each round of discussion. **Links:** B2.U06.L02; C1.U05.

**Mastery criteria:**
- produce a clear argument with a thesis, reasons, examples, and a response to a counterargument
- read and interpret more complex but accessible texts, identifying claim, evidence, and limits
- use clear signposting and a level-appropriate formal or neutral tone
- sustain discussion on familiar but more abstract issues with controlled accuracy

These are course outcomes, not validated descriptor-level CEFR claims. Exposure, task completion, performance on a check, and a mastery decision must be recorded separately.

## 9. Common errors and support

**Common errors:**
- weak or missing thesis statement
- unsupported or exaggerated claims
- overuse of informal language in formal writing
- unclear signposting between points
- using `prove` where the evidence is only suggestive

**Support:**
- present a thesis frame for each essay
- teach evidence–limitation–conclusion sequencing
- provide a discourse marker bank for argument paragraphs
- practise counterargument and response with small cases before longer writing
