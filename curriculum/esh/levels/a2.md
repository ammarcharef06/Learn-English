# A2 — Pre-intermediate

## 1. Purpose and scope

A2 expands communication from short survival exchanges to connected everyday communication. Learners describe personal experiences, plans, preferences, and practical situations with more detail, sequence, and control. This course uses A2 as a broad design target, not a quoted or descriptor-by-descriptor CEFR crosswalk; see `REFERENCES.md` for source-access limits.

**Prerequisite:** successful A1 outcomes or equivalent evidence in familiar exchanges, present-time descriptions, short messages, and basic reading/listening. Begin with a short retrieval check; reteach an individual gap rather than repeating the entire preceding level.
Selected unit vocabulary with contextual Arabic glosses, collocations, and examples is in `resources/a1-a2-vocabulary.md`; the set is illustrative rather than exhaustive.

## 2. Learning outcomes

By the end of A2, learners can:

- narrate past and recent experiences
- talk about plans, intentions, and predictions
- compare people, places, and things
- ask for services and explain simple problems
- describe health, travel, and social life
- write a short paragraph with sequence and contrast connectors
- use a basic present-perfect pattern for selected continuing situations and contrast it with a finished past time
- understand the main points and key details in short, clear messages on familiar topics
- manage a simple exchange about an immediate need, explaining a problem and checking the response
- connect a short account with time order and a reason, while keeping past events distinct from plans

## 3. Grammar and functional focus

- past simple
- present perfect basics
- `going to` and `will`
- comparatives and superlatives
- `should`, `must`, `have to`
- connectors: `because`, `but`, `so`, `although`, `when`, `before`

## 5. Detailed teach-ready lesson sequence

The lessons below develop all six unit outlines. Each has a measurable goal, prerequisite, explanation, guided and independent practice, a key or scoring criterion, and a repair path. Fixed-answer keys are given; personal stories and role-plays accept multiple valid responses. The integrated workshop `A2.U01.L03` is in `resources/integrated-skill-labs.md`. Listening scripts remain printed teacher-read source assets; selected activities link experimental synthetic Amy audio. Listening and pronunciation are not automatically scored.

### Lessons for unit A2.U01 — Past experiences

#### A2.U01.L01 — Build a past-time account

**Goal:** tell a six-event weekend account using at least four accurate past forms and three sequence markers. **Prerequisite:** A1 present simple, time expressions, common irregular verbs.

Use past simple for a finished time: `We went to the coast last Saturday.` Regular verbs often take `-ed`; common irregular forms must be learned individually (`go/went`, `have/had`, `see/saw`). `Was/were` describe past states; they do not replace the action verb: `We were tired, but we walked home.`

**Guided:** complete `I ___ (see) my cousin and we ___ (have) lunch.` **Key:** saw; had. Add `First`, `then`, `after that`, `finally` to put four picture cards in order.

**Event cards:** `leave home → meet a cousin → travel to the coast → eat lunch → walk by the water → return home`. **Independent speaking:** tell a real or imagined account using these or six learner-created events. **Check:** at least four accurate past forms and three sequence markers in logical order. **Remedy:** sort verb cards into regular/irregular and state/action; retell with new cards. **Links:** A1.U02 routines; A2.U01.L02.

#### A2.U01.L02 — Add a reaction and a reason

**Goal:** write a 70–90-word account with an event, reaction, reason, and contrast. **Prerequisite:** A2.U01.L01.

Join events with `and`, `then`, and `after`; use `because` for a reason and `but/although` for contrast. `Although the room was small, we enjoyed the stay.` Do not put `but` in the same clause structure immediately after `although` (`Although ..., but ...` is not standard in this pattern). Use `was/were` for description and action verbs for events.

**Reading:** `On Sunday, I took my niece to the science museum. We saw a robot show and made a paper bridge. The museum was busy, but the staff were helpful. I enjoyed the visit because my niece asked many questions.`

### Lessons for unit A2.U02 — Travel and services

#### A2.U02.L01 — Plan a journey from a timetable

**Goal:** retrieve four journey details and ask two accurate timetable questions. **Prerequisite:** A1 times, dates, places, present simple questions.

Timetables commonly use present simple for scheduled transport: `The train leaves at 10:30.` Use `Which platform does it leave from?` and `How long does the journey take?` `How long` asks about duration, not distance. Check date, departure, arrival, and platform separately.

**Timetable:** North Station → Hilltown; 09:20 depart, 10:05 arrive, platform 3; return 16:40.

**Guided/key:** departure? 09:20. Arrival? 10:05. Platform? 3. Journey duration? 45 minutes. Return departure? 16:40.

**Independent pair task:** use a second timetable card to plan a trip; ask a partner two questions and repeat the answer to confirm. **Check:** 4/5 details, both questions understandable, no confusion of departure and arrival. **Remedy:** mark the route first, then read the matching row left to right. **Links:** A1.U04 timetables; A2.U02.L02.

#### A2.U02.L02 — Explain a travel problem and request help

**Goal:** explain a travel problem and complete a four-turn help exchange, including one clarification. **Prerequisite:** polite requests and journey words.

Use past simple to report what happened (`I missed the bus`) and present forms for the current problem (`I don't know which platform`). `Could you tell me ...?` is a polite request. Explain the problem before asking for a solution; confirm the key detail after the answer.

**Guided:** choose `didn't catch / don't catch` for “The bus left ten minutes ago; I ___ it.” **Key:** didn't catch. **Reading:** `Platform 2 is closed. For trains to Hilltown, use platform 4. Ask staff if you need assistance.` Which platform? 4. What is closed? Platform 2.

#### A2.U02.L03 — Compare accommodation and confirm a booking

**Goal:** compare two lodging options and confirm three booking details in a role-play. **Prerequisite:** numbers/prices, comparatives, question forms.

Read a listing for exact included services; do not infer them. `The guesthouse is cheaper than the hotel, but it is farther from the station.` Use `How much is a room for two nights?` and confirm dates, room type, and total price.

**Listings:** Guesthouse: £42/night, breakfast included, 20 minutes from station. Hotel: £58/night, breakfast not included, 5 minutes from station.

**Guided/key:** Which is cheaper? guesthouse. Which is nearer the station? hotel. What is the hotel price for two nights before any extra charge? £116. State that breakfast is not included.

**Independent:** select based on a traveler profile, explain one trade-off, then role-play booking. **Check:** comparison accurate, all three details repeated, no invented amenities. **Remedy:** make a two-column evidence chart before deciding. **Links:** A2.U02.L01–L02.

### Lessons for unit A2.U03 — Health and body

#### A2.U03.L01 — Describe symptoms and ask for help

**Goal:** describe two symptoms, their duration, and one effect in a clear health-service exchange. **Prerequisite:** body/illness words, present and past time.

Use `I have a headache` for a symptom and `I've had a headache since Monday` for a situation that began in the past and continues now. `For two days` gives duration; `since Monday` gives a start point. This is a useful pattern, not a claim that every past-to-present meaning requires one tense.

Contrast a finished past time with a continuing situation: `I met Salma in 2021` (finished event) / `I've known Salma since 2021` (the relationship continues). Use `for` with a duration and `since` with a starting point. Some verbs and contexts favor one form; the time meaning and the verb both matter.

**Guided:** `I've been ill ___ two days / ___ Tuesday.` **Key:** for; since. `We ___ (meet) in 2021.` **Key:** met. `We ___ (know) each other since 2021.` **Key:** have known. **Listening (teacher reads twice):** “I've had a sore throat since yesterday. I feel tired, but I don't have a fever.” Ask symptom, start, fever? **Key:** sore throat; yesterday; no.

#### A2.U03.L02 — Give simple advice and understand instructions

**Goal:** give two relevant suggestions and follow three short care instructions. **Prerequisite:** symptom vocabulary and basic imperatives.

`Should` gives advice (`You should rest.`); `must` can express a strong requirement; `have to` expresses necessity, often from circumstances or rules. They are not always interchangeable. In an actual health concern, follow a qualified clinician; these language tasks are not medical advice.

**Instructions:** “Drink water. Take this medicine after food. Call the clinic if the fever gets worse.” **Questions/key:** When take medicine? after food. What to do if fever worsens? call clinic. Which action is hydration? drink water.

**Guided:** `You ___ rest today.` (should); `Visitors ___ check in at reception.` (have to / must, depending on rule context; both acceptable with stated rule). **Independent:** respond to a fictional mild-symptom card with two sensible language suggestions and one “ask a clinician” boundary.

**Check:** 3/3 instructions understood and advice form is grammatical/relevant. **Remedy:** match modal to advice/rule cards, explain the difference. **Links:** A2.U03.L01/L03.

#### A2.U03.L03 — Make an appointment and summarize instructions

**Goal:** arrange an appointment by phone and accurately relay three appointment details. **Prerequisite:** days/time, polite requests, symptoms.

Useful language: `I'd like to make an appointment.` `Is there anything available on Thursday?` `Could you repeat the time?` Confirm date, time, and location. In a health context, repeat instructions rather than paraphrasing if a detail is uncertain.

**Reading:** `Clinic: appointments Tuesday–Friday. Bring your appointment card. If you cannot attend, call before 9 a.m.` Ask available days, what to bring, and when to call if unable to attend. **Key:** Tuesday–Friday; appointment card; before 9 a.m.

### Lessons for unit A2.U04 — Work and daily obligations

#### A2.U04.L01 — Describe duties and schedules

**Goal:** describe four work/study duties and compare a weekly schedule using `have to` accurately. **Prerequisite:** present simple, days/time, common action verbs.

Use `have to` for a requirement: `I have to wear a badge at work.` Negative `don't have to` means no requirement, not prohibition. `Mustn't` means prohibited: `You mustn't share your password.` Distinguish this carefully. `She has to` adds third-person `-s`.

**Guided/key:** “It is optional to work late” → `You don't have to work late.` “It is forbidden to enter” → `You mustn't enter.` “Nora / submit a report” → `Nora has to submit a report.`

**Role card:** `Nora — Monday: check stock, reply to customers, and send a report. She has to wear a badge. She does not work on Wednesday.` **Independent:** use the card to explain three duties and one free period to a partner. **Check:** 4/5 modal/subject forms; correctly distinguish optional from forbidden. **Remedy:** sort requirement/no requirement/prohibition before choosing the form. **Links:** A1.U02 routines; A2.U04.L02.

#### A2.U04.L02 — Give advice and make a request at work

**Goal:** respond to a workplace problem with relevant advice and make one polite request. **Prerequisite:** obligation forms and polite requests.

`Should` offers advice (`You should save a copy.`); `could` makes a suggestion (`You could ask the team lead.`). Requests: `Could you send me the schedule, please?` Avoid presenting a preference as a rule: `You must ...` is strong and should be used only when a requirement exists.

**Reading:** `The shared printer is out of paper. The report is due at 3 p.m. Ask the office assistant for more paper or use the printer on floor two.` What is wrong? printer has no paper. When is report due? 3 p.m. What alternatives are stated? ask assistant/use floor two.

### Lessons for unit A2.U05 — Future plans and predictions

#### A2.U05.L01 — Explain intentions and arrangements

**Goal:** describe three plans and distinguish an intention from a fixed arrangement. **Prerequisite:** present continuous, future time expressions.

Use `going to` for an intention/plan (`I'm going to study tonight`). Present continuous often describes a definite arrangement (`I'm meeting Aya at six`). The boundary can overlap; context matters. Do not treat the forms as rigid rules based only on the calendar word.

**Guided:** choose the form that best fits: “We agreed on a table at 7; we ___ (meet) at the café.” **Key:** are meeting. “I bought paint because I ___ (decorate) my room.” **Key:** am going to decorate.

**Independent speaking:** explain a plan, reason, and one arrangement; partner asks when/where. **Check:** 3/4 choices fit context, arrangement details included. **Remedy:** mark “decided/intended” versus “agreed/scheduled” on a new set. **Links:** A1.U06 invitations; A2.U05.L02.

#### A2.U05.L02 — Make predictions and spontaneous offers

**Goal:** respond to four situations using a context-appropriate prediction, offer, or intention. **Prerequisite:** `going to` and present future arrangements.

Use `will` often for an offer (`I'll carry that`) or a prediction/opinion (`I think it will rain`). Use `going to` for a visible sign or prior intention (`Look at those clouds; it's going to rain`; `I'm going to call Sam—I decided earlier`). These are common preferences, not absolute laws.

**Guided scenario cards:** (1) A phone rings while you are free: `I'll answer it.` (spontaneous offer/decision). (2) Dark clouds are overhead: `It's going to rain.` (prediction from present evidence). (3) You are asked about tomorrow's weather and give your opinion: `I think it will be sunny.` (opinion prediction). (4) You bought paint yesterday for a plan you already made: `I'm going to paint my room.` (prior intention). **Key:** these forms fit the supplied contexts; equivalent natural wording is acceptable.

**Independent:** create two realistic scenarios and respond spontaneously; partner identifies the function. **Check:** 3/4 forms fit the supplied context and function is explained. **Remedy:** pair each form with a context clue, then re-test with different scenarios. **Links:** A2.U05.L01/L03.

#### A2.U05.L03 — Negotiate a plan and send a confirmation

**Goal:** negotiate a plan, resolve one conflict, and write a 70–90-word confirmation with complete details. **Prerequisite:** dates/times, invitations, future forms.

Ask availability, suggest alternatives, and confirm the final plan: `Are you free on Saturday?` `I can't then. How about Sunday afternoon?` `That works. Let's meet at ...` A message should state who, activity, date/time, place, and any required item.

**Schedule cards:** A is free Sunday 1–4 p.m. and prefers a film. B is free Sunday 2–5 p.m. and prefers the park. Both can meet at the central station or the cinema. Saturday is unavailable to both. Agree on a time between 2 and 4 and choose a shared place/activity; explain any compromise. Multiple agreements are valid if they fit both schedules.

**Independent interaction:** negotiate from separate schedule cards without showing them, then tell a third partner the plan. **Writing:** send confirmation; partner checks all five details.

**Check:** no constraint violated, final details repeated accurately, message includes all five elements. **Remedy:** draw overlapping availability, then confirm one detail at a time. **Links:** A2.U05.L01–L02; mediation rubric.

### Lessons for unit A2.U06 — Preferences and comparisons

#### A2.U06.L01 — Compare choices using evidence

**Goal:** compare two options in four accurate sentences and justify a preference with two details. **Prerequisite:** adjectives, numbers, `than`.

Short adjectives often take `-er` (`cheaper`), longer adjectives often use `more` (`more comfortable`); forms vary and common irregulars include `good/better/best`, `bad/worse/worst`. Use `than` for a two-item comparison (`Lake Café is cheaper than Park Café`). For three or more, use a superlative with `the` (`Lake Café is the quietest of the three`; `It is the most comfortable option`). Compare like with like and use only facts supplied.

**Options:** Lake Café: £8 lunch, 15 minutes away, quiet. Park Café: £6 lunch, 25 minutes away, noisy at noon.

**Guided/key:** cheaper? Park Café. nearer? Lake Café. Which is quieter? Lake Café. **Superlative practice:** given three prices (£6, £8, £10), which is the cheapest? **Key:** the £6 option. **Independent:** recommend one option for a profile (limited budget / wants quiet / short lunch break) and cite two matching facts.

**Check:** 4/5 comparison forms/facts accurate and reasons support stated preference. **Remedy:** highlight the compared feature in each option before constructing the comparative. **Links:** A2.U06.L02.

#### A2.U06.L02 — Compare experiences and express preferences

**Goal:** give an opinion with two supporting comparisons and respond to a different preference. **Prerequisite:** comparative forms and `because`.

`I prefer the train because it is faster than the bus.` `For me, the bus is more convenient because it stops near my home.` A preference can differ because people value different features; do not present one preference as universal fact.

**Reading:** `Maya prefers the train: it is faster and she can read. Rafi prefers the bus because it is cheaper and stops near his home. Neither travels every day.` Ask who values price, who values speed, and whether either goes daily. **Key:** Rafi; Maya; no.

#### A2.U06.L03 — Write a short recommendation

**Goal:** write a 90–110-word recommendation comparing two familiar choices and acknowledging one trade-off. **Prerequisite:** comparatives, opinion language, connectors.

Structure: introduce the choice, compare two features, state the intended reader's priority, mention a trade-off, conclude. Use `although` to concede a contrast and `because` for a reason; avoid unsupported superlatives (`the best`) unless criteria are clear.

**Guided:** revise `Park Café is best. It is cheaper.` **Sample:** `Park Café is cheaper than Lake Café, so it may suit someone on a tight budget. However, it is farther away and can be noisy at noon.` More than one recommendation is valid if supported by the options.

## 6. Reading sample

`Last weekend, I visited my aunt in the countryside. We walked in the park, cooked dinner together, and watched a film at night. It was relaxing, but the weather was cold. Next month, I am going to visit my cousins in another city.`

Questions:
- Where did the learner go?
- What did they do?
- What was the weather like?
- What is planned for next month?

## 7. Speaking tasks

- tell a story about an event from the past
- describe future plans in a short interview
- compare two cities or two restaurants
- role-play asking for help in a station or hospital

## 8. Writing tasks

Write 80–120 words on a topic such as:
- a recent trip
- a weekend activity
- a visit to a doctor
- a favorite place

Requirements:
- at least three past-tense verbs
- at least three time expressions
- basic linking words (`because`, `but`, `so`)

## 9. Assessment

- short listening exercise with detail questions
- pair interview about past events or future plans
- paragraph writing with sequence and comparison
- end-of-level test covering grammar and vocabulary

Use the ungraded entry diagnostic, six keyed mixed-skill unit checks, and 40-point stage-end test in `resources/a1-a2-assessment.md`. Record attempted, completed, and mastered separately. The local thresholds are unstandardized and are not CEFR certification.

## 10. Mastery criteria

The learner can use this course as preparation for B1 when they can:

- describe events in the past and near future with reasonable accuracy
- use a basic present-perfect pattern for selected continuing situations and contrast it with a finished past time
- ask for help and information in everyday life
- compare and give opinions with simple support
- organize a short personal story or routine description clearly

## 11. Common mistakes

- mixing `went` and `was`
- using `yesterday` with future forms
- confusion between `do` and `did`
- failing to add `-ed` or use irregular past forms correctly

Support:
- use verb tables
- pair grammar with real speaking tasks
- revisit A1 grammar cases in context
