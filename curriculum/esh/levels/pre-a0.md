# PRE-A0 — Literacy and first classroom English

## Purpose and boundaries

PRE-A0 is a project-specific entry stage for learners with no assumed English literacy. It develops recognition of print, early sound awareness, a small controlled set of decodable words, classroom listening, and first social phrases. It does **not** assume that a learner can read Latin letters or name English sounds.

The sequence is: see and write letter forms → hear and say letter names → hear speech sounds in words → connect a small set of spellings to sounds → blend/read and segment/spell → use words and phrases in context → demonstrate readiness. A familiar letter name is not the same as the sound represented in a word. English spelling is not a one-letter/one-sound code; instruction teaches useful patterns and their limits.

### Materials and accessibility

Use large printed letter cards, lined paper, picture cards or teacher-drawn pictures, a board, and a teacher voice. Listening activities retain their teacher-read scripts; selected items now have supplemental Piper Amy clips linked at the activity. These synthetic clips are experimental and not human-validated; they do not replace varied listening materials. Face the learner for instructions, but when testing listening, say the script without gestures or visible text. Offer larger print, tactile tracing, additional wait time, and oral response where a motor or vision need is not the target being assessed.

### Prerequisite notation

- **None**: true starting point.
- **Earlier PRE-A0 lesson**: review its target briefly before introducing new material.
- A lesson is not mastered by attendance. Use its stated check and retry with new examples after targeted practice.

## Outcomes and transition standard

By the end of PRE-A0, the learner can:

1. match all 26 upper/lower-case letter forms and write recognizable forms;
2. say letter names and distinguish them from initial/final sounds in taught words;
3. identify and produce beginning, ending, and (in simple words) middle sounds with picture or oral support;
4. blend and segment a taught set of short-vowel CVC words;
5. recognize that common letter groups and silent `e` can change spelling-to-sound patterns;
6. copy and spell taught short words and write a few meaningful labels;
7. understand and respond to familiar classroom instructions and short greetings;
8. exchange a name and a basic greeting, using a rehearsed phrase.

**Transition to A0 / Pre-A1:** on two checks on separate occasions, the learner recognizes at least 24/26 mixed-case letters, writes at least 20 recognizable lower-case forms from a mixed model or dictation, correctly blends at least 8/10 taught decodable words including two unfamiliar combinations from taught patterns, identifies first sounds in at least 4/5 spoken words, follows at least 4/5 familiar commands, reads at least 4/5 familiar printed instructions, and completes a greeting/name exchange. The stage-end check samples these abilities; use the relevant unit checks and a parallel follow-up for any target not sampled fully. If a single subskill is not met, teach it again; do not make weak speech production a barrier when comprehension is the skill being tested.

## Unit PRE-A0.U01 — Letter forms and names

**Unit outcome:** match and form upper/lower-case letters and say their conventional names.  
**Review:** none. **Progression:** forms before sound-to-spelling work.
**Unit check:** T-PRE-A0-U01 in [the foundation assessments](../resources/foundation-assessment.md).

### PRE-A0.U01.L01 — Recognize the alphabet

**Goal:** correctly identify 20/26 uppercase letters in a mixed set and match at least 18 to their lowercase forms. **Prerequisite:** none.

**Teach:** English print goes from left to right and from top to bottom on the page. A letter has a conventional name: `M`/`m` is called “em”; it is not pronounced /m/ when we say the alphabet. Demonstrate one letter at a time, then mix the cards. Some lowercase forms look quite different from capitals (`A/a`, `G/g`, `R/r`).

**Model:** `A a`, `B b`, `C c`, `D d`. Say the names; point to the matching forms. Avoid introducing all letter sounds at once.

**Guided:** learner sorts a small group of 5–7 cards into matching pairs. Increase the set only after accurate matching. **Independent:** point to 10 requested letters in random order; match 10 uppercase cards to lowercase choices.

**Check/key:** record each correct response; target is 20/26 identification and 18/26 matching. The answer is the identical letter in the other case, not a letter that merely looks similar.

**Common difficulty/remedy:** `b/d`, `p/q`, and `m/n` may be confused. Place each pair side by side, trace the starting stroke, say the letter name, and retest with a shuffled set. Do not mark a reversed form as a speech error.

**Links:** PRE-A0.U01.L02; handwriting in PRE-A0.U04.L01.

### PRE-A0.U01.L02 — Match uppercase and lowercase

**Goal:** match 24/26 case pairs without relying on alphabet order. **Prerequisite:** L01.

**Teach:** uppercase and lowercase are two written forms of the same alphabet letter. Example: the first letter in `Book` may be uppercase at the start of a sentence; the same letter inside `book` is lowercase. Introduce capitals in names and at the beginning of sentences as a print convention, not a different sound.

**Guided:** use pairs `A/a, B/b, D/d, E/e, G/g, H/h, M/m, N/n, P/p, R/r`. Say one name and have the learner find either form. **Independent:** sort a shuffled 12-card set into pairs; then mark the first letter in `Ali`, `book`, `Mona`, `map`.

**Key:** `Ali` A; `book` b; `Mona` M; `map` m. Capitalization of the supplied word is intentional.

**Check:** 24/26 pairs correct in two shuffled rounds. If not, practice only the missed pairs and retest with cards in a different order.

**Common difficulty/remedy:** some fonts make `a`, `g`, or `r` look unfamiliar. Use one clear print font and one handwritten sample; explain that letter shape varies while the letter identity remains the same.

**Links:** PRE-A0.U01.L01; PRE-A0.U04.L01.

### PRE-A0.U01.L03 — Say letter names; do not confuse names with sounds

**Goal:** name 20/26 shown letters and select the spoken letter name when it is said aloud. **Prerequisite:** L01–L02.

**Teach:** the name of `B` is “bee”; the beginning sound in `bag` is /b/. The name of `S` is “ess”; the beginning sound in `sun` is /s/. A sound is heard in a word; a letter name is used to identify a written symbol. Model with no written word first, then show the word.

**Guided:** teacher says either “letter name B” or “the first sound in bag”; learner points to the letter or picture as instructed. **Independent:** learner names shown cards and says the first sound in `map`, `sun`, `fish`.

**Key:** letter names B, S; initial sounds /m/, /s/, /f/. Accept the target consonant sound without an added vowel (e.g. /b/, not “buh” when identifying the isolated sound), while recognizing that beginner speech may naturally release a consonant.

**Check:** 8/10 mixed name-versus-sound prompts correct. Repeat using different taught letters if missed.

**Common difficulty/remedy:** saying “bee” for the sound in `bag`. Contrast `B` (“bee”) with the short start of `bag` (/b/), then ask the learner to sort prompts into “name” and “sound.”

**Links:** PRE-A0.U01.L02; the next sequence is PRE-A0.U02.L01.

### PRE-A0.U01.L04 — Write recognizable letter forms

**Goal:** form at least 20 lowercase and 20 uppercase letters so a reader can identify them. **Prerequisite:** L01–L02.

**Teach:** start at the marked dot, follow the arrow, and keep letters on the line. Demonstrate a small set of straight-line forms (`l`, `t`, `i`) and curves (`c`, `o`, `a`) before writing the full alphabet. Letter-writing direction is a practical handwriting convention; readable shape matters more than decorative style.

**Guided:** trace `l, i, t, c, o, a`; copy each three times. **Independent:** write the learner's name, then copy `map`, `sun`, `book`.

**Check:** teacher identifies at least 20/26 uppercase and lowercase attempts without being told which letter was intended. **Key:** judge legibility against a printed alphabet chart; do not score penmanship style.

**Common difficulty/remedy:** reversed or floating letters. Use a start dot, baseline, and a large-to-small tracing sequence; retest a few forms later in a word rather than drilling the whole alphabet.

**Links:** PRE-A0.U04.L01 (copying/spelling); PRE-A0.U05.L01 review.

## Unit PRE-A0.U02 — Listening to sounds and beginning phonics

**Unit outcome:** hear and produce selected speech sounds and use initial, final, and short-vowel cues in simple words.  
**Review:** letter names in U01. **Progression:** oral sound work before decoding.
**Unit check:** T-PRE-A0-U02 in [the foundation assessments](../resources/foundation-assessment.md).

### PRE-A0.U02.L01 — Vowels, consonants, and speech sounds

**Goal:** identify the five vowel letters and classify the other 21 letters as consonant letters; identify the first sound in 4/5 taught words. **Prerequisite:** U01.

**Teach:** in the English alphabet, the vowel letters are `a, e, i, o, u`; the remaining letters are consonant letters. This label is about spelling categories. Speech also has vowel sounds and consonant sounds. Letter `y` often represents a consonant sound as in `yes`, and it can function as a vowel letter in words such as `my`; introduce this as a later exception, not an additional basic vowel letter.

**Guided:** sort letter cards; say `sun`, `book`, `map`; learner points to the picture and names the first sound. **Independent:** circle vowel letters in `cat`, `pen`, `dog`, `sun`; say the beginning sound in `fish`.

**Key:** vowels: a in `cat`, e in `pen`, o in `dog`, u in `sun`; first sound in `fish` is /f/. The letters `i` and `o` in `fish`/`dog` are not examples of every sound those vowel letters can represent.

**Check:** 5/5 vowel-letter identification and 4/5 initial sounds. If letter categories are secure but sound responses are weak, keep the categories and reteach listening separately.

**Common difficulty/remedy:** treating “vowel” as one sound. Explain that the letters are categories; the vowel sound varies across `cat`, `cake`, and `about`.

**Links:** PRE-A0.U01.L03; PRE-A0.U02.L02.

### PRE-A0.U02.L02 — Hear and find first, middle, and last sounds

**Goal:** identify the first and last sounds in 4/5 familiar words and the short vowel sound in 3/4 spoken CVC words. **Prerequisite:** L01 and oral familiarity with the words.

**Teach:** say each word slowly without stretching it into extra syllables. In `map`, the first sound is /m/, the middle vowel sound is the short sound in `map`, and the last sound is /p/. The word has three phonemes even though the middle vowel name is not spoken.

**Guided:** teacher says `map`; learner places one counter for each sound while saying /m/ /a/ /p/. Repeat with `sun`. **Independent:** listen to `cat`, `pen`, `dog`, `fish`; tell the first sound and, for the CVC words, point to the middle vowel letter on a card.

**Key:** `cat`: /k/ ... /t/, middle short-a; `pen`: /p/ ... /n/, middle short-e; `dog`: /d/ ... /g/, middle short-o; `fish`: first /f/, final /sh/ (one consonant phoneme written with two letters).

**Check:** at least 4/5 initial/final responses and 3/4 vowel-pattern matches. If spelling is not yet known, assess oral identification with picture cards.

**Common difficulty/remedy:** counting letters instead of sounds in `fish`. Demonstrate that `sh` uses two letters for one sound; use counters for heard sounds, not printed letters.

**Links:** PRE-A0.U02.L03–L04; PRE-A0.U03.L01.

### PRE-A0.U02.L03 — Contrast sounds without guessing from letter names

**Goal:** distinguish at least 4/5 teacher-spoken contrasts from the taught set, using picture or word cards. **Prerequisite:** L02.

**Teach:** some English sounds may be unfamiliar or difficult to distinguish for an individual learner. Never assume a difficulty from the learner's first language; check their performance. Demonstrate the mouth cue only when it helps: for /f/, air passes between lower lip and upper teeth; for /v/, the same position includes voicing. For /p/ and /b/, the lips close; voicing differs.

**Listening set (teacher says one word at a time):** `fan/van`, `pat/bat`, `sip/ship`. Confirm that pictures or meanings are known first; otherwise test sound discrimination rather than vocabulary.

### PRE-A0.U02.L04 — Short-vowel CVC patterns

**Goal:** blend and read 8/10 taught CVC words with short `a, e, i, o, u`. **Prerequisite:** identify first/middle/last sounds.

**Teach:** CVC means consonant letter, vowel letter, consonant letter (a spelling description). In these selected examples, the vowel is usually short: `map`, `bed`, `sit`, `hot`, `sun`. Do not say that all three-letter words have this pattern: compare `she`, `see`, or `cake` later.

**Guided:** model `m-a-p`: say the sounds, slide a finger under the letters, blend to `map`. Repeat `p-e-n`, `s-i-t`, `d-o-g`, `c-u-p`. **Independent:** read shuffled word cards, then choose the matching picture.

**Key list:** `map`, `cat`, `bed`, `pen`, `sit`, `fish` (not CVC; use only for sound review), `hot`, `dog`, `sun`, `cup`. For a strict 8/10 CVC check replace `fish` with `top`; accepted CVC assessment set: `map, cat, bed, pen, sit, top, hot, dog, sun, cup`.

**Check:** 8/10 correctly blended, including one example from each taught vowel set over the lesson sequence. If missed, sort by vowel and blend three words from the weak group before reassessment.

**Common difficulty/remedy:** naming letters instead of blending sounds. Return to oral phoneme counters, then blend while pointing. Avoid adding schwa after each consonant.

**Links:** PRE-A0.U02.L02; spelling in PRE-A0.U04.L02.

## Unit PRE-A0.U03 — Decode and build short words

**Unit outcome:** segment, blend, read, and spell a small set of regular words and common letter groups.  
**Review:** U02 sounds and CVC words. **Progression:** add word families and selected digraphs only after CVC readiness.
**Unit check:** T-PRE-A0-U03 in [the foundation assessments](../resources/foundation-assessment.md).

### PRE-A0.U03.L01 — Blend, segment, and change one sound

**Goal:** read 8/10 taught CVC words and spell 4/5 from oral dictation using sound-to-letter mapping. **Prerequisite:** U02.L04.

**Teach:** say the whole word; stretch the sounds; map one sound to each sound position; blend again. To change `map` to `mat`, keep /m/ and /a/ and change the final sound /p/ to /t/. This is sound segmentation, not memorizing a picture of the whole word.

**Guided:** use counters for `map`, then replace final sound to make `mat`. **Independent:** read `cat, cap, can, bed, bet`; write `sun, sit, map, pen, dog` after the teacher says each once, then repeats the word in a sentence.

**Key:** dictated spellings: `sun`, `sit`, `map`, `pen`, `dog`. Accept standard letter case at the start of a sentence; test spelling separately from handwriting.

**Check:** 8/10 reading and 4/5 spelling. Remediate only the missed position (initial, middle, final) with new examples such as `pin`/`pan` or `map`/`mat`.

**Links:** PRE-A0.U02.L04; PRE-A0.U04.L02.

### PRE-A0.U03.L02 — Word families, rhyme, and syllables

**Goal:** identify a rhyming word for 4/5 spoken prompts and clap the syllables in 4/5 familiar words. **Prerequisite:** oral vocabulary for the items; print knowledge is not required for the first activity.

**Teach:** rhyming words share a final sound sequence, not necessarily spelling (`sun/fun`; `day/play`). A syllable is a spoken beat containing a vowel sound. Clap `cat` once, `sunset` twice, `banana` three times. Syllables are sounds/beats, not letters.

**Guided:** choose the rhyme from `cat—hat / dog`; `sun—fun / pen`; `map—mat / sun`. **Independent:** clap syllables in `book, apple, banana, window, family`.

**Key:** `hat`, `fun`, `mat`; syllables: `book` 1, `apple` 2, `banana` 3, `window` 2, `family` commonly 3 in careful classroom pronunciation (some natural speech compresses it; accept the learner's clear spoken grouping).

**Check:** 4/5 rhymes and 4/5 syllable counts. For a missed item, speak it naturally, tap beats, and repeat without written prompts.

**Links:** PRE-A0.U02.L02; PRE-A0.U03.L03.

### PRE-A0.U03.L03 — Common consonant teams: `sh`, `ch`, `th`, `ck`

**Goal:** recognize `sh`, `ch`, and `ck` in taught words and decode 6/8 taught words. **Prerequisite:** CVC decoding.

**Teach:** a digraph is two letters representing one consonant sound in a particular word. Examples: `sh` in `shop`, `ch` in `chip`, `ck` in `sock`. `th` has more than one common pronunciation in English (`thin` versus `this`); teach it with two clear models and do not claim one fixed sound. These spelling patterns are useful clues, not universal rules.

**Guided:** sort `ship, shop, chip, chat, sock, back, thin, this` by visible letter team; listen to the teacher model the words. **Independent:** read six cards; point to the team that signals the start or end sound.

### PRE-A0.U03.L04 — Silent `e` as a useful pattern

**Goal:** distinguish four taught short-vowel/`e` word pairs by listening and match each written word to its spoken form. **Prerequisite:** short vowels and CVC decoding.

**Teach:** a final silent `e` often changes the vowel pattern in words such as `cap/cape`, `kit/kite`, `hop/hope`. The `e` itself is not usually pronounced in these examples. This is a common pattern, not a rule that explains every English word; compare `have` later.

**Guided:** teacher models `cap` then `cape`, pointing to the changed spelling. **Independent:** select the word heard from `kit/kite`, `hop/hope`, `mad/made`, `cub/cube`.

## Unit PRE-A0.U04 — Meaningful print, copying, and spelling

**Unit outcome:** read familiar labels and short instructions; copy and spell a small functional vocabulary set.  
**Review:** words and sound patterns from U02–U03.
**Unit check:** T-PRE-A0-U04 in [the foundation assessments](../resources/foundation-assessment.md).

### PRE-A0.U04.L01 — Copy letters, labels, and word spaces

**Goal:** copy 8/10 familiar labels legibly and identify the word boundary in 4/5 short phrases. **Prerequisite:** recognizable letter forms.

**Teach:** print separates words with spaces. Copy one word at a time from left to right; compare the first, middle, and last letters with the model. A label names an object: `book`, `pen`, `door`, `map`. Do not require cursive.

**Guided:** copy `book`, `pen`, `door`. **Independent:** copy the labels onto a picture or real-object card; mark the spaces in `a red bag` and `my book`.

**Key:** the phrase has 3 words (`a / red / bag`) and 2 words (`my / book`). Judge spelling/legibility, not artistic quality.

**Check:** 8/10 labels recognized by a second reader; 4/5 boundaries marked. If handwriting obscures spelling, allow letter cards or oral spelling to separate knowledge from motor control.

**Links:** PRE-A0.U01.L04; PRE-A0.U04.L02.

### PRE-A0.U04.L02 — Spell and retrieve familiar words

**Goal:** spell 8/10 familiar words from picture, oral cue, or real object. **Prerequisite:** taught word set.

**Teach:** look, say, cover, write, and check. Use sound positions for decodable words (`map`: /m/ /a/ /p/); remember that some familiar words are not fully predictable from sound, so copy and revisit them. Do not label every error “careless”; identify which part of the word is uncertain.

**Guided:** teacher displays `map`, learner says it, covers it, writes it, checks each letter. **Independent dictation:** `cat, pen, sun, book, desk, bag, map, door, cup, fish` (say each in a short context and repeat once).

**Key:** exactly those spellings. `fish` and `book` are not CVC spellings; they are taught words and should not be used to judge CVC decoding.

**Check:** 8/10. For errors, sort into first sound, vowel pattern, final sound, or remembered spelling; practice that type and retest with a parallel word.

**Links:** PRE-A0.U03.L01, L03; PRE-A0.U05.L04.

### PRE-A0.U04.L03 — Read familiar signs and instructions

**Goal:** read and follow 4/5 familiar printed one-step instructions, follow 4/5 spoken instructions, and match 4/5 classroom labels to objects. **Prerequisite:** orally taught words.

**Teach:** language is used to do something. Pair the printed phrase with a demonstration first; later remove the gesture. `Open your book.` begins with a verb telling the learner what to do. `Look.` and `Listen.` are one-word instructions. The printed label `door` names an object; it is not a command.

**Links:** PRE-A0.U03 greetings; PRE-A0.U05.L01.

## Unit PRE-A0.U05 — First interaction and readiness

**Unit outcome:** use a few greetings, respond to a simple question, and demonstrate the literacy/listening foundation needed in A0.  
**Review:** all previous units.
**Unit check:** T-PRE-A0-U05 and T-PRE-A0-END-01 in [the foundation assessments](../resources/foundation-assessment.md).

### PRE-A0.U05.L01 — Greetings and names

**Goal:** complete a two-turn greeting and state a name in an understandable phrase. **Prerequisite:** oral imitation is sufficient; reading not required.

**Teach:** model `Hello.` / `Hi.` / `Good morning.` / `Goodbye.` and `My name is …` or `I’m …`. Explain that contractions are common in speech: `I’m` = `I am`. For “How are you?”, a short answer may be `I’m fine, thank you.` or another truthful answer. Do not insist on one scripted response.

### PRE-A0.U05.L02 — Sounds, words, and classroom response

**Workshop:** complete the original multi-skill workshop in [`resources/integrated-skill-labs.md`](../resources/integrated-skill-labs.md). It provides supported practice identifying taught letters, decoding a short CVC set, and responding to spoken classroom instructions.

### PRE-A0.U05.L03 — Review and learner self-check

**Goal:** retrieve one letter, one sound, one word, one instruction, and one greeting without a model. **Prerequisite:** lessons above.

**Review prompts:** (1) point to lowercase `g` when shown G; (2) say the name of `M`; (3) say the first sound in `sun`; (4) blend `p-e-n`; (5) respond to `Close your book`; (6) greet a partner.

**Key:** g; “em”; /s/; `pen`; close the book; suitable greeting. Use voice or pointing as appropriate to the target.

**Spaced practice:** review at the next lesson, after 2–3 days, and after one week. Mix new order and old/new words; do not rehearse a fixed sequence only.

### PRE-A0.U05.L04 — Integrated sound, word, and classroom check

**Goal:** use taught letters, decode taught words, understand instructions, and exchange names. **Prerequisite:** U01–U05.L01.

**Sequence:** after the workshop practice in `PRE-A0.U05.L02`, use the separate unit and end-of-stage checks in `resources/foundation-assessment.md`. The checks use changed item order and separate reading, listening, spelling, and interaction evidence.

**Transfer task:** after workshop practice, show five taught word cards in a new order (including one `sh` word), then give five familiar classroom commands without gestures. **Key/criteria:** word response must match the printed card; command response must match the spoken instruction. Record reading and listening separately; do not score pronunciation as a substitute for either.

**Writing/interaction transfer:** spell three familiar words from dictation and exchange greetings/names with a new partner. **Key/criteria:** compare spellings with the taught word list; accept natural greetings and any clear name exchange. The stage gate's written-form and interaction criteria are scored separately.

**Decision/remediation:** use the component thresholds in `resources/foundation-assessment.md`. For each missed criterion, reteach its linked earlier lesson and retest a parallel item on another day; do not repeat the identical answer sheet as the sole evidence.

## Spelling-to-sound notes for teachers

- Letter names do not equal phonemes: `B` is named “bee”; `b` is the initial consonant sound in `bag`.
- A vowel letter may have different pronunciations: compare `cat`, `cake`, `about`.
- Two letters can represent one sound (`sh` in `ship`); `th` commonly represents more than one sound (`thin`, `this`).
- A written letter may be silent in a pattern (`e` in `cape`), but not every word follows the same pattern (`have`).
- `y` has more than one spelling function; defer detail until the learner has a stable core.
- Accent variation is normal. Assess intelligibility and the target contrast, not imitation of a single accent.
- Teacher-read scripts support practice but do not replace varied, genuine listening materials.
