# B1–B2 diagnostic, unit checks, and stage-end assessment

These are project-authored classroom assessments, not standardized exams or CEFR tests. Thresholds below are local decision rules and have not been piloted or normed. Use with the lessons in `levels/b1.md`, `levels/b2.md`, and the input in `b1-b2-learning-pack.md`. Listening scripts remain teacher-readable source text; selected indexed prompts have supplemental experimental Amy recordings. No automated pronunciation rating is supplied, and generated files are not validated assessment evidence.

## Administration and evidence rules

- Keep reading passages visible only during the reading section. For listening, conceal the script, read it twice at a natural but measured pace, and allow note-taking only on the second reading.
- Unit checks use new questions against the named unit input; stage-end sections use the fresh text/scripts printed below. Do not award a reading/listening score for recalled text seen before the check.
- Score each skill separately. Record exposure, attempted practice, performance on the check, and a mastery decision as separate states.
- Closed responses use the keys below. For open production, score only the listed features; accept any accurate, intelligible answer that meets them. The sample is not a single required answer.
- Give a repair task for any weak component and recheck with a parallel prompt on another day. A total score must not conceal a failed essential skill.

Use before B1 or B2 instruction to identify support needs; do not use as a placement or pass/fail decision.

### B2-DIAG-01

1. **Argument language:** replace “This proves the new rule works” when the only evidence is a six-week voluntary pilot.  
   Sample key: “The pilot provides preliminary evidence that the rule may help.” Accept equivalent cautious, faithful wording.
2. **Reading:** give the B2 Unit 1 passage in the pack. Ask for one observation and one limitation.  
   Key: attendance rose slightly/student reports indicated more study time; short volunteer pilot in one department with no comparison group.
3. **Listening:** read the B2 Unit 5 script in the pack. Ask what creates self-selection and what next design is proposed.  
   Key: participants chose whether to use reminders; assign groups to schedules and use a common concentration task.
4. **Interaction:** ask learner to state an evidence-based recommendation and fairly summarize a counterargument. Record evidence fidelity and response separately.
5. **Writing:** ask for a 150-word recommendation on a fictional policy, including one limitation and one condition.
6. **Mediation:** ask learner to brief a non-specialist on two positions while retaining uncertainty and not inventing facts.

## B1 unit checks

Each check is worth 10 points: reading 2, listening 2, language 2, interaction 2, and writing 2. One point is awarded for each keyed reading/listening/language response. For interaction, award 1 point each for the required communicative moves; for writing, award 1 point each for the required content features. These short checks indicate what to reteach; they are not by themselves stage mastery.

### B1-T-U01 — Experiences and narratives (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B1 Unit 1 passage from the learning pack. (1) Where was the ticket found? (1) What did the writer learn?  
**Listening (2):** use B1 Unit 1 script. (1) What had the speaker prepared? (1) When did the offer arrive?  
**Language (2):** complete (1) “While I ___ (wait), the bus ___ (arrive).” (1) “By the time we got there, the shop ___ (close).”  
**Interaction (2):** retell a six-event experience: 1 point for clear sequence; 1 for outcome/reflection.  
**Writing (2):** write 80–100 words: 1 point for a clear sequence with accurate time relations; 1 for outcome and reflection.  
**Key:** under a bus seat; check important items twice; had prepared for the interview; a week later; was waiting / arrived; had closed. Other sections use criteria above.

### B1-T-U02 — Opinions and reasons (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B1 Unit 2 passage. (1) Why do some learners prefer homework time? (1) What does the report not establish?  
**Listening (2):** use B1 Unit 2 script. (1) Name one benefit. (1) What does the garden need?  
**Language (2):** choose `because / however` in each: (1) “I support the plan ___ it gives learners time to read.” (1) “The report is positive; ___, it has no comparison school.”  
**Interaction (2):** give a claim and reason (1), then accurately state a partner’s opposing view (1).  
**Writing (2):** state a claim with one reason (1) and a specific example or fair counterpoint (1).  
**Key:** use time for homework, especially before exams; whether the reading period alone caused the change; food-growing or meeting neighbours; volunteers and regular water; because; however.

### B1-T-U03 — Problem solving and advice (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B1 Unit 3 passage. (1) How long is the trial? (1) What will the office count?  
**Listening (2):** use B1 Unit 3 script. (1) Who could benefit? (1) What staffing constraint is stated?  
**Language (2):** complete (1) “If the route is extended, more students ___ (use) it.” (1) “If the route ___ (be) shorter, more people would use it.”  
**Interaction (2):** propose a feasible action with reason (1) and identify a cost/concern plus review measure (1).  
**Writing (2):** include problem/proposed response (1) and trade-off/review measure (1).  
**Key:** eight weeks; passenger counts; people who work during the day; one evening staff member; will use; were. Accept `was` in the hypothetical clause when the subject is singular; `were` is also standard.

### B1-T-U04 — Work and study contexts (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B1 Unit 4 passage. (1) Why was the deadline missed? (1) What remained unfinished?  
**Listening (2):** use B1 Unit 4 script. (1) Why has the schedule changed? (1) What must be confirmed before noon?  
**Language (2):** choose (1) “I usually ___ (prepare / am preparing) the room.” (1) “The participant list ___ (updated / was updated) yesterday.”  
**Interaction (2):** agree on an action owner (1) and a date/time (1), then confirm both.  
**Writing (2):** include cause and action/status (1) and a clear next deadline (1).  
**Key:** different versions of the brief; final images; the speaker is helping a new colleague; projector availability; prepare; was updated.

### B1-T-U05 — Society and public issues (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B1 Unit 5 passage. (1) Who expects more visitors? (1) What will happen before a permanent decision?  
**Listening (2):** use B1 Unit 5 script. (1) Why does the group want benches? (1) What concern was raised?  
**Language (2):** select the correct connector: (1) “___ the trial is short, it may identify practical concerns.” (`Although / Therefore`) (1) “The trial is short; ___, it can provide useful local feedback.” (`however / because`)  
**Interaction (2):** state a proposal and reason (1), then relay a concern without changing it (1).  
**Writing (2):** include two positions (1) and a practical next step/conclusion (1).  
**Key:** shop owners; collect visitor counts and invite residents to comment; older residents need a place to rest; possible evening noise; Although; however.

### B1-T-U06 — Media and technology (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B1 Unit 6 passage. (1) How many students answered the poll? (1) What remains unknown about the poll?  
**Listening (2):** use B1 Unit 6 script. (1) What helps the speaker meet deadlines? (1) Which claim does the speaker avoid?  
**Language (2):** complete (1) “The article ___ that most students prefer digital tasks.” (`reports / proves`) (1) “The poll had 24 respondents, ___ it may not represent the whole school.” (`so / although`)  
**Interaction (2):** distinguish one source claim from one's inference (1), ask a relevant follow-up question (1).  
**Writing/mediation (2):** state recommendation and reason (1), preserve a limitation when relaying it (1).  
**Key:** 24; how many were invited/response rate; reminders; that the calendar raised grades; reports; so.

## B2 unit checks

Each check is worth 10 points with the same five 2-point components and rules as the B1 unit checks. Give credit for reasoned, evidence-faithful wording; do not reward confidence or length by themselves.

### B2-T-U01 — Debate and argumentative structure (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B2 Unit 1 passage. (1) What is one reported observation? (1) Name one design limitation.  
**Listening (2):** use B2 Unit 1 script. (1) State one concern. (1) Name one proposed evidence step.  
**Language (2):** complete (1) “The pilot ___ (suggest / prove) a possible benefit.” (1) “___ the sample was small, the result may justify a larger trial.” (`Although / Therefore`)  
**Interaction (2):** state claim + evidence (1); fairly restate an objection before responding (1).  
**Writing (2):** include a qualified conclusion (1) and a limitation/counterargument (1).  
**Key:** attendance rose slightly or participants reported more independent-study time; short, single department, volunteers, no comparison group; campus services every weekday; compare courses or consult non-volunteers; suggests; Although.

### B2-T-U02 — Media and persuasion (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B2 Unit 2 passage. (1) What percentage supported reducing traffic on one street? (1) Give one missing detail.  
**Listening (2):** use B2 Unit 2 script. (1) What proposal is promoted? (1) Name one missing evidence area.  
**Language (2):** complete (1) “The campaign ___ the change as a safety measure.” (`frames / proves`) (1) “The poster cites a survey; ___, it gives no response rate.” (`however / because`)  
**Interaction (2):** identify a persuasive feature (1) and ask a relevant evidence/context question (1).  
**Writing (2):** state claim and audience (1), qualify judgment using evidence/omission (1).  
**Key:** 58%; number invited, response rate, or shop-owner views; a new road; travel-time data or cost; frames; however.

### B2-T-U03 — Education and society (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B2 Unit 3 passage. (1) How many eligible applicants could initially receive support? (1) What process concern is raised?  
**Listening (2):** use B2 Unit 3 script. (1) What information should families receive? (1) When is the decision to be reviewed?  
**Language (2):** complete (1) “If funding is limited, the district ___ (could / must) begin with a trial.” (1) “Applications ___ (review) each month.”  
**Interaction (2):** state a benefit and who may be excluded (1), propose one safeguard (1).  
**Writing (2):** include an outcome/measure (1) and a safeguard or appeal process (1).  
**Key:** half; appeal process may be difficult; clear reasons for declined applications; after one term; could; are reviewed.

### B2-T-U04 — Work, leadership, and environment (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B2 Unit 4 passage. (1) What happened to overtime costs? (1) What comparison is missing?  
**Listening (2):** use B2 Unit 4 script. (1) What does Finance want? (1) Which effect has not been estimated?  
**Language (2):** complete (1) “Customer requests ___ (delay) during the change.” (2) “The report ___ (not/separate) the busiest season from other months.”  
**Interaction (2):** give a conditional recommendation (1), answer one challenge with a safeguard/measure (1).  
**Writing (2):** separate an observation from an inference (1) and state a measure/review point (1).  
**Key:** they fell; busiest season was not separated; overtime capped; staff retention; were delayed; did not separate.

### B2-T-U05 — Health, science, and technology (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B2 Unit 5 passage. (1) What association was reported? (1) Why is cause not established?  
**Listening (2):** use B2 Unit 5 script. (1) Why may groups differ before a trial? (1) What design change is proposed?  
**Language (2):** select the warranted phrase: (1) `caused / was associated with` (the pilot result); (1) `proves / may indicate` (a preliminary pattern).  
**Interaction (2):** state a cautious finding (1) and propose relevant next evidence (1).  
**Writing (2):** name a limitation (1) and a proportionate next step/safeguard (1).  
**Key:** more reminder use aligned with better reported concentration; no comparison group and app use was not independently verified; people chose whether to use reminders; assign groups to schedules and use the same task; was associated with; may indicate.

### B2-T-U06 — Culture, identity, and global issues (10 points)

**Reading (2):** use B2 Unit 6 passage. (1) What did participants learn? (1) Whose perspective is missing?  
**Listening (2):** use B2 Unit 6 script. (1) What barrier did the friend report? (1) What next step is suggested?  
**Language (2):** revise “All exchange students feel uncomfortable at first” into a qualified claim (1); choose a precise attribution (1): `everyone says / several participants reported`.  
**Interaction (2):** make a specific, non-generalized claim (1); fairly summarize a different view (1).  
**Writing/mediation (2):** preserve one position (1) and one uncertainty/limit (1).  
**Key:** new group-work approaches; students who declined; transport cost; ask students what would make participation possible; sample: “Some participants reported feeling uncomfortable at first”; several participants reported.

## B1-END-01 — B1 stage-end assessment (40 points)

Award 8 points per section. Record section scores separately.

### A. Language control (8 points)

1. “While we ___ (wait), the train ___ (arrive).” (2)
2. “By the time I called, she ___ (leave).” (2)
3. “If the council ___ (extend) the service, more people ___ (use) it.” (2)
4. Join with a defining relative clause: “I spoke to a student. She organized the meeting.” (2)

**Key:** were waiting / arrived; had left; extends / will use; “I spoke to a student who organized the meeting.” Give 1 point per correctly formed clause in items 1–3; item 4 earns 1 for a suitable relative pronoun and 1 for correct clause structure.

### B. Reading (8 points)

Read: “The town library opened two evenings a week for a six-week trial. Visits increased on those evenings, and students said the later hours helped them finish group projects. The report did not compare the trial period with the same months in the previous year. Staff also noted that the computer room was often full. The council will review visitor counts, staffing costs, and comments before deciding whether to continue.”

### D. Speaking and interaction (8 points)

In pairs, choose between extending library hours, improving the computer room, or adding a study group. Each learner must state a preference, give a reason, respond to a concern, ask one clarification question, and confirm the group’s next step. Score 0–2 each for task completion, intelligible organization, interaction/repair, and fidelity to the information card. The partner should be able to repeat the agreed action accurately.

### E. Writing and mediation (8 points)

Write 140–180 words recommending one option from section D. Include the problem, evidence or stated information, one benefit, one limitation, a recommendation, and a review measure. Then add a two-sentence neutral summary for a person who did not attend.

Score 0–2 each: required content; cohesion; language control; accurate neutral summary. Accept varied positions if they use the given information fairly and do not invent facts.

**Local decision rule:** provisional readiness for further B2 study requires at least 26/40 overall, at least 4/8 in every section, and satisfactory performance on a new speaking and writing transfer task after feedback. This local rule is unpiloted and must not be used as an official B1 result.

## B2-END-01 — B2 stage-end assessment (40 points)

Award 8 points per section. Record section scores separately.

### A. Language and evidence control (8 points)

1. “The findings ___ (suggest / prove) that the approach may help.” (2)
2. “Although the sample ___ (be) small, the results ___ (be) useful for planning a larger trial.” (2)
3. Rewrite cautiously: “The app caused users to become more productive.” Only a voluntary survey found that frequent users reported higher productivity. (2)
4. Rewrite to foreground the process: “The team reviewed 48 applications.” (2)

**Key:** suggest; was / may be (accept `are` if referring to current results); “Frequent users reported higher productivity, but the survey does not establish that the app caused it.” or equivalent; “Forty-eight applications were reviewed by the team.” Award 1 point for form and 1 for preserving the meaning/qualification in each item.

### B. Reading (8 points)

Read: “A regional college piloted recorded lecture summaries in three courses for one term. Students who used the summaries submitted more assignments on time than they had the previous term. The college did not randomly assign students, and the courses also introduced weekly planning sessions. In optional feedback, some students said the summaries helped them review difficult topics; others said they rarely used them. The report recommends a second trial with comparison courses and usage records before the college invests across all programmes.”

### D. Speaking and interaction (8 points)

Give a 2-minute recommendation on whether the college should expand, repeat, or pause the pilot. Use one finding, one limitation, one alternative explanation, and one next step. Respond to a challenge and summarize the other speaker’s concern before replying. Score 0–2 each for evidence fidelity, qualification, organization/register, and interaction/response.

### E. Writing and mediation (8 points)

Write 220–260 words for a college decision group. Recommend a next step, using the reading and listening evidence, distinguishing finding from inference, noting a limitation, and specifying a safeguard or evaluation measure. Add a 60–80-word plain-language briefing for students.

Score 0–2 each: accurate synthesis/attribution; qualification and evidence limits; organization and audience-appropriate register; faithful accessible briefing. No points for invented evidence; material misrepresentation caps the section at 3/8.

**Local decision rule:** provisional readiness for human review toward C1 requires at least 28/40, at least 5/8 in reading, listening, speaking, and writing, and successful performance on a new-topic argument task. This unpiloted course rule is not an official B2 result.

## Recording, remediation, and review record

For each learner, keep the prompt/version, date, conditions, section scores, a brief evidence note for speaking/writing, support provided, and result of the delayed parallel check. Do not record identifiable learner speech without appropriate consent and data-handling safeguards.

When a learner misses a component:

- narrative/time relations: sort event cards, retell, and use a fresh story;
- opinion/evidence: label claim, reason, example, and counterpoint before speaking;
- recommendations: fill problem, affected group, option, trade-off, measure;
- media/science evidence: separate source statement, inference, and unknown;
- interaction: rehearse clarification and accurate restatement before a new role card;
- writing: revise against the task-specific checklist, then complete a parallel prompt.

Teacher review is still required for appropriateness, naturalness, response fairness, access needs, and score interpretation. No assessment in this document has been piloted, normed, or independently validated.
