# Integrated skill labs

These are teachable, original supplementary lessons. IDs in this file are stable and are the lesson IDs used by the coverage matrix. The existing level files remain the broader course map; this file supplies one assessed, integrated workshop per level. Scripts remain written for a teacher; selected scripts have supplemental synthetic Amy links at their activities. Those clips are experimental, and no automated pronunciation scoring is included.

All people, organizations, and figures in the B1, B2, C1, and C2 case materials are fictional teaching scenarios, not real research or operational evidence.

## PRE-A0.U05.L02 — Sounds, words, and classroom response

**Linked target:** identify taught letters, decode a short CVC set, and respond to spoken classroom instructions. **Prerequisite:** none. **Resources:** alphabet cards, picture cards, teacher voice.

**Teach:** name a letter and make its common sound separately: the letter name `m` is /em/; the initial sound in `map` is /m/. Explain that spelling patterns are clues, not a promise that each letter always has one sound. Model slow blending: `/m/ /a/ /p/` → `map`.

**Guided practice:** match `cat`, `map`, `sun`, `pen` to pictures; mark the first and last letter; read left to right. Then follow the spoken commands below.

**Reading check:** read the word cards `cat`, `map`, and `sun`. Which word names an animal? Which begins with the sound /s/? **Key:** `cat`; `sun`.

**Listening script (read twice, naturally and slowly):** “Look at the map. Point to the cat. Open your book. Write the letter M. Sit down.”

## A0.U01.L04 — A simple personal introduction

**Linked target:** exchange personal details, understand a short profile, and write four accurate sentences. **Prerequisite:** basic letter recognition. **Lesson ID:** A0.U01.L04.

**Teach:** use `be` for identity, age, and location: `I am Lina.`, `I am 19.`, `I am from Rabat.` In a simple present statement with an ordinary verb, use `do/does` to make questions and negatives: `Do you live here?` / `I do not live here.` Contrast this with `Are you from Rabat?` / `I am not from Rabat.` Do not put `do` before `be`.

## A1.U02.L03 — A routine and a simple invitation

**Linked target:** understand a short routine, ask and answer about schedules, and write a short message. **Prerequisite:** present simple and basic question forms. **Lesson ID:** A1.U02.L03.

**Teach:** use present simple for habits (`I walk to work every day`) and present continuous for an action happening now or around now (`I am waiting for the bus`). Third-person singular normally takes `-s` (`Mina walks`); after `does`, the main verb returns to base form (`Does Mina walk?`).

## A2.U01.L03 — A past visit and a future arrangement

**Linked target:** distinguish a completed past event from a future intention, ask for practical information, and write a short account. **Prerequisite:** past simple and basic future forms. **Lesson ID:** A2.U01.L03.

**Teach:** use past simple for a finished time (`We visited on Saturday`). Use `be going to` for a plan already intended (`I am going to visit next month`). `Will` often expresses a spontaneous decision or prediction; the choice depends on context, not merely on a future time word.

**Reading:** “On Saturday, Huda visited the old market with her cousin. They bought fruit and had lunch at a small café. Huda liked the market because it was lively, although it was crowded. She is going to return next month.”

## B1.W03.L02 — Explain a problem and recommend a solution

**Linked target:** identify a claim and supporting reason, discuss alternatives, and write a connected recommendation. **Prerequisite:** past/present forms and basic linking words. **Lesson ID:** B1.W03.L02.

**Teach:** a recommendation is stronger when it names a problem, gives a reason, and proposes a realistic action. `If + present, will + base verb` describes a possible future result: `If the city adds a bus lane, buses will move faster.` `If + past, would + base verb` presents a more hypothetical situation: `If I lived nearer, I would cycle.` Avoid treating every `if` sentence as the same pattern.

## B2.W01.L02 — Weigh evidence and qualify an argument

**Linked target:** evaluate a claim, use cautious language, respond to a counterargument, and produce a structured argument. **Prerequisite:** connected argument paragraphs. **Lesson ID:** B2.W01.L02.

**Teach:** distinguish claim from evidence and inference. `The survey suggests ...` is more cautious than `The survey proves ...`; a small survey rarely proves a general causal claim. Use `although` to introduce a concession (`Although the sample was small, ...`) and `therefore` only when the conclusion follows from the preceding reason. Do not use a discourse marker as a substitute for reasoning.

**Reading:** “A college trialled a four-day teaching week in one department. Attendance rose slightly, and students reported more time for independent study. The trial lasted six weeks and included 42 volunteers. These results are promising, but they do not establish that the schedule would work across the whole college.”

## C1.U05.L02 — Synthesize sources and communicate uncertainty

**Linked target:** compare two short source claims, distinguish evidence from interpretation, and produce a source-attributed synthesis. **Prerequisite:** B2 argument structure and paraphrase. **Lesson ID:** C1.U05.L02.

**Teach:** synthesis connects sources around a question rather than listing them. Attribute ideas (`The first report indicates ...`; `By contrast, the interviewees ...`) and keep the strength of the claim no greater than the evidence. A survey of self-reports can show reported experience; by itself it cannot establish a causal effect.

**Source A:** “In a survey of 300 employees at one company, 62% said flexible start times made their commute less stressful. The survey measured self-reported experience over one month.”

**Source B:** “Interviews with 18 team leaders found that staggered starts sometimes made collaboration harder when teams had no shared overlap period. Several leaders said a two-hour overlap helped.”

**Reading tasks:** What does A measure? What difficulty does B identify? Can either source prove that flexible schedules improve productivity? **Key:** self-reported commute stress; difficulty collaborating without shared hours; no, neither measures productivity causally.

**Listening script:** read aloud: “Both reports suggest that schedule flexibility affects people differently. One emphasizes commute experience; the other highlights coordination. A policy may therefore need a shared collaboration window, but the evidence does not establish a single best arrangement.”

## C2.U04.L02 — Make a high-stakes recommendation with precision

**Linked target:** interpret competing priorities, negotiate a conditional decision, and produce a concise recommendation that preserves uncertainty. **Prerequisite:** C1 synthesis and register control. **Lesson ID:** C2.U04.L02.

**Teach:** distinguish a firm finding from an inference or proposal. Use precise verbs (`indicates`, `is consistent with`, `does not establish`) and explicit conditions. Polished language must not conceal missing evidence. `Reduce cost without compromising quality` means lower cost while maintaining quality; it is idiomatic and not a claim that quality cannot ever change.

**Briefing:** “A service team reports rising response times during two recent peak periods. The sample covers four weeks and does not include weekend shifts. A temporary staffing change could shorten queues, but it may raise overtime costs. A limited four-week pilot with agreed service and cost measures is proposed.”

**Reading tasks:** What is observed? What is unknown? What does the proposed pilot test? **Key:** longer response times in two peak periods; weekend performance and broader causes; whether temporary staffing improves service within an acceptable cost.

**Listening script:** “Operations supports a pilot if weekend data are collected. Finance prefers a cap on overtime. The service lead says any cap must not leave the busiest shift uncovered. No team has yet estimated the effect on customer satisfaction.”

**Listening check:** state one condition from each function and one missing measure. **Key:** Operations requires weekend data; Finance prefers an overtime cap; the service lead requires peak-shift coverage; customer-satisfaction impact is unestimated.

**Interaction:** negotiate a decision. Each role must propose a condition, ask one challenge question, and restate the other party’s position fairly before responding.

**Assessment:** 0–2 each for factual fidelity, explicit uncertainty, workable safeguards, and concise register. Success: 6/8 with no invented facts; specialist review is required for actual operational decisions.

## POST-C2.T03.L01 — Editorial and translation review

**Linked target:** revise a short professional passage for clarity, explain a translation choice, and preserve the source’s claims. **Prerequisite:** strong C2 control and relevant domain knowledge. **Lesson ID:** POST-C2.T03.L01.

**Source passage:** “We are pleased to inform you that, due to the fact that the review has been completed, the revised guidance will be made available to teams from 1 June.”

**Reading check:** What explains the guidance revision? From what date will teams receive it? Does the sentence present release as certain or merely possible? **Key:** the review is complete; from 1 June onward; it states that it will be available, not that it may be.

**Editorial task:** produce a clearer version without changing the date or certainty. **Sample:** “The review is complete. Teams will receive the revised guidance from 1 June.” Other concise versions are valid if meaning and timing remain unchanged.
