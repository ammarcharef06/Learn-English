# Post-C2 specialist learning pack

This project-authored pack accompanies [`levels/post-c2.md`](../levels/post-c2.md). Post-C2 is an optional specialist extension, not a CEFR level, professional qualification, or claim of ultimate proficiency. The source cards below are fictional teaching materials unless explicitly described otherwise. Do not cite them as real research or operational evidence.

## How to teach the sequence

Each track has three lessons: analyze a model, practice a decision, then transfer to unfamiliar material. Start with the source and task conditions. Learners should maintain an evidence log with four columns: source wording, explicit proposition, interpretation/inference, and unresolved question. This is especially important when editing, translating, mediating, negotiating, and analyzing rhetoric.

Use the lesson checks and capstones in [`post-c2-assessment.md`](post-c2-assessment.md). An instructor should record the prompt, time, support allowed, learner product, and scoring rationale. A completed lesson or portfolio is evidence of task performance only; it is not evidence that all specialists or audiences will judge the work alike.

## Reusable fictional case file: Northbridge service trial

The following invented data are for practice only:

| Item | Fictional source statement | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Trial | A four-week trial processed 120 requests. | Whether the previous process handled fewer requests; quality or response-time change. |
| Staff pulse | 18 of 27 voluntary respondents preferred the revised rota. | The views of non-respondents or all staff; an effect on absence. |
| Supervisor interviews | 9 of 12 supervisors described handovers as clearer. | A measured change in errors or service outcomes. |
| Constraint | Finance can release the current budget but cannot approve additional overtime. | That current staffing can cover weekends. |
| Open issue | No response-time comparison has been completed. | Whether response times improved, worsened, or stayed the same. |

### Worked claim audit

Claim: “The revised rota improved service.”

1. **Explicit evidence:** request count, voluntary preference responses, and supervisor descriptions.
2. **Constructs:** volume, reported preference, and perceived handover clarity are different measures.
3. **Limit:** no response-time, error, or service-outcome comparison is reported.
4. **Calibrated rewrite:** “The trial processed 120 requests; voluntary staff responses favored the revised rota, and most interviewed supervisors described clearer handovers. The available information does not establish whether service outcomes improved.”

**Practice:** write one sentence that accurately communicates the finance constraint.  
**Key:** Finance can release the current budget but cannot approve additional overtime. Do not infer that the trial is approved or that weekend coverage is possible.

## Track-specific teaching routines

### Academic research communication

Use a source-comparison grid with: research question, population/sample, method, construct, result, limitation, and claim. Compare constructs before combining findings. In the case file, staff preference and supervisor perceptions are not one shared outcome. A synthesis can group them under “experience of the rota” only if it preserves what each source actually measured.

**Practice:** write a 70-word synthesis of the staff pulse and supervisor interviews.  
**Sample key:** “In the fictional case, 18 of 27 voluntary staff respondents preferred the revised rota, while 9 of 12 supervisors described handovers as clearer. These findings concern reported preference and supervisor perception, respectively; neither measures response times, errors, or service outcomes.” Equivalent concise responses are acceptable if attribution and limitations remain accurate.

For literature reviews, organize paragraphs around questions or constructs. Introduce a source only when it contributes evidence to the synthesis. Keep author claims, study results, and the learner's interpretation distinct. A citation style must be selected by the institution or task owner; this pack does not teach a complete citation manual. The sample `(Lee, 2024)` in the level lesson is fictional and must never be treated as a real reference.

### Business and professional communication

Use a decision record with: decision requested, decision owner, evidence, constraints, options, risk, action owner, deadline, and review measure. A meeting summary must distinguish agreement from preference. Close a negotiation by reading back each condition, owner, and date; invite correction before recording it as settled.

**Practice:** prepare the next meeting question from the case file.  
**Sample:** “What weekend coverage can be provided within the current budget, and who will confirm it before the trial decision?” This asks for missing information without assuming the answer.

### Advanced writing and editing

Edit in distinct passes:

1. **Purpose and claim:** what should the reader understand or decide?
2. **Fidelity:** are facts, conditions, dates, and commitment unchanged?
3. **Structure:** does the order help the intended reader?
4. **Cohesion:** are references and transitions clear?
5. **Sentence and tone:** is the wording direct and appropriate?
6. **Proof:** correct remaining mechanical errors.

Classify changes as required (meaning, factual accuracy, or house style), recommended (clarity or flow), or optional (preference). Record a query when the draft lacks information. A clean edit that silently supplies an answer is not a faithful edit.

**Practice:** edit “The trial is a success because 120 requests were processed.”  
**Sample:** “The trial processed 120 requests; without a comparison or service-outcome measure, this count alone does not establish success.” Accept another version that preserves the reported count and clearly limits the conclusion.

### Public speaking and discourse leadership

Turn an evidence table into an oral argument map: issue → claim → evidence → limit → alternative → decision rule. Use slides or notes to make relationships visible, not to place a dense script on screen. Prepare three answer types: answerable from evidence, answerable only with qualification, and currently unknown.

**Practice challenge:** “Does the trial prove that service improved?”  
**Sample response:** “No. It records 120 processed requests and favorable reports from some participants, but there is no comparison of response times or outcomes. A trial with those measures could test the question.” The response is direct and does not dismiss the evidence that exists.

A chair should restate each position neutrally and ask the speaker to confirm the summary. If participants disagree about values rather than facts, name the value conflict; do not pretend another statistic will necessarily resolve it.

### Translation and mediation

Before translating, record: who speaks, to whom, for what purpose, what is asserted, what is conditional, what is implied, and what is ambiguous. Then create a terminology list and mark choices that may not travel naturally. In mediation, preserve the decision-relevant message and indicate any simplification.

**Practice:** translate/mediate “The trial may continue if weekend coverage is confirmed.”  
**Key:** preserve possibility (`may`) and condition (`if ... is confirmed`). Do not render it as a definite continuation. A valid translation depends on the named target language and audience; the evaluator scores fidelity and rationale, not one memorized wording.

When an ambiguity could change obligations, dates, safety, or eligibility, ask for clarification or add a clearly marked translator’s note. Do not invent a resolution.

### Discourse analysis and rhetoric

Use an observation–inference–alternative grid. Record textual feature, exact cue, plausible effect, contextual support, alternative reading, and missing evidence. “The statement uses passive voice” is observable. “The writer intentionally hides responsibility” is an inference that needs more evidence.

**Practice:** compare “The team delayed the launch” with “The launch was delayed.”  
**Key:** the first names the actor; the second does not. Both report a delay. Neither sentence alone establishes motive, blame, or whether the delay was justified.

Audience effects cannot be claimed from text analysis alone. To claim actual persuasion, comprehension, or emotional impact, collect appropriate audience evidence; otherwise use “may foreground,” “can invite,” or similar qualified phrasing.

## Spaced retrieval and transfer

At the end of each lesson, close the notes and reconstruct the evidence/claim/limit distinction from memory. Revisit after one day, one week, and three weeks using a new source card, not the same model answer. Record which decisions transferred and which needed prompting. This is a recommended teaching routine, not an implemented scheduling or learner-tracking feature.

**Transfer prompt:** A new fictional memo says a pilot received positive comments but gives no response rate. Write (a) one defensible statement, (b) one unsupported conclusion, and (c) one question for the next data collection.  
**Sample key:** (a) “Some recorded comments were positive.” (b) “Most users support the pilot.” (c) “How many people were invited, and how many responded?” Accept alternatives that maintain the distinction between recorded comments and the wider population.

## Media and source limits

This pack does not include pronunciation evaluation, an authentic paid research corpus, or learner portfolios. Teacher-read scripts remain the source text; selected prompts in the level lessons now link experimental synthetic Amy clips, which are not human-validated. For a real professional, academic, legal, medical, or public translation use case, obtain qualified domain review and appropriate source permissions before using material or acting on a recommendation.
