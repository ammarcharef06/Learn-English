# Grammar core reference

## Pre-A0 to A0

- alphabet recognition and sound awareness
- `I am`, `You are`, `He is`, `She is`
- `This is`, `That is`
- `I have`, `She has`
- `can` and simple question forms
- basic negatives: `I am not`, `He is not`
- very short sentences built from nouns and verbs

## A1

- present simple
- present continuous
- `there is / there are`
- articles: `a / an / the`
- `can` for ability and requests
- countable and uncountable nouns
- basic adverbs of frequency

## A2

- past simple
- present perfect simple in common contexts
- `going to` and `will`
- comparatives and superlatives
- `should`, `must`, and `have to`
- connectors: `because`, `but`, `so`, `although`, `when`

## B1

- conditionals
- relative clauses
- passive forms in common contexts
- past continuous and past perfect basics
- reporting verbs and simpler reported speech
- linking words for reason, contrast, and result

## B2

- complex conditionals
- inversion and emphasis
- hedging and cautious language
- nominalization
- formal and informal register management
- argument and counterargument structure

## C1 and C2

### C1

- stance and calibrated modality (`may`, `appears to`, `the evidence suggests`)
- reporting verbs and accurate source attribution (`reports`, `argues`, `finds`)
- concession and qualification across clauses and paragraphs
- nominalization and agency choices in formal prose
- cohesive reference, information flow, and controlled fronting
- paraphrase and synthesis that preserve differences in source constructs
- rhetorical signposting for spoken and written audiences

### C2

- exact control of epistemic strength, scope, conditions, and commitments
- lexical choice by sense, collocation, register, and pragmatic effect
- presupposition, entailment, and defeasible implicature
- clefting, parallelism, emphasis, and information focus
- revision for audience, rhythm, concision, and fidelity to source
- conditional decision language and repair in high-stakes interaction
