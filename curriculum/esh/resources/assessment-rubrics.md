# Assessment rubrics

These are local classroom rubrics, not official CEFR scales. Assessors should note the task, conditions, and evidence; a single short task cannot certify an overall level.

## Oral interaction rubric

Score 1–4 in each category:
- **4 — Effective:** message is clear and sustained; errors rarely impede meaning; responds and repairs independently; tone suits the task.
- **3 — Secure for the task:** message is mostly clear; some errors or pauses occur but meaning remains accessible; responds with occasional prompting.
- **2 — Developing:** key meaning is sometimes unclear; errors or pauses often interrupt; needs support to respond or repair.
- **1 — Not yet demonstrated:** cannot communicate the required message in this task, even with available support.

Record a brief example of evidence for each score. Do not equate the numeric total to a CEFR level.

## Writing rubric

Score each category 1–4 using the same evidence bands above, adapted as follows:
- task completion and required content
- organization and cohesion
- grammar and sentence control
- vocabulary range and precision
- appropriateness of style and register

## Reading comprehension rubric

Record correct responses separately for each question. For open inference, accept any answer supported by relevant text evidence. Do not infer reading ability from a memorized response.

## Listening rubric

Record correct responses separately for key information, detail, and inference. For listening scripts without recorded audio, the assessment is teacher-read and cannot be treated as validated audio listening measurement.

## Mastery rule

A learner should advance only after consistent evidence across at least two distinct tasks in the target skill area, including at least one task that requires transfer to new content. Set and record a course-specific threshold before testing; this rubric does not supply a universal pass score.
