# Sample assessment items and answer notes

The items below are illustrative checks, not complete level examinations. They do not sample all skills or demonstrate CEFR-level validity. For an assessed integrated task with explicit criteria, see `integrated-skill-labs.md`. Sample answers are examples; open production tasks accept other valid responses.

## Diagnostic test: Pre-A0 to A1

1. Write the alphabet in order.
   - Expected answer: full alphabetical sequence.
2. Circle the vowels in `orange`.
   - Answer: `o`, `a`, `e`.
3. Match the spoken word `book` to a teacher-provided picture card.
   - Accept the card depicting a book. A picture card must be supplied by the teacher; no image asset is included in this project.
4. Say your name and age.
   - Accept learner-produced correct short answer.
5. Write 3 classroom instructions.
   - Example: `Open your book. Sit down. Write.`

## A1 sample answer notes: everyday communication

- `I get up at six.` → correct if it matches the learner's stated routine.
- `There are two windows in my room.` → grammatical plural existential construction.
- `Can I have a coffee, please?` → natural café request in many varieties of English; `Could I have ...?` is also acceptable.

## A2 sample answer notes: past and future

- `Last weekend, I went to the park.` → correct use of past simple.
- `I am going to visit my aunt next week.` → correct future intention.
- `He should rest because he is tired.` → acceptable cause-and-advice structure.

## B1 sample answer notes: opinion and explanation

- `In my opinion, public transport should be improved.` → acceptable opinion statement.
- `Although the bus is slower, it is cheaper.` → correct contrast.
- `The main reason is that it reduces pollution.` → correct explanation.

## B2 sample answer notes: argument structure

- Thesis statement: `Online learning is useful, but it cannot fully replace classroom learning.`
- Counterargument marker: `However` or `On the other hand`.
- Conclusion: `For these reasons, a blended approach is more effective.`

## C1 sample answer notes: formal analysis

- `The policy may improve efficiency, but the evidence remains limited.`
- `A balanced assessment requires both quantitative and qualitative review.`
- Accept evidence-based paraphrasing with formal wording.

## C2 sample answer notes: advanced negotiation

- `We need a solution that reduces cost without compromising quality.`
- `The key challenge is balancing budget limits with customer expectations.`
- Accept varied but accurate professional phrasing.
