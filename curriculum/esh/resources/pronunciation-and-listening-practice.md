# Pronunciation and listening practice

This resource links the level path to real audio files in [`audio/generated/en-US-amy/`](audio/generated/en-US-amy/) and their metadata in [`audio/audio-catalog.json`](audio/audio-catalog.json). The clips are synthetic Piper Amy (`en_US/amy/medium`), not human recordings. They have not passed human quality review or rights clearance. Read the prompt before playing but cover the transcript and answer key until after the first attempt.

## How to run an activity

1. Check that the player is not showing the transcript.
2. Play the clip once at normal speed. Give the learner time to respond.
3. Replay once if the task allows it. Do not slow or alter the pitch unless the activity explicitly compares a supported variant.
4. Score listening answers separately from speech production. Record whether support, replay, or spelling help was provided.
5. For production, use the rubric below; a text match or automatic speech recognition result is not a pronunciation score.

## Level checks: separate listening and speaking evidence

For each stage, use the listed asset IDs plus one unseen but level-appropriate teacher-created item. Score four listening questions as 1 point each; for open production, score four rubric dimensions from 0 to 2. Report both scores separately. The local suggested target is 3/4 in listening and 6/8 in production on two tasks, one of which uses new content. These are draft classroom thresholds, not standardized cut scores or proof of a CEFR level.

| Stage | Listening sample | Production sample |
|---|---|---|
| PRE-A0 | PA0-AUD-01, PA0-AUD-02: distinguish name/sound, identify initial sound, select four CVC words | name a letter, produce initial sound, blend a taught word |
| A0 | A0-AUD-01, A0-AUD-02: identify four minimal-pair words and retrieve place/time | read two contextual sentences and distinguish live/leave to a listener |
| A1 | A1-AUD-01, A1-AUD-02: identify target consonant and recover time/place | give a new date/place and confirm listener's restatement |
| A2 | A2-AUD-01, A2-AUD-02: distinguish vowel pair and recover route | give a two-step route with clarification/repair |
| B1 | B1-AUD-01, B1-AUD-02: retrieve qualification and corrected date | correct a mistaken detail and confirm repair |
| B2 | B2-AUD-01, B2-AUD-02: preserve negation, contrast, and scope | explain a qualified result without overclaiming |
| C1 | C1-AUD-01, C1-AUD-02: separate result/limit and explicit/inferred stance | deliver a qualified argument and discuss an alternative interpretation |
| C2 | C2-AUD-01, C2-AUD-02: recover measured construct and nuanced acceptance | produce contextual focus variants while preserving propositions |
| Post-C2 | POST-AUD-01, POST-AUD-02: recover recommendation, limitation, and condition | present a specialist brief and answer a transfer question |

### Production rubric (0–2 each)

- **Intelligibility:** 2 = intended words/details are understood on first hearing; 1 = listener needs one repetition or clarification; 0 = key meaning remains unavailable after repair.
- **Target contrast or prominence:** 2 = the task's relevant contrast/focus is conveyed; 1 = inconsistent but recoverable; 0 = contrast/focus changes or obscures the message.
- **Phrase grouping and pace:** 2 = grouping supports comprehension; 1 = one disruption; 0 = frequent grouping/pace problems obscure meaning.
- **Meaning and repair:** 2 = required meaning/qualification is preserved and repair works; 1 = partially preserved or needs prompting; 0 = changes key meaning or does not repair.

Record examples of what the listener heard. Do not score accent similarity, identity, or a single phonetic realization as a universal norm. Where voice quality, hearing access, motor speech, or the target's phonological status is relevant, adapt the task with a qualified teacher and keep the construct under assessment explicit.

## Status and limitations

These materials distinguish a symbol explanation, a written example, an actual audio asset, and an administered learner task. The audio catalog lists the present generated assets and provenance. The synthetic voice is one en-US SAPI speaker; it does not represent accent diversity or independently validate any IPA transcription. No recording has been checked by a human phonetician or compared against a human reference speaker. There are no human recordings, contrastive speaker sets, recording of learners, or automated pronunciation results.
