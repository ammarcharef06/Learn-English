# Foundation diagnostics, unit checks, and transition tests

These are local classroom assessments for PRE-A0 and A0 / Pre-A1. They are not standardized, normed, or official CEFR examinations. The teacher records the task, date, support given, and result by skill. Do not combine a strong skill score with a failed essential skill to claim mastery.

## Administration and evidence rules

1. First use the diagnostic without teaching during the test; it identifies the starting point, not a pass/fail grade.
2. Read listening scripts aloud. Do not show the script or use gestures in the listening check. Repeat once only when the task says so. Selected indexed prompts also link experimental synthetic Amy clips; these are not human-validated or a substitute for unseen listening checks.
3. Give ordinary processing time. Record prompted/hinted answers separately from independent answers.
4. Open speaking and writing tasks have sample answers or criteria, not a single required wording.
5. To confirm readiness, repeat the relevant task with changed examples on another day. A repeated identical worksheet alone is not independent evidence.
6. If the task is inaccessible for a reason unrelated to the target (for example, handwriting limitation during a spelling check), accept an equivalent response mode and note the adaptation.

## T-PRE-A0-DIAG-01 — Entry diagnostic (no literacy assumed)

**Purpose:** find what the learner already knows. Allow pointing, matching, or oral response. Do not penalize silence or an unfamiliar accent in this entry check.

### Tasks

1. Show 6 upper/lower-case letter pairs (`A/a`, `M/m`, `B/b`, `S/s`, `P/p`, `G/g`). Ask the learner to match any they recognize.
2. Say the letter names `M`, `S`, `B`; ask the learner to point to the matching card from 3 choices.
3. Say the words `map`, `sun`, `book`; ask the learner to point to a matching picture if familiar. If meanings are unknown, do not score this as a sound failure.
4. Give one nonverbal demonstration, then say `Stand up`, `Sit down`, `Look`, `Listen`; observe response.
5. Greet the learner with `Hello`; allow a spoken or gestured response.
6. Offer a pencil and paper; ask the learner to make any mark or write a familiar letter if able.

### Record

Use `not yet observed`, `with support`, or `independently` for each task. There is no total score and no minimum. Start at the earliest unmet skill; do not assume alphabet familiarity from age or schooling.

## PRE-A0 unit checks

### T-PRE-A0-U01 — Letter forms and names

**Tasks (26 points):**
- Name or identify 10 randomly selected uppercase letters (10).
- Match 10 uppercase cards to lowercase choices (10).
- Write six dictated letter names as recognizable upper- or lowercase forms (6).

**Key:** use the teacher's randomized card list; accepted answer is the requested letter in either case for the dictated writing portion, unless case itself is the named target. Record letter name and written form separately.

**Success rule:** 8/10 names, 8/10 matches, 5/6 readable forms. If only one category fails, use the matching lesson and reassess that category.

### T-PRE-A0-U02 — Speech sounds and initial phonics

**Tasks (15 points, reported as three separate categories):**
- Sort five letter cards: `a, e, m, s, o` into vowel/consonant letters (5).
- Hear five familiar words and choose the first-sound card: `map, sun, fish, book, pen` (5).
- For five teacher-spoken words (`cat, bed, sit, hot, cup`), choose the matching short-vowel word card/picture (5).

**Key:** vowel letters a, e, o; consonants m, s. Initial sounds /m/, /s/, /f/, /b/, /p/. Middle vowel targets: short a/e/i/o/u respectively. Use familiar word pictures or demonstrate meanings before the test.

**Success rule:** 4/5 in each separate category. If oral recognition succeeds but writing fails, plan letter mapping rather than reteaching listening.

### T-PRE-A0-U03 — Word decoding and spelling

**Tasks (19 points):**
- Read ten shuffled taught-pattern words, one point each: `map, cat, bed, pen, sit, top, hot, sun, fin, mop`.
- Spell five dictated taught CVC words, one point each: `cup, bag, dog, mat, pin`.
- Hear four contrasts (`fan/van`, `pat/bat`, `sip/ship`, `thin/this`), point to the spoken card (4).

**Key:** exact displayed words; dictation spellings `cup, bag, dog, mat, pin`; contrast card must match the teacher's word. For `thin/this`, check the whole word distinction; do not demand an accent imitation.

**Success rule:** 8/10 decoding; 4/5 spelling; 3/4 listening contrasts. These are separate records, not an averaged claim.

### T-PRE-A0-U04 — Meaningful print and classroom language

**Print/production tasks (13 points), plus separate 7-point reading and 4-point listening tasks:**
- Match five labels to real objects/pictures: `book, pen, desk, chair, door` (5).
- Mark spaces in `a red bag` and `my book` (2).
- Copy four labels and write the first letter of `egg` and `sun` (6).
- Read and act on five printed commands: `Open your book`, `Point to the door`, `Sit down`, `Stand up`, `Close your book` (5).
- Read `A cat is on a mat. The cat can sit.` and answer `What is on the mat?` and `What can the cat do?` (2).
- Respond to the same four spoken commands without seeing print (4).

**Key:** phrases have 3 and 2 words; first letters e and s; printed commands match the stated actions; text answers are `a cat` and `sit`.

**Success rule:** at least 4/5 labels, 5/6 writing, 4/5 printed commands, 2/2 text questions, 3/4 spoken commands, and both word boundaries. Print/production is out of 13; reading is separately out of 7 and listening out of 4. Do not combine the skills into an unexplained grade.

### T-PRE-A0-U05 — First interaction and readiness review

**Tasks:**
- Follow five instructions from a new order (5).
- Read five taught words, including one digraph word (5).
- Copy or spell five familiar words (5).
- Exchange a greeting and names with a partner (3 criteria: initiates/responds, says own name, attends to partner's name).

**Key:** actions match spoken instructions; word cards are read as printed; spelling is checked against the word list; open interaction is scored by the three criteria.

**Success rule:** 4/5 instructions, 4/5 words, 4/5 spellings, and 3/3 interaction criteria. Confirm with a second short check and apply the transition gate in `levels/pre-a0.md`.

## T-PRE-A0-END-01 — PRE-A0 end-of-stage check

**Maximum 89 points plus a separate interaction record; report every component. Do not use a compensatory total.**

### A. Letter knowledge (36 points)

1. Match 26 shuffled uppercase letters to lowercase cards (26).
2. Show 10 randomly selected cards from a mixed-case set and ask the learner to say each conventional letter name (10). If speech production is not the target or is inaccessible, record an adapted response separately.

**Key:** each case pair represents the same alphabet letter; each spoken name maps to that letter. Record pair matching and name recognition separately.

### B. Sound awareness and decoding (17 points)

1. Identify first sound in `map, sun, fish, book, pen` (5). Key /m/, /s/, /f/, /b/, /p/.
2. Blend/read `cat, bed, sit, hot, cup, ship, shut, lick, made, cube` (10). Key is the exact printed word; `shut` and `lick` transfer taught spellings to combinations not used in the unit check. Do not require prior knowledge of word meaning for the decoding score.
3. Tell the number of sounds in `map` and `fish` (2). Key: 3 and 3; `sh` represents one sound in this word.

### C. Writing/spelling (25 points)

1. Write 20 lowercase letter forms after hearing their letter names, sampled across the alphabet (20). The teacher records whether each form is recognizable without being told the intended letter.
2. Spell the dictated words `sun, pen, map, dog, cup` (5). **Key:** exact spellings; record legibility separately.

### D. Functional print and phrase reading — 6 points

Show three familiar printed labels/phrases (`book`, `a red bag`, `my book`), two instructions (`Sit down`, `Open your book`), and the short text `A cat is on a mat. The cat can sit.` Ask the learner to read, point, or act as appropriate; ask one factual question about the text.

**Key:** point to the named object or matching phrase picture; perform each stated action; the cat is on the mat. Record label/phrase reading, command reading, and text comprehension separately. The sixth response is the factual text question.

### E. Listening — 5 points

Read twice: “Point to the book. Write M. Sit down. Look at the door. Close your book.”

### F. Interaction (record 0–3, separate from 89)

Learner greets a partner, says a name, asks the partner's name, and listens to the response. Award one each for an understandable greeting, own name, and relevant response/attention. Accept `Hello/Hi`, `My name is ...` or `I'm ...`; no exact wording required.

### Decision

Ready for A0 only when the learner meets every component target: at least 24/26 case matches and 8/10 letter names; at least 4/5 initial sounds, 8/10 words decoded on this check and on the unit check, and 2/2 sound counts; 20/20 recognizable dictated lowercase forms and 4/5 spellings; at least 5/6 in functional print with 2/3 labels/phrases, both printed commands, and the text question correct; 4/5 spoken commands; and 3/3 interaction criteria. Confirm on another day with a parallel set of 10 taught-pattern words and new commands. If not, record and reteach the missed component.

## T-A0-DIAG-01 — A0 entry diagnostic

Use after PRE-A0 or for placement. It is not a pass/fail test.

1. Read five familiar words and write a name/label (record separately).
2. Match four spoken numbers (ages 13, 30, 42, 58) to cards.
3. Ask `What is your name?` and `How old are you?` (spoken or supported response).
4. Show `I am ready.` and `She is at home.` Ask learner to point to `I` and `she`.
5. Give two familiar one-step commands and ask the learner to follow them.
6. Show a picture of a family/home and invite words or phrases.

Record `independent`, `with support`, or `not yet observed`. Begin the relevant unit; skip only content demonstrated independently and confirm later through transfer.

## A0 unit checks

### T-A0-U01 — Identity, numbers, and dates

**Tasks (12 points):**
- Fill `I __ ready.`; `She __ 20.`; `They __ at home.` (3).
- Make negative `He is a student.` and question `You are from Amman.` (2).
- Write numbers 14, 30, 42, 57, 80 (5).
- Complete `My birthday is __ 3 May.` and `School begins __ September.` (2).

**Key:** am; is; are; `He is not a student`; `Are you from Amman?`; fourteen, thirty, forty-two, fifty-seven, eighty; on; in.

**Success rule:** at least 9/12, and no confusion between `be` and do-support in both transformation items. Retry errors with different subjects/numbers.

### T-A0-U02 — Family, objects, description, ownership

**Tasks (12 points):**
- Plural: `box, bus, child, chair` (4).
- Article: `__ apple`, `__ bag`, `__ book` (3).
- Choose possessive: `Mina has a sister. __ sister is ten.` (1).
- Choose verb: `I __ a brother`; `He __ two books.` (2).
- Put words in order: `blue / a / bag`; `books / red / two` (2).

**Key:** boxes, buses, children, chairs; an, a, a; her; have, has; `a blue bag`; `two red books`.

**Success rule:** 10/12. If one subskill is below 2/3, retain that gap and remediate it separately.

### T-A0-U03 — Time and routine

**Tasks (12 points):**
- Say/write 7:00 and 2:30 (2).
- Choose `at/on/in`: `__ Monday`, `__ 6:00`, `__ May`, `__ the morning`, `__ night` (5).
- Complete: `She usually __ (go) at eight.`; `They __ (study) at night.` (2).
- Negative: `He works on Sunday.` (1).
- Question: `Mina reads at night.` (2).

**Key:** seven o'clock; half past two/two thirty; on, at, in, in, at; goes, study; `He doesn't work on Sunday.`; `Does Mina read at night?`

**Success rule:** 9/12 and correct auxiliary/base verb in the question/negative items.

### T-A0-U04 — Places, prepositions, and requests

**Tasks (12 points):**
- Picture locations: choose `in/on/under/next to` for four shown arrangements (4).
- Complete `__ a park near the school.` (one park) and `__ two shops on this street.` (2).
- Form question: “a library / near here?” (1).
- Complete polite ability/request: `Can she __?` (read); `Can you __ me, please?` (help) (2).
- Give two-step directions on a simple map (3 criteria: route, two direction phrases, destination) (3).

**Key:** depends on pictured arrangement; `There is`; `There are`; `Is there a library near here?`; read; help; route-specific.

**Success rule:** 9/12 with at least 2/3 direction criteria. Use a different object layout/map for retest.

### T-A0-U05 — Connected language and retrieval

**Tasks (12 points):**
- Correct: `She are at home.` (1)
- Correct: `Does he lives here?` (1)
- Read the message and answer: Who wrote it? Where is Noor? What is next to the station? What time can Noor meet? What should Ali bring? (5).
- Write four sentences about a familiar person/place/routine (4 criteria: 4 sentences, subject+verb, relevant detail, capitals/full stops) (4).
- Relay one fact from a partner's message without changing it (1).

**Message:** `Hi Ali, I am at the market. It is next to the station. Meet me at five. Please bring the small bag. — Noor`

**Key:** `She is at home.`; `Does he live here?`; Noor; at the market; the market; five; the small bag. Writing and relay are open; score the four stated criteria.

**Success rule:** 9/12 and at least 3/4 writing criteria. Treat reading, writing, and mediation scores separately in the progress record.

## T-A0-END-01 — A0 / Pre-A1 transition test

Use unfamiliar but taught-pattern items. Maximum 40; report each section and do not compensate for an essential skill failure by adding unrelated points.

### A. Numbers, dates, and time — 5 points

Write the spoken number 36; write the date `14 April` in the format taught; complete `__ Friday`; complete `__ 8:00`; write the time `half past six`.

**Key:** thirty-six/36; `14 April` or `April 14`; on; at; 6:30 or six thirty.

### B. Grammar in context — 8 points

1. `I __ a student.` (1)
2. `My parents __ at home.` (1)
3. Make negative: `Mona is tired.` (1)
4. Ask a `be` question about `you / ready` (1)
5. `He __ (go) to work at eight.` (1)
6. Make negative: `They live here.` (1)
7. Ask: `She studies English.` (1)
8. Complete: `There __ two chairs.` (1)

**Key:** am; are; Mona is not/isn't tired; Are you ready?; goes; They do not/don't live here; Does she study English?; are.

### C. Reading — 5 points

Read: `My name is Rami. I am 21. I live near a small park. I work at a shop from nine to five. My sister studies at home. On Friday, we visit our grandmother.`

### E. Speaking/interaction — 5 points

Role-play: the partner asks four questions about name, age, city, and routine; answer them, then ask the partner one relevant question. A fictional profile is acceptable.

Award one point for each detail conveyed understandably: name, age, city, routine, partner question/response. Accept truthful alternatives, contractions, and sensible pronunciation variation. Teacher records an example; a single task is not a speaking-level certificate.

### F. Writing — 8 points

Write 6–8 sentences about self, family, home, or routine. The learner may invent a profile.

Score one each for: 6–8 understandable sentences; appropriate subject+verb in at least 5 of 6 clauses; readable order; appropriate `be`; appropriate `have/has`; at least two routine verbs; at least one time/place phrase; capitals/full stops. Accept alternate facts and more than one correct construction.

### G. Spelling and reading transfer — 4 points

1. Read new taught-pattern words `shop`, `flag`, `desk` (3).
2. Spell one familiar word after hearing it in a sentence (`pen`) (1).

**Key:** exact target words and spelling; record intelligibility, not imitation of a particular accent.

### Transition decision

**Local readiness criterion:** at least 30/40 overall, plus minimums of A 3/5, B 6/8, C 4/5, D 4/5, E 4/5, F 5/8, G 3/4. In addition, the writing response must meet the subject+verb clause criterion and include the specified `be` and `have/has` forms; these cannot be compensated for by unrelated points. Confirm essential reading/listening or productive gaps with a parallel task on another day. If any minimum is missed, continue targeted practice and retest that skill; do not label it an overall A1 failure or pass solely from the total.

This threshold is a course decision for this local assessment. It has not been normed or validated against CEFR and does not award a formal level.
