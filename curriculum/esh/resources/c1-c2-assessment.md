# C1–C2 diagnostic, unit checks, and stage-end assessments

These assessments are locally authored samples. They are not standardized, normed, piloted, or validated CEFR tests. The official Council of Europe source pages could not be text-verified in this environment; the objectives and thresholds are curriculum decisions, not descriptor-level certification. Listening scripts remain prompts for live teacher reading; selected indexed prompts have supplemental experimental Amy recordings. These clips are not human-validated, so no score is evidence from a validated independent audio-listening test.

## Administration and reporting

- The diagnostic is ungraded and identifies support needs; it is not a placement/pass-fail test.
- Unit checks use the associated pack input as formative evidence. For an attainment decision, use a parallel prompt not previously seen and record its version.
- The stage-end reading and listening inputs below are new, separate from the learning pack. Conceal the listening script; read twice without displaying it.
- Report reading, listening, language, speaking, writing, and mediation separately. Do not let a high writing score conceal a listening or interaction gap.
- Keep exposure, practice, performance on a check, and a mastery decision as separate records. This project includes prepared assessments only; no learner has been assessed here.
- Open answers have multiple acceptable forms. Score against the stated construct and rubric, not resemblance to a model.

## C1 unit checks

Each formative unit check totals **8 points**: reading 3, listening 2, language/interpretation 1, and open transfer 2. Open work is scored 0–2 using the unit-specific success criterion below. A local formative benchmark is 6/8 with no zero in an essential skill; it is unpiloted and does not establish level mastery.

### C1-T-U01 — Argument architecture (8 points)

Use the learning-pack text “The quiet measure” and its listening script.
- **Reading (3):** identify council evidence (1); state the access limitation (1); explain why success depends on the measure (1).
- **Listening (2):** state the cited measure (1); name the outcome not yet known (1).
- **Interpretation (1):** give a textual cue supporting an inference about unmet need.
- **Transfer (2):** write a 60–80-word summary that preserves evidence, limitation, and uncertainty.

**Key:** attendance rose; some callers unable to book online were less likely to secure an appointment; attendance can rise without proving equitable access; galleries/time spent; understanding; the passage contrasts a rise in those served with continuing unmet need. Transfer: 1 point for all three required relations; 1 for no causal or universal overclaim.

### C1-T-U02 — Professional writing (8 points)

Use the learning-pack text “A report that travels” and listening script.
- **Reading (3):** name both interventions (1); state the causal limitation (1); give the recommended next step (1).
- **Listening (2):** identify the tone problem (1); give the recommended information order (1).
- **Language (1):** revise `A review of the complaints was undertaken by the team` to an active, neutral sentence.
- **Transfer (2):** draft a 70–100-word manager note including evidence, limitation, and action.

**Key:** guide and counter demonstrations; effects not separated; retain guide/train staff/check queries for eight weeks; opening by blaming the client; action first then explanation; `The team reviewed the complaints.` Transfer: 1 for source/limit/action, 1 for concise appropriate register and faithful claim strength.

### C1-T-U03 — Oral argumentation (8 points)

Use the learning-pack presentation excerpt and listening script.
- **Reading (3):** explain why dense written syntax is risky aloud (1); name a signposting function (1); state the text's distinction about confidence (1).
- **Listening (2):** identify what the speaker supports (1); name the proposed cost measure (1).
- **Language (1):** complete with a suitable repair: `What I mean by “effective” is ...` Accept a clear definition tied to an outcome.
- **Transfer (2):** deliver a one-minute recommendation and answer one question. Score 1 for a clear claim/evidence/limit chain; 1 for a relevant answer or explicit clarification.

**Key:** listeners cannot revisit dense paragraphs; make the relation between points audible; confident delivery is not evidence/reasoning; continue the clinic for one term; cost per completed appointment. Open tasks use criteria.

### C1-T-U04 — Implication and tone (8 points)

Use the learning-pack text “The launch” and listening script.
- **Reading (3):** identify two contextual cues for restrained irony/criticism (1); say whether sarcasm is certain and explain why (1); identify an explicitly unknown fact (1).
- **Listening (2):** state the speaker's explicit position (1); infer a concern supported by wording (1).
- **Interpretation (1):** state one plausible alternative reading of a cue.
- **Transfer (2):** paraphrase the script neutrally, separating explicit content from inference.

**Key:** e.g. “ambitious,” “admirable patience,” “useful rehearsal” in contrast with delay/rescheduling; no, context permits but does not force an ironic reading; reason for delay; caution about calling change a success; concern about non-response bias. Transfer: 1 point for accurate explicit content; 1 for labeled, cue-based inference rather than a claim of certain emotion.

### C1-T-U05 — Research synthesis (8 points)

Use Sources A/B and the listening script in the pack.
- **Reading (3):** state A's measured construct (1); state B's focus (1); explain whether learning outcome is established (1).
- **Listening (2):** explain why the sources need not conflict (1); name the evidence gap for the proposed combined format (1).
- **Language (1):** complete with accurate attribution: `Source A measures ___, whereas Source B reports ___ .`
- **Transfer (2):** write a 70–100-word synthesis with one shared theme and one difference.

**Key:** learner preference for recorded review; tutors' view of live discussion/identifying misunderstanding; no comparative learning outcome; sources concern different functions/perspectives; need outcome evidence beyond preference; responses vary but must preserve those distinctions.

### C1-T-U06 — Negotiation and mediation (8 points)

Use the learning-pack “shared deadline” text and listening script.
- **Reading (3):** state each team's interest (1); name their shared point (1); identify the unresolved issue (1).
- **Listening (2):** state the conditional support (1); state what is not agreed/deferred (1).
- **Language (1):** complete a neutral decision record: `Agreed: ___. Conditional on: ___. Unresolved: ___.`
- **Transfer (2):** relay the decision to a new audience without turning conditional support into full agreement.

**Key:** verification time and preserving uncertainty; one-page summary; whether full methods note accompanies it; uncertainty statement remains on first page; methods-note optionality is not agreed, format deferred; accept accurate completions. Transfer: one point for accurate decision/condition, one for unresolved issue and audience clarity.

## C1 stage-end assessment — C1-END-01 (40 points)

Five separately reported sections, **8 points each**.

### A. Reading and interpretation (8 points)

Read: “The regional archive opened its catalogue online in January. Searches increased by 40% over the next quarter, and staff received fewer requests for opening hours. The director called the launch a success. The report, however, counted searches rather than completed research visits; it did not distinguish local users from remote users, and a publicity campaign began in the same week. Researchers interviewed later said the catalogue was easier to search, while some community groups reported that older records remained difficult to locate. The archive plans to review search completion, user groups, and requests for help before deciding whether to digitize the next collection.”

### C. Language and discourse (8 points)

1. Rewrite actively: `A review of the user comments was conducted by the archive team.` (2)
2. Attribute and qualify: `The publicity campaign caused searches to rise.` The report shows a campaign and increased searches in the same quarter. (2)
3. Combine using a concession without changing meaning: “The catalogue became easier to search. Some older records remained difficult to locate.” (2)
4. Repair the claim: `The launch proved the catalogue served every community.` (2)

**Sample keys:** `The archive team reviewed the user comments.`; `Searches rose during the quarter in which the publicity campaign began, but the report does not establish that the campaign caused the rise.`; `Although the catalogue became easier to search, some older records remained difficult to locate.`; `The launch may have improved search access for some users, but evidence about all communities is not provided.` Award half-points for preserving both proposition and degree of certainty.

### D. Speaking and interaction (8 points)

Present a 3–4-minute recommendation on whether the archive should digitize the next collection. Use the reading and your own decision criterion, not invented statistics. Answer one challenge and clarify one term. Score 0–2 for evidence fidelity, organization and audience orientation, qualified reasoning, and responsive interaction/repair; annotate evidence.

### E. Writing and mediation (8 points)

Write a 180–230-word committee note with a qualified recommendation, relevant evidence, one limitation, a next measure, and a review condition. Add a 50–70-word public summary.

Score 0–2 each for synthesis/factual fidelity, reasoning/qualification, cohesion/register, and plain-language mediation. Materially invented evidence caps the section at 3/8. Multiple recommendations are valid if the stated evidence and limits are respected.

**Provisional local readiness rule:** at least 28/40, at least 4/8 in every section, and successful new-topic transfer in writing and interaction after feedback. This has not been piloted and is not a CEFR decision.

## C2 unit checks

Each formative check totals **8 points**: interpretation/reading 3, listening 2, language choice 1, and transfer 2. For open production, score precision, fidelity, and fit to the stated audience using the criterion. Local formative benchmark: 6/8 with no zero in an essential skill; this threshold is unpiloted.

### C2-T-U01 — Precision and editorial choice (8 points)

Use “One sentence, three commitments” and its listening script.
- Reading (3): state observation (1), causal limit (1), decision status (1).
- Listening (2): say whether proposal was rejected (1); state requested items (1).
- Language (1): explain why `disproved` is not an accurate substitute for `did not establish`.
- Transfer (2): produce an executive edit preserving fact, condition, and modality.

**Key:** delays fell during pilot; cause not established; provisional retention while gathering another quarter; no; narrower version/cost estimate; `did not establish` means evidence is insufficient, not contrary evidence has disproved the claim. Transfer: 1 point for factual fidelity, 1 for exact commitment/scope.

### C2-T-U02 — Dense-text interpretation (8 points)

Use “A measured endorsement” and its listening script.
- Reading (3): state the criterion being measured (1), missing outcome (1), and adoption decision (1).
- Listening (2): state reported fact (1); give reason safety conclusion is resisted (1).
- Interpretation (1): label an inference and a cue supporting it.
- Transfer (2): rank interpretations from best supported to unsupported and explain the ranking.

**Key:** number of sessions delivered; total workload; wider discussion but not automatic adoption; no serious incident reported; one-month review/no weekend use; narrow criterion and missing outcome imply insufficiency; (b) is best supported, (a) is contradicted by “not yet for automatic adoption,” and (c) is unsupported because the report does not say whether workload changed.

### C2-T-U03 — Public speaking and influence (8 points)

Use “The address” and the listening script.
- Reading (3): explain the repeated contrast (1), identify the missing outcome (1), state the recommendation (1).
- Listening (2): name the assumption challenged (1); give one governance question (1).
- Language (1): complete the parallel frame: `Not ___, but ___`; award for a clear contrast with matched grammatical form.
- Transfer (2): deliver a 90-second address and answer a challenge with evidence, not rhetoric alone.

**Key:** outputs counted versus outcomes unmeasured; cases resolved/time transferred to callers; measure whole journey, publish limits, return with inspectable decision; publication alone increases trust; who selected measures / who can challenge / what if target met while quality falls. Open items scored for accurate structure and evidence.

### C2-T-U04 — Professional decision and negotiation (8 points)

- Listen (2): state the speaker's accepted condition (1); state the explicit non-agreement (1).
- Language (1): distinguish preference (`prefers a cap`) from commitment (`will cap`).
- Transfer (2): create a decision note with condition and unresolved item.

**Key:** longer response times in two peak periods; weekend/customer satisfaction missing; authorize a measured pilot, not permanent model; overtime cap has busiest-shift exception; permanent expansion is not agreed; preference is not a commitment. The report treats the pooled figure as evidence that the system is ready to expand, while noting that users who abandoned the online form were not included in the feedback survey. ### C. Language and editorial judgment (8 points)

1. Calibrate: revise `The system proved that service access improved` to fit the data. (2)
2. Preserve condition: rewrite `The extension is approved` to match `A limited extension is proposed, provided the next review reports three measures.` (2)

4. Explain the difference between `not established` and `disproved` in one sentence. (2)

**Sample keys:** `The results suggest faster completion among requests measured, but do not establish wider access.`; `A limited extension is proposed, conditional on reporting the three measures.`; `The association observed a shift in workload.`; `Not established means evidence is insufficient to support a claim; disproved means evidence supports its negation.` Accept equivalent wording preserving the distinctions.

### D. Speaking, negotiation, and response (8 points)

Deliver a 3–5-minute recommendation to an oversight panel on whether to extend the system. Then respond to (a) an evidence challenge and (b) a resource concern. Must distinguish data, interpretation, and decision. Score 0–2 each for precision/factual fidelity, argument architecture, calibrated stance, and responsive interaction/repair.

### E. Writing and audience adaptation (8 points)

Write a 220–280-word oversight memorandum with decision, rationale, conditions, risks, measures, and review point. Then write a 50–70-word public-facing version. Score 0–2 each for evidential precision, conditional/uncertainty control, structure and register, and fidelity of adaptation. A material change to the condition or a fabricated figure caps the section at 3/8.

**Provisional local readiness rule:** at least 30/40, no section below 5/8, and a successful fresh-topic editorial/mediation transfer task. This unpiloted rule is not a CEFR result and must not be used as formal certification.

## Open-task scoring guide

For each 0–2 dimension, award **2** when the task criterion is clearly met with accurate, independent performance; **1** when it is partly met, needs prompting, or contains a correctable imprecision; **0** when it is absent, materially inaccurate, or changes the source's meaning. Record a brief evidence note. For reading inference, require textual cues; for speaking, note task conditions and whether interlocutors could reconstruct the intended point; for writing, assess content, organization, control, and register; for mediation, compare the summary with source positions. Do not score accent, personal opinion, or stylistic preference unless it affects the stated construct. These local bands are not the 1–4 overall oral/writing rubric and are not calibrated.

## Remediation and retesting

- **Evidence overreach:** sort statements into observation, source claim, inference, and recommendation; rewrite a new case.
- **Weak synthesis:** build a source-by-question grid before composing; recheck attribution and difference.
- **Unclear implication:** cite the cue, state the inference as tentative, and offer an alternative.
- **Register mismatch:** rewrite the same information for two audiences; preserve facts and commitment.
- **Negotiation drift:** record position, interest, condition, commitment, and unresolved issue separately.
- **Editing alters meaning:** compare dates, quantities, actors, modality, scope, and exceptions line by line.

Use a parallel unseen item after feedback. Record the original and recheck separately; do not overwrite a failed attempt or mark mastery from exposure alone.
