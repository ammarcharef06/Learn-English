# Post-C2 checks, projects, and scoring

These are project-authored specialist task instruments, not CEFR tests, professional certifications, or validated qualifications. No learner has taken them. Scripts and source cards remain the text sources; selected prompts have supplemental experimental Amy recordings linked beside them. A teacher-read task is not an independently delivered audio test, and generated clips are not human-validated. Use new material for transfer; do not score memorized model answers as independent performance.

## Entry portfolio diagnostic — PC2-DIAG-01

Ask the learner to submit one recent writing sample and complete a 20-minute source-fidelity task: identify a claim, evidence, limitation, and unknown in a short text; write a 100-word summary; explain one editing or translation choice; answer one oral follow-up. The teacher notes task conditions and whether support was used. This diagnostic guides track selection and is not scored as a pass/fail placement decision.

**Evidence record:** sample/source, learner output, prompts and supports, observed strengths, development priorities, and chosen track. Do not infer a CEFR level from this brief sample.

## POST-C2-T01-CHECK — Academic reading and synthesis

Use these new fictional source cards (not the lesson case): **A:** In a voluntary survey of 52 first-year students, 34 said they used the research clinic at least weekly; the survey did not assess research assignments. **B:** In interviews at two campuses, 11 of 15 librarians said the clinic helped students refine search questions; there was no before/after comparison or student-work sample.

**Items**

1. State what each source measures or reports.
2. Is the statement “The clinic improved research skill” established? Explain with one evidence limit.
3. Write a 70–100-word synthesis that attributes both sources and preserves their difference.
4. Name one additional measure that would address a remaining question.

**Answer key:** (1) A reports self-reported clinic use among voluntary student respondents; B reports librarians' views about search-question refinement. (2) No; neither source directly assesses research skill or compares student work over time/against a comparison group. (3) Accept any accurate synthesis that distinguishes reported use from librarian judgments and does not claim measured skill gains. (4) A blind-rated search-task sample before/after the clinic, or a suitable comparison design, could address the outcome question.

**Scoring:** 0–2 each for construct accuracy, evidence calibration, synthesis/attribution, and a relevant additional measure. Success is 6/8 with no invented finding. The writing response is scored with the open-task criteria in the track portfolio rubric.

No one has counted how many requests are abandoned.” The learner may take notes but may not see the script during listening.

## POST-C2-T03-CHECK — Editing diagnosis and rationale

Edit: “It was decided that the successful trial will be expanded because 120 requests were processed.” The source card says only that the fictional trial processed 120 requests; it gives no decision, comparison, or outcome measure.

**Items**

1. Identify two unsupported or obscured elements.
2. Produce a faithful revision.
3. Explain one consequential edit and one query you would send the author.

**Answer key:** possible issues: actor and decision are omitted/unsupported; “successful” and “will be expanded” are not supported by the source; processing 120 requests alone is not a comparison or outcome. Sample revision: “The trial processed 120 requests.” Suitable query: “Has an expansion decision been made, and what comparison or outcome measure supports describing the trial as successful?” Other faithful revisions are accepted.

**Scoring:** 0–2 each for diagnosis, source fidelity, clarity, and rationale/query. Success is 6/8 with no invented decision. Optional stylistic changes are not penalized if justified and faithful.

## POST-C2-T04-CHECK — Argument and question response

The learner prepares a three-minute recommendation from this new fictional evidence: in a voluntary poll of 42 residents, 26 requested a community clinic opening before 8 a.m.; no visitor counts before 8 a.m. or staffing-cost estimate are available. A partner asks: “Does this prove that permanent early opening is needed?” The learner answers, then gives a one-sentence fair summary of the partner’s concern.

**Answer key:** no; the voluntary poll records expressed requests, not observed early-morning use or the case for permanent hours. A limited trial with attendance and staffing-cost measures could inform the decision. A fair summary recognizes the concern about committing permanent resources without evidence of use or cost. Equivalent responses are acceptable.

**Scoring:** 0–2 each for claim/evidence link, preserved limitation, relevant answer, and fair summary/repair. Success is 6/8 with no claim that demand or cost has already been measured. Record delivery conditions; do not infer a pronunciation result without a separate, appropriate assessment.

## POST-C2-T05-CHECK — Translation and mediation fidelity

Translate or mediate for a named audience: “The board may review the proposal next week if the revised figures arrive by noon on Thursday.” Before producing the target text, mark the actor, action, modality, time phrase, condition, and one ambiguity.

**Answer key:** actor = board; action = possible review; modality = may, not will; time = next week; condition = revised figures arrive by Thursday noon. “Next week” is relative to the message date and can become unclear if forwarded; a date clarification is appropriate. Target-language wording varies and is not keyed to one fixed translation.

**Scoring:** 0–2 each for proposition/actor, modality/condition, audience fit, and ambiguity note. Success is 6/8 with no change from possible to definite and no invented calendar date. A qualified bilingual reviewer is required before publication for any particular language pair.

## POST-C2-T06-CHECK — Discourse analysis

Compare these new invented sentences: A: “The city closed two lanes after the review.” B: “Two lanes were closed after the review.”

**Items**

1. Name one observable difference.
2. State one inference that is possible but not established.
3. Give an alternative explanation or identify missing context.
4. Write a 100–140-word analysis using a quoted cue and qualified language.

**Answer key:** A names the city as actor; B omits the actor. Possible inference: B foregrounds the event/result or leaves responsibility unspecified. Neither sentence proves deliberate concealment or motive. Additional context could show that the actor is already known, irrelevant, or intentionally backgrounded. Open responses need a textual cue and must distinguish observation from inference.

**Scoring:** 0–2 each for accurate observation, calibrated inference, contextual alternative, and evidence-based analysis. Success is 6/8; unsupported intent claims cannot receive the inference/analysis points.

## Track portfolio projects

Each project is assessed with six criteria scored 0–3. **3 — strong:** accurate, independent, and well supported; **2 — effective:** meets the criterion with a minor lapse; **1 — developing:** partial, uneven, or dependent on prompting; **0 — absent or materially inaccurate.** The local threshold is 12/18, with the track-specific fidelity condition below. A reviewer records a short evidence note for every score. This threshold is provisional and has not been piloted or normed.

| Track | Required independent submission | Six scored criteria | Fidelity condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| T01 Academic/research | 900–1,200-word mini literature review; four verified sources; synthesis table; limitation paragraph; five-minute presentation | source verification, construct comparison, synthesis, qualification, organization/citation, oral response | no fabricated sources or findings |
| T02 Business/professional | one-page decision memo; six-minute stakeholder meeting; follow-up action record | evidence clarity, decision framing, audience fit, negotiation/listening, owners/dates, respectful directness | no invented commitment or operational fact |
| T03 Writing/editing | annotated 700–900-word source; clean copy; two audience versions; rationale log | fidelity, structure, clarity, register, query discipline, rationale | no silent resolution of factual ambiguity |
| T04 Speaking/discourse leadership | 6–8-minute address; evidence map; three unscripted answers; chaired discussion | reasoning, evidence/qualification, audience adaptation, response/repair, fair facilitation, organization | no unsupported claim presented as fact |
| T05 Translation/mediation | source brief; two audience versions; comparative commentary; query log; oral mediation | fidelity, register, terminology, ambiguity, rationale, audience transfer | no unmarked change of commitment or invented detail |
| T06 Discourse/rhetoric | 1,000–1,300-word analysis; coded evidence table; counter-reading; oral defense | observation, context, evidence, alternative reading, terminology, limits on effects | no claim of intent or audience impact without evidence |

Open projects have multiple valid answers. Reviewers assess the specified criteria, not conformity to sample wording. If the work fails a fidelity condition, the learner revises the relevant component on new material before the portfolio is recorded as meeting the local threshold.

## Performance and administration status

All checks and rubrics are prepared instruments. They have not been administered to learners; no score, reliability estimate, validity evidence, or track-completion outcome is available. The project has no learner audio recordings or learner portfolios. “Covered with existing content” in the matrix means that instruction, activity, and a prepared assessment/criterion are linked; it does not mean a learner has demonstrated the skill.
